Lesson 13

Living by Faith, Not by Sight

Primary Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:7

Supporting Scriptures: Hebrews 11:1; Romans 10:17; James 2:17; Proverbs 3:5–6

Memory Verse: 2 Corinthians 5:7

Central Teaching Truth

Walking by faith means trusting God's character, His Word, and His promises even when we cannot see or understand what He is doing.

Lesson Objective

To understand what Paul means by walking by faith, recognize that fear does not cancel genuine faith, and learn to depend on God's Word and the Holy Spirit in daily obedience.

Introduction

Every day we make decisions based upon what we can see, hear, and understand. Yet the Christian life calls us to trust God even when circumstances are unclear. Faith is not pretending that difficulties do not exist. Faith is choosing to believe that God remains faithful even when we cannot see the outcome.

1 | Observation — What Does Scripture Say?

Read 2 Corinthians 5:1–10.

Paul contrasts our present earthly condition with the future hope believers have in Christ. His confidence rests upon what God has promised, even though that future is not yet visible.

  1. What does Paul mean when he says, “we walk by faith”?
  2. What is the difference between walking by faith and walking by sight?
  3. What promises in this passage give Paul confidence?
  4. How does Paul describe the believer's future hope?

2 | Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

“We walk.” This describes an ongoing manner of living. Faith is not limited to one decision or one difficult moment. It becomes the direction of the believer's life.

“By faith.” Our confidence rests in God's character and revealed promises, particularly our hope in Christ.

“Not by sight.” What we presently see is not the final measure of what God has promised.

Walking by faith does not mean ignoring evidence, refusing wise counsel, or assuming that every personal desire is a promise from God. Faith is rooted in who God is and in what He has actually revealed in His Word.

3 | Faith and Our Five Senses

We naturally depend upon what we can see, hear, touch, taste, and smell. These are valuable gifts, but they cannot fully reveal spiritual realities or the future God has promised.

Our senses tell us what is happening around us. God's Word teaches us how to understand those circumstances from His perspective.

Faith does not deny what our senses tell us. Instead, faith refuses to allow present circumstances to have the final word when God has spoken.

4 | Faith and Fear

Experiencing fear does not automatically mean that a person has no faith. Many faithful people in Scripture experienced fear, uncertainty, and weakness.

Faith becomes visible when we choose to trust and obey God despite those feelings.

Fear asks, “What if everything goes wrong?” Faith asks, “What has God said, and how should I obey Him today?”

5 | Faith Comes Through God's Word

Romans 10:17 teaches that faith comes by hearing the Word of Christ. Biblical faith is therefore not blind optimism. It grows as we learn God's Word, understand His character, and remember His faithfulness.

When circumstances challenge our confidence, Scripture gives us truth upon which our faith can stand.

6 | Faith Produces Obedience

James 2 teaches that genuine faith produces action. Trusting God should affect the decisions we make and the way we live.

Sometimes obedience comes before we understand the entire outcome. We take the next faithful step because we trust the One who has instructed us.

7 | Depending on the Holy Spirit

Believers are not called to manufacture faith through human determination alone. The Holy Spirit helps us understand God's Word, reminds us of truth, strengthens us in weakness, and leads us toward obedience.

Walking by faith means depending upon God rather than depending only upon our own understanding.

8 | Application — Live It

  1. What circumstance in my life is presently difficult for me to understand?
  2. Am I allowing what I see to become more powerful than what God has said?
  3. What promise or biblical truth do I need to remember concerning this situation?
  4. What step of obedience can I take even though I cannot see the entire outcome?
  5. Where do I need to depend more upon the Holy Spirit rather than my own strength or understanding?

Personal Action Step

This week, identify one area where uncertainty or fear has been controlling your thinking. Write down one Scripture that speaks truth into that situation. Pray over that Scripture each day and take one practical step of obedience based upon what God has said.

Group Discussion

How can believers distinguish between genuine biblical faith and simply hoping that God will give us whatever we personally want?

Closing Prayer

Father, teach us to walk by faith and not merely by what we can see. Help us trust Your character, believe Your Word, and obey You even when we do not understand the outcome. Strengthen us through Your Holy Spirit and help us live each day with confidence in Jesus Christ. Amen.
