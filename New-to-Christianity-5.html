New to Christianity • Lesson 5

Understanding the Bible

God’s Word for Your New Life

Central Truth

God has given us His Word so that we can know Him, understand His truth, grow in our faith, and learn how to live as followers of Jesus Christ.
Key Scriptures:
2 Timothy 3:16–17
Acts 17:11
James 1:5
1 Peter 2:2
James 1:22–25
John 8:31–32
Psalm 119:105

1. What Is the Bible?

The Bible is God’s written Word.

It teaches us who God is, who we are, what sin is, what Jesus Christ has done for us, and how God wants us to live.

The Bible is not simply a religious book or a collection of old stories. Scripture gives us the truth God wants us to know.

Second Timothy 3:16–17 teaches that Scripture is inspired by God and is useful for teaching, correction, instruction, and preparing believers for good works.

2. Every Believer Must Know God’s Word Personally

God gives pastors, teachers, and mature believers to help us understand Scripture, but we should not depend completely on someone else’s interpretation.

Every believer should learn to read the Bible and understand what it says for themselves.

Acts 17:11 tells us that the Bereans listened to Paul’s teaching and then searched the Scriptures daily to see whether what he taught was true.

Teaching Truth:

God uses teachers to help us, but every believer must learn to know His Word personally.

3. What Should I Do When I Do Not Understand the Bible?

There will be times when you read something in the Bible that you do not understand. Do not become discouraged.

Pray About It

Ask God for wisdom and understanding. James 1:5 teaches that if we lack wisdom, we should ask God.

Ask the Holy Spirit for Guidance

Ask the Holy Spirit to help you understand what the passage is saying and how it applies to your life.

Seek Help From Mature Believers

Ask for help from a mature Bible teacher or believer whose life demonstrates godly character and good fruit.

Compare Everything With Scripture

Even when receiving help from someone else, continue to compare their teaching with the Bible. Scripture remains the standard.

4. Where Should a New Christian Begin Reading?

A good place to start is the Gospel of John.

John teaches us about Jesus Christ, who He is, why He came, what He taught, and what it means to believe in Him.

The Psalms are also a good place to read.

They teach us about worship, trust, fear, joy, sorrow, thankfulness, trouble, and hope.

Read slowly. Pray for understanding. Ask the Holy Spirit to help you understand and apply what you are reading.

5. Begin With Small, Consistent Portions

You do not have to read large amounts of the Bible at once.

Trying to read too much too quickly can become overwhelming.

First Peter 2:2 compares spiritual growth to newborn babies desiring milk so they can grow.

Begin with a short section of Scripture. Read it carefully. Think about what it teaches. Pray about what you read.

Teaching Truth:

Read the Bible as often as possible, but begin with small portions. Spiritual growth takes place gradually.

6. Reading the Bible Is Not Enough

James 1:22 teaches us to be doers of the Word and not hearers only.

God wants His Word to affect the way we live.

As believers, we will experience both successes and failures.

When we fail, we learn, repent, get back up, and continue moving forward.

The Christian Life Is Like a Marathon

The Christian life is not a short race. It is a lifelong journey.

Sometimes we may become tired, discouraged, spiritually stuck, or stagnant.

When that happens, we should not give up.

Teaching Truth:

Spiritual growth is not perfection. It is continuing to learn, repent, obey, and move forward with God.

7. A Simple Way to Read and Understand Scripture

What Did I Read?

Slow down and pay attention to what the passage actually says.

What Does This Teach Me About God or Jesus?

Look for what the passage reveals about God’s character, His promises, His commands, or the person and work of Jesus Christ.

What Does This Show Me About Myself?

Ask whether the passage reveals something about your attitude, behavior, faith, priorities, or relationships.

What Should I Do With What I Have Read?

Is there something I need to believe?

Is there something I need to change?

Is there something I need to obey?

Is there something I need to pray about?

8. Let God’s Word Guide Your Life

Psalm 119:105 describes God’s Word as a lamp to our feet and a light to our path.

The Bible helps guide us as we make decisions, face temptation, handle relationships, and grow in our faith.

You will not understand everything immediately. You will not change everything overnight.

But as you continue reading, praying, learning, and applying Scripture, God’s Word will increasingly shape your life.

A Simple Bible Reading Plan

Begin with a small section from the Gospel of John.

Read slowly.

Ask God for understanding.

Think about what the passage says about Jesus.

Write down one thing that stood out to you.

Ask yourself how you can apply it.

Pray about what you learned.

Questions for Reflection

1. Why is it important for you to read the Bible for yourself?

2. What should you do when you read something you do not understand?

3. Why is the Gospel of John a good place for a new Christian to begin?

4. What is the difference between reading the Bible and applying the Bible?

5. What should you do when you fail in your Christian walk?

This Week’s Practical Step

Choose one short passage from the Gospel of John.

Read it slowly, then answer these four questions:

What did I read?

What does this teach me about God or Jesus?

What does this show me about myself?

What should I do with what I have read?

Then pray and ask God to help you live out what you learned.

Remember

You do not have to understand the entire Bible at once.

Start small. Pray. Read. Learn. Ask questions. Seek wise guidance. Apply what you understand.

When you fail, repent and continue moving forward.

The goal is not simply to finish reading the Bible.

The goal is to allow God’s Word to help shape the person you are becoming in Christ.
