Psalm 3 — The LORD Is My Shield

A deeper study of David's trust in God while surrounded by opposition, fear, betrayal, and uncertainty.

Purpose of This Study

Psalm 3 shows us how faith responds when trouble becomes personal and overwhelming.

David is surrounded by enemies, many people believe he has no hope, and even his own son has turned against him. Yet David continues to pray, trust, rest, and look to God for deliverance.

Big Idea: Even when trouble surrounds us, God's presence and protection give His people confidence to pray, rest, and trust Him.

Historical Background

The heading of Psalm 3 identifies the setting as the time when David fled from his son Absalom.

The historical background is recorded in 2 Samuel 15–18. Absalom had gained the loyalty of many Israelites and led a rebellion against his father.

David was forced to leave Jerusalem and flee for his life. His crisis involved political rebellion, family betrayal, personal danger, public humiliation, and uncertainty about what would happen next.

Important Background Passages
  • 2 Samuel 15:13–14
  • 2 Samuel 15:23
  • 2 Samuel 16:5–8
  • 2 Samuel 17:1–4

Understanding this background helps us appreciate the strength of David's words. He is not writing about fear in theory. He is speaking from the middle of a genuine crisis.

Structure of Psalm 3

Study Outline
  • Psalm 3:1–2 — Trouble Multiplies
  • Psalm 3:3–4 — The LORD Is My Shield
  • Psalm 3:5–6 — Faith That Can Rest
  • Psalm 3:7–8 — Salvation Belongs to the LORD

Notice how the psalm moves. David begins by looking at his enemies, but then he turns his attention toward God.

His circumstances have not yet changed, but his perspective changes as he remembers who God is.

Psalm 3:1–2 — Trouble Multiplies

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

David begins by telling the LORD how greatly his enemies have increased.

Many have risen against him, and many are saying that God will not save him.

David does not minimize his danger. He honestly describes the seriousness of his situation.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

David is experiencing two kinds of attack.

First, he faces physical danger from people who want to remove him. Second, he faces spiritual discouragement from people claiming that God will no longer help him.

During suffering, one of the strongest temptations is to interpret God's faithfulness through our circumstances.

David's enemies look at his trouble and conclude that God has abandoned him. David will soon answer that claim by remembering who God is.

Biblical Truth — What Does This Teach Us?

Difficult circumstances do not prove that God has abandoned His people.

Truth to Remember: The voices around us do not determine whether God is faithful.

Application — How Should We Respond?

We should be honest with God about the pressure we are facing.

Faith does not require pretending that trouble is smaller than it really is.

But we must also be careful not to allow fear, other people, or difficult circumstances to define what we believe about God.

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus also experienced rejection, false accusations, betrayal, and opposition.

Yet He remained faithful to the Father. Christ reminds us that opposition does not mean God's purposes have failed.

Psalm 3:3–4 — The LORD Is My Shield

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

Verse 3 begins with an important contrast: David turns from speaking about his enemies to speaking about the LORD.

He describes God in three ways:

  • The LORD is his shield.
  • The LORD is his glory.
  • The LORD is the One who lifts his head.

David cries aloud to God, and God answers him.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

David's circumstances have not changed between verses 2 and 3.

The enemies are still present. The rebellion is still happening. The danger is still real.

What changes is David's focus.

A shield represents protection. David recognizes that his ultimate defense is not military strength or political influence, but God Himself.

Calling God his glory is especially significant because David had lost much of his public honor. He had been forced away from Jerusalem and his throne was threatened.

Yet David's deepest worth and identity did not come from his crown. His glory came from God.

The One who lifts his head is the God who restores courage, dignity, and hope.

Biblical Truth

God is able to protect, sustain, and restore confidence when His people feel surrounded.

Truth to Remember: Faith changes its focus from the greatness of the problem to the greatness of God.

Application

When trouble comes, what receives most of my attention: the problem or the character of God?

Prayer helps us turn our attention from what threatens us toward the One who sustains us.

Christ-Centered Application

Through Jesus Christ, believers have access to the Father and may confidently bring their fears, burdens, and needs before Him.

Our deepest identity does not come from reputation, position, possessions, or the approval of others.

Our security is found in our relationship with God through Christ.

Psalm 3:5–6 — Faith That Can Rest

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

David says that he lay down and slept.

He then woke again because the LORD sustained him.

David declares that he will not fear even if thousands surround him.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

David's sleep is one of the most powerful pictures of faith in this psalm.

He is not sleeping because the danger disappeared. He is sleeping while the danger still exists.

David entrusts himself to God during a situation he cannot control.

When he wakes, he recognizes that God sustained him through the night.

This does not mean believers will never experience fear or anxiety. It means fear does not have to become the controlling power of our lives.

Biblical Truth

God can give His people peace before their circumstances change.

Truth to Remember: Biblical peace is not the absence of trouble; it is confidence in God in the middle of trouble.

Application

What situation am I trying to control that I need to place into God's hands?

Trust involves taking reasonable action while also recognizing that our lives ultimately remain in God's care.

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus teaches His followers to trust the Father rather than live under the control of fear.

Through Christ, believers have a security deeper than changing circumstances.

Supporting Scriptures: Matthew 6:25–34; Philippians 4:6–7

Psalm 3:7–8 — Salvation Belongs to the LORD

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

David now asks God to arise and save him.

His language becomes strong as he asks God to defeat those who oppose him.

The psalm concludes with the declaration that salvation belongs to the LORD.

David then asks God's blessing to rest upon His people.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

David does not merely trust God passively. He actively prays for God to intervene.

His confidence is not ultimately in his own strength, his soldiers, his strategy, or his position as king.

Deliverance belongs to God.

David's final words also reveal that his concern has expanded beyond his personal survival.

Even in his own crisis, he asks God to bless His people.

Biblical Truth

God is the ultimate source of deliverance and salvation.

Truth to Remember: Our hope rests not in our ability to rescue ourselves, but in the God who saves.

Application

When I face something beyond my control, where do I instinctively look first for deliverance?

Faith does not prevent us from using wisdom or seeking help, but our ultimate confidence must remain in God.

Christ-Centered Application

Psalm 3 speaks of deliverance from immediate danger, but the Gospel reveals an even greater deliverance.

Through the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ, God provides salvation from sin, condemnation, and death.

Our greatest hope is therefore not merely that God changes our circumstances, but that we belong to Christ.

The Movement of Faith in Psalm 3

1. David acknowledges the problem.

He does not deny the seriousness of his circumstances.

2. David remembers who God is.

He turns from the voices of his enemies to the character of the LORD.

3. David prays.

He brings his situation directly to God and believes that God hears him.

4. David rests.

His trust becomes practical enough that he can sleep while danger remains.

5. David asks God to act.

Faith does not eliminate prayer for deliverance. David confidently asks God to intervene.

Major Truths in Psalm 3

1. Trouble does not mean God has abandoned us.

Circumstances are not the final measure of God's faithfulness.

2. God is our true source of protection.

Human resources have value, but our ultimate security rests in Him.

3. Our identity should be rooted in God.

David's throne could be taken away, but God remained his glory.

4. Faith can produce peace before circumstances change.

David was able to rest because he trusted the God who sustained him.

5. Salvation belongs to the LORD.

God is the ultimate source of deliverance and lasting hope.

Personal and Group Study Questions

  1. What two kinds of opposition does David describe in Psalm 3:1–2?
  2. Why is the word "but" so important as David turns from his enemies toward God?
  3. What does it mean for God to be David's shield?
  4. Why might David describe God as his glory during this particular crisis?
  5. What does David's ability to sleep teach us about trust?
  6. Does trusting God mean that danger is not real? Why or why not?
  7. What does David mean when he says salvation belongs to the LORD?
  8. What fear or burden do you need to place more fully into God's hands?

Putting Psalm 3 Into Practice

Read Psalm 3 once each day this week.

Each time you read it, identify one fear, burden, or discouraging voice that needs to be brought before God.

Then identify one truth about God's character that answers that fear.

Personal Question: What would change in the way I respond to trouble if I truly believed that God is my shield, my glory, and the One who sustains me?
