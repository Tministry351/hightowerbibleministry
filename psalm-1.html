Old Testament Verse-by-Verse Study

Psalm 1 - The Way of the Righteous and the Wicked

Psalm 1 introduces two ways of life: one shaped by God's Word and one shaped by rebellion against God. The psalm asks us to consider what influences us, what we delight in, and where the path we are walking ultimately leads.

Chapter Overview

Main Truth

The person who delights in God's instruction is pictured as rooted, fruitful, and stable, while the way of the wicked has no lasting foundation.

Study Aim

To identify the influences that shape our lives, develop delight in God's Word, and understand the difference between the path of the righteous and the path of the wicked.

Before you begin: Read Psalm 1:1-6 slowly in your preferred Bible translation. Notice repeated ideas, contrasts, actions, images, and results.
Study Sections:

Psalm 1:1 - The Influences We Reject

Observation

The blessed person is described by three things he does not do. The verse moves through the pictures of walking, standing, and sitting, while also describing the counsel, path, and company of people who reject God's way.

Interpretation

The verse warns that our thinking and conduct can be shaped by the voices and patterns around us. The progression suggests increasing identification with a way of life that is opposed to God.

Biblical Truth

God cares about the influences that shape our thinking, our choices, and the direction of our lives.

Application

What voices, relationships, entertainment, online content, or attitudes are having the strongest influence on the way I think?

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus perfectly obeyed the Father and never followed the counsel of wickedness. As His disciples, we are called to follow Christ rather than allow the surrounding culture to define what is true and good.

Psalm 1:2 - Delight in God's Word

Observation

The righteous person's life is not defined only by what is avoided. His delight is in the instruction of the Lord, and he continually meditates on it.

Interpretation

Biblical meditation is sustained attention to God's truth. The Word moves from something we merely read to something that shapes our thoughts, values, decisions, and desires.

Biblical Truth

Spiritual stability grows as God's Word becomes something we treasure, think about, understand, and obey.

Application

Is God's Word mainly something I hear occasionally, or is it becoming a regular influence on the way I think and live?

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus lived in complete submission to the Father's Word. Following Christ includes learning to value God's truth above competing voices.

Psalm 1:3 - The Fruitful Life

Observation

The righteous person is compared to a tree planted by streams of water. The picture includes roots, fruit, endurance, and stability.

Interpretation

The psalm does not promise a trouble-free life. It describes a life whose source and stability are outside changing circumstances. Fruit develops because the tree is rooted where it can continually receive what it needs.

Biblical Truth

A life rooted in God's truth develops spiritual fruit and stability over time.

Application

What evidence of spiritual fruit is growing in my life, and where do I still need deeper roots?

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus teaches that lasting fruit comes from abiding in Him. Christian growth is not produced by self-effort alone but through a continuing relationship with Christ and obedience to His Word.

Psalm 1:4-5 - The Wicked Are Not So

Observation

The wicked are contrasted with the firmly planted tree. They are compared to chaff - light, rootless, and driven away. The passage also looks ahead to judgment.

Interpretation

A life can appear successful for a time and still lack an eternal foundation. The psalm measures life from God's perspective, not merely by temporary appearance.

Biblical Truth

God sees beyond temporary success and will ultimately judge every life according to His righteousness.

Application

Am I evaluating success mainly by what is popular and impressive now, or by what matters before God?

Christ-Centered Application

The Gospel reminds us that our hope before God's judgment is not our own righteousness. We need the salvation God provides through Jesus Christ.

Psalm 1:6 - Two Ways, Two Destinations

Observation

The psalm ends with a final contrast between the way of the righteous and the way of the wicked.

Interpretation

Scripture presents life as morally significant. Our direction matters. God's knowledge of the righteous speaks of His attentive care, while the way of wickedness ultimately comes to ruin.

Biblical Truth

There are ultimately two ways to live: under God's rule or in rejection of it. Those paths do not lead to the same destination.

Application

Which path is shaping the direction of my life, and what step of obedience is God calling me to take?

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus calls people to follow Him. The Christian life is not merely avoiding the wrong path; it is trusting Christ and learning to walk in His way.

Putting Psalm 1 Into Practice

This Week

Read Psalm 1 once each day. Each time, identify one influence to evaluate and one truth from God's Word to meditate on.

Personal Question

What would change in my everyday life if I truly delighted in God's Word instead of merely knowing that I should read it?
