Psalm 1 — The Way of the Righteous and the Wicked

A deeper study of the two ways of life presented in Psalm 1: the life rooted in God's Word and the life that rejects His rule.

Purpose of This Study

Psalm 1 introduces the book of Psalms by presenting two different ways of life. One way is shaped by God's instruction. The other is shaped by rebellion against Him.

The psalm asks us to consider what influences us, what we delight in, what gives stability to our lives, and where the path we are walking will ultimately lead.

Big Idea: The person who delights in God's Word is pictured as rooted, fruitful, and stable, while the way of the wicked has no lasting foundation.

Understanding Psalm 1

Psalm 1 functions as an introduction to the Psalms. Before the reader enters the prayers, praises, struggles, and worship found throughout the book, Psalm 1 presents a foundational question: Which way will you walk?

The psalm contrasts two kinds of people, two sources of influence, two spiritual conditions, and ultimately two different destinations.

Psalm 1:1 — The Influences We Reject

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

The blessed person is described first by what he does not do. He does not walk in the counsel of the wicked, stand in the way of sinners, or sit in the seat of scoffers.

The verse uses the pictures of walking, standing, and sitting. It also describes counsel, conduct, and companionship.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

Psalm 1 warns us that our thinking and behavior can gradually be shaped by the people, ideas, values, and patterns we allow to influence us.

The movement from walking to standing to sitting suggests increasing identification with a way of life that rejects God's truth.

Biblical Truth — What Does This Teach Us About God?

God cares about the influences that shape our thinking, our choices, and the direction of our lives.

Truth to Remember: The direction of our lives is often shaped by the voices we continually allow to influence us.

Application — How Should We Respond?

We should carefully examine the voices, relationships, entertainment, online content, and attitudes that have the strongest influence upon us.

Ask yourself: Are these influences drawing me toward God's truth or slowly moving me away from it?

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus perfectly obeyed the Father and never followed the counsel of wickedness. As His disciples, we are called to follow Him rather than allow the surrounding culture to determine what is true and good.

Psalm 1:2 — Delight in God's Word

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

The righteous person is not defined only by what he avoids.

His delight is in the instruction of the Lord, and he continually meditates upon it.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

Biblical meditation means giving sustained attention to God's truth.

God's Word moves from something we merely read to something that shapes our thoughts, values, decisions, desires, and actions.

Biblical Truth

God has given His Word to instruct, guide, correct, and shape His people.

Truth to Remember: Spiritual stability grows as God's Word becomes something we treasure, think about, understand, and obey.

Application

Is God's Word mainly something I hear occasionally, or is it becoming a regular influence on the way I think and live?

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus lived in complete submission to the Father's will. Following Christ includes learning to value God's truth above competing voices.

Psalm 1:3 — The Fruitful Life

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

The righteous person is compared to a tree planted beside streams of water.

The picture includes roots, fruit, endurance, and stability.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

Psalm 1 does not promise a trouble-free life. It describes a life whose source and stability are deeper than changing circumstances.

A healthy tree produces fruit because it is rooted where it can continually receive what it needs.

Biblical Truth

God produces spiritual stability and fruitfulness in lives that are continually shaped by His truth.

Truth to Remember: A life rooted in God's truth develops spiritual fruit and stability over time.

Application

What evidence of spiritual fruit is growing in my life?

Where do I still need deeper roots in God's Word and obedience?

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus teaches that lasting spiritual fruit comes from abiding in Him.

Christian growth is not produced by self-effort alone, but through continuing dependence upon Christ and obedience to His Word.

Supporting Scripture: John 15:4–5

Psalm 1:4–5 — The Wicked Are Not So

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

The wicked are contrasted with the firmly planted tree.

Instead, they are compared to chaff: light, rootless, unstable, and easily driven away.

The passage also looks ahead to judgment.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

A life can appear successful for a time and still lack an eternal foundation.

Psalm 1 evaluates life from God's perspective, not merely by temporary appearance, popularity, comfort, or success.

Biblical Truth

God sees beyond outward appearance and will ultimately judge every life according to His righteousness.

Truth to Remember: What appears strong today may have no lasting foundation if it is separated from God.

Application

Am I measuring success mainly by what the world values, or by what God says has lasting value?

Christ-Centered Application

The Gospel reminds us that our hope before God's judgment is not our own righteousness.

We need the salvation God provides through Jesus Christ.

Psalm 1:6 — Two Ways, Two Destinations

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

Psalm 1 ends with a final contrast between the way of the righteous and the way of the wicked.

The Lord knows the way of the righteous, while the way of the wicked will perish.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

Scripture presents life as morally significant. Our direction matters.

God's knowledge of the righteous speaks of His attentive care, while the way of wickedness ultimately comes to ruin.

Biblical Truth

God knows those who belong to Him, and no path of rebellion against Him can ultimately succeed.

Truth to Remember: There are ultimately two ways to live: under God's rule or in rejection of it. Those paths do not lead to the same destination.

Application

Which path is shaping the direction of my life?

What step of obedience is God calling me to take?

Christ-Centered Application

Jesus calls people to follow Him. The Christian life is not merely avoiding the wrong path; it is trusting Christ and learning to walk in His way.

Major Truths in Psalm 1

1. Our influences matter.

The voices we continually listen to can shape the direction of our lives.

2. God's Word should become our delight.

Spiritual maturity involves more than reading Scripture. God's truth must increasingly shape our thinking and living.

3. Spiritual fruit develops from deep roots.

Stability and fruitfulness grow over time in a life continually nourished by God's truth.

4. Temporary appearance is not the final measure of life.

God sees beyond outward success and evaluates life from an eternal perspective.

5. Every path has a destination.

Psalm 1 calls us to examine not merely where we are today, but where the path we are walking is leading.

Personal and Group Study Questions

  1. What progression do you see in walking, standing, and sitting in Psalm 1:1?
  2. What kinds of influences most strongly shape your thinking today?
  3. What is the difference between simply reading God's Word and delighting and meditating upon it?
  4. What does the tree beside streams of water teach us about spiritual stability?
  5. Why is chaff an appropriate picture of a life without a lasting foundation?
  6. What does Psalm 1:6 reveal about God's relationship with the righteous?
  7. Which of the two ways described in Psalm 1 is shaping the direction of your life?

Putting Psalm 1 Into Practice

Read Psalm 1 once each day this week.

Each time you read it, identify one influence that needs to be evaluated and one truth from God's Word that deserves deeper meditation.

Personal Question: What would change in my everyday life if I truly delighted in God's Word instead of merely knowing that I should read it?
