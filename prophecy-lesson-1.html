Biblical Prophecy & End Times
Lesson 1

God's Eternal Plan for Mankind

From Creation to Eternity

Illustrated printable study guide coming soon.

Central Truth

Biblical prophecy is not merely a revelation of future events. It reveals God's eternal purpose, centered in Jesus Christ, through whom mankind is redeemed, believers are transformed, and God's people will ultimately dwell with Him forever.

Foundational Scripture

Isaiah 46:9–10 (KJV)

Remember the former things of old: for I am God, and there is none else; I am God, and there is none like me,

Declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times the things that are not yet done, saying, My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure.

God declares the end from the beginning because He possesses perfect knowledge and sovereign authority over His creation.

Biblical prophecy rests upon the character of God. His promises are dependable because His knowledge, faithfulness, and purposes do not fail.

Introduction

Before we examine the events associated with the End Times, we must first understand God's eternal purpose for mankind.

The Bible is God's revelation to mankind, encompassing the past, the present, and the future. Scripture records God's dealings with mankind throughout history and reveals His purposes for creation, redemption, transformation, restoration, and eternal life.

From Genesis to Revelation, we discover that God is not merely revealing what will happen in the future. He is revealing His purpose to redeem a people through Jesus Christ and prepare them to dwell with Him eternally.

Therefore, our study of biblical prophecy must begin with God's eternal purpose rather than with speculation concerning future events.

In-Depth Biblical Teaching

1. God Reveals the Past, Present, and Future

Isaiah 46:10 (KJV)

Declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times the things that are not yet done, saying, My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure.

God has revealed His eternal purposes through His Word. The Bible does not simply provide a collection of historical events or predictions concerning the future. It reveals the relationship between God's sovereign purpose and His dealings with mankind throughout history.

The Past — Creation and the Fall

Genesis reveals that God created mankind in His image and likeness. Humanity was created to reflect God's character and live in relationship with Him.

Through mankind's disobedience, sin entered the world, bringing death and disrupting fellowship with God. Yet mankind's fall did not bring God's eternal purpose to an end.

The Present — Redemption and Transformation

Through the sacrificial death and resurrection of Jesus Christ, God provides salvation to those who place their faith in Him.

Through the Holy Spirit, believers are being transformed into the likeness of Christ. God continues to work within His people, preparing them for eternal fellowship with Him.

The Future — Restoration and Eternity

Scripture reveals that Jesus Christ will return, God will bring His purposes to fulfillment, and His redeemed people will dwell with Him eternally.

Understanding the relationship between the past, present, and future helps us recognize the unity of God's redemptive purpose throughout Scripture.

Supporting Scriptures

  • Genesis 1:26–27 — Mankind was created in God's image.
  • Romans 5:12 — Sin and death entered the world.
  • John 3:16 — God provides eternal life through His Son.
  • Romans 8:29 — Believers are conformed to the image of Christ.
  • Revelation 21:1–4 — God will dwell with His redeemed people.

2. God's Redemptive Plan Existed Before Creation

1 Peter 1:18–20 (KJV)

Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers;

But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:

Who verily was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but was manifest in these last times for you.

Before God created the heavens and the earth, His eternal purpose for mankind had already been established.

In His foreknowledge, God knew that mankind would fall into sin. Yet He had already established His redemptive purpose through Jesus Christ.

Redemption was not an afterthought, an emergency response, or a correction to a failed plan. Scripture reveals that God's purpose and grace were established in Christ before the world began.

Ephesians 1:4 (KJV)

According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love.

Notice that God's purpose included not only redemption from sin but also holiness before Him.

Before mankind existed, God's purpose for those who are in Christ had already been established. Throughout history, God progressively revealed this purpose through His promises, His prophets, the coming of Jesus Christ, and the proclamation of the gospel.

God's Foreknowledge and Human Responsibility

God's foreknowledge of mankind's disobedience does not mean that God caused mankind to sin. Scripture teaches both God's sovereign knowledge and mankind's responsibility for disobedience.

The fall of mankind demonstrates the seriousness of sin, while God's provision of redemption demonstrates His grace, mercy, love, and wisdom.

Supporting Scriptures

  • 2 Timothy 1:9 — God's purpose and grace were given in Christ before the world began.
  • Titus 1:2 — God promised eternal life before the world began.
  • Genesis 3:15 — God announced the future defeat of the serpent.
  • Acts 2:23 — Christ's death occurred according to God's determined counsel and foreknowledge.
  • Ephesians 3:10–11 — God's eternal purpose is accomplished in Christ Jesus.

3. God's Purpose Is Both Redemption and Transformation

Philippians 2:12–13 (KJV)

Wherefore, my beloved, as ye have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling.

For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.

God's eternal purpose extends beyond forgiving mankind's sins. His desire is to transform believers into the likeness of Jesus Christ and prepare them for eternal fellowship with Him.

This ongoing process of spiritual transformation is commonly called sanctification.

Throughout the Christian life, God works within believers through the Holy Spirit, helping them overcome sinful habits, renew their minds, surrender worldly desires, and lay aside the burdens that hinder spiritual growth.

Working Out Our Salvation

Philippians 2:12–13 must be understood together. Paul is not teaching that believers must earn their salvation through human works.

Rather, believers are called to actively live out the salvation they have received, recognizing that God Himself is working within them.

We willingly respond to God's transforming work through faith, obedience, prayer, repentance, and the application of His Word.

Laying Aside Sin and Every Weight

Hebrews 12:1 (KJV)

Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us.

Scripture distinguishes between sins and weights. Some things that hinder spiritual growth may not necessarily be sinful in themselves, but they can become distractions or burdens that interfere with our spiritual development.

As believers mature, they increasingly desire to put away old thoughts, sinful habits, and attitudes that do not reflect the character of Christ.

These are not three separate salvations, but three aspects of God's saving work.

Becoming Living Examples of Jesus Christ

Our transformation has both a present purpose and an eternal purpose. God is preparing us for eternity while using our lives as a testimony to those around us today.

Our words, attitudes, relationships, decisions, and conduct should increasingly reflect the character of Jesus Christ.

God is not simply preparing a place for His people; He is also preparing His people for that place.

Supporting Scriptures

  • Romans 8:29 — Conformed to the image of God's Son.
  • Romans 12:2 — Transformed through the renewing of our minds.
  • Ephesians 4:22–24 — Put off the old self and put on the new.
  • Galatians 5:22–23 — Demonstrate the fruit of the Holy Spirit.
  • Matthew 5:16 — Let our light shine before others.
  • 1 Timothy 4:12 — Be an example to believers.

4. Jesus Christ Is the Center of God's Eternal Plan

Ephesians 1:9–10 (KJV)

Having made known unto us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he hath purposed in himself:

That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him.

From the beginning of creation to the fulfillment of God's eternal kingdom, Jesus Christ occupies the central position in God's revealed plan.

The Old Testament points toward His coming. The Gospels reveal His earthly ministry, sacrificial death, and resurrection.

The Epistles explain His redemptive work and continuing ministry, while Revelation reveals His glorious victory and the fulfillment of God's eternal purposes.

Revelation 19:10 (KJV)

For the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy.

Biblical prophecy bears witness to Jesus Christ, His identity, His redemptive work, His authority, and His ultimate victory.

Jesus Revealed Throughout Scripture

Following His resurrection, Jesus explained to His disciples how the Scriptures pointed toward Him.

Luke 24:27 (KJV)

And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.

Our study of the End Times must therefore remain centered upon Jesus Christ rather than merely upon prophetic events.

Although understanding future events is important, our greatest concern should be knowing Christ, becoming more like Him, remaining faithful to Him, and preparing for His return.

These subjects will be examined individually in later lessons. Our primary concern here is their relationship to Jesus Christ, rather than establishing a prophetic timetable.

Supporting Scriptures

  • Colossians 1:16–17 — All things were created through Christ and for Him.
  • Isaiah 53 — The suffering and redemptive work of the promised Servant.
  • John 1:14 — The eternal Word became flesh.
  • 1 Corinthians 15:3–4 — Christ died for our sins and rose again.
  • Matthew 24:30 — Christ will return in power and glory.
  • Revelation 22:1–5 — God and the Lamb are central to the eternal kingdom.

5. God's Ultimate Purpose — Dwelling With His People

Revelation 21:3–4 (KJV)

And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.

And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.

The ultimate hope of every believer is to live eternally in the presence of God and Jesus Christ.

From the beginning of Scripture to its conclusion, God reveals His desire to dwell with His people. Through the redemptive work of Jesus Christ, believers are reconciled to God and are being transformed in preparation for eternal fellowship with Him.

An Eternity of Worship, Praise, and Service

Revelation 22:3–5 (KJV)

And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him:

And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads.

And there shall be no night there; and they need no candle, neither light of the sun; for the Lord God giveth them light: and they shall reign for ever and ever.

Eternal life involves more than simply existing forever. Scripture reveals that God's people will serve Him, see His face, experience His presence, and reign forever.

Eternity will be a life of purpose, fellowship, worship, service, and joy.

Eternal Life Is More Than Living Forever

John 17:3 (KJV)

And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent.

Eternal life is not merely about the duration of life but also about the relationship believers have with God through Jesus Christ.

That relationship begins now and reaches its fullness in eternity.

Although freedom from sickness, pain, suffering, and death is a wonderful promise, the greatest blessing of eternal life is God Himself.

The ultimate goal of the believer is eternal fellowship with God and Jesus Christ, worshipping, praising, and serving Him forever.

Practical Christian Application

Understanding God's eternal purpose should influence how we live today.

Biblical prophecy should not merely increase our knowledge of future events. It should strengthen our faith, encourage spiritual growth, and motivate us to live in anticipation of Christ's return.

  • Trust God's sovereignty. Remember that His eternal purposes are not defeated by the uncertainties of this world.
  • Keep Jesus Christ central. Do not allow speculation about prophetic events to become more important than your relationship with Christ.
  • Participate in spiritual transformation. Identify sinful habits, old attitudes, and burdens that hinder your spiritual growth. Submit these areas to God through prayer and obedience to His Word.
  • Become an example to others. Allow your daily conduct to reflect the character and love of Jesus Christ.
  • Live with an eternal perspective. Remember that the difficulties of this present life do not compare with the glory God has promised His people.
  • Share the hope of the gospel. Encourage others to discover the eternal life God offers through Jesus Christ.
Romans 8:18 (KJV)

For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.

Questions for Deeper Understanding

  1. Why is it important to understand God's eternal purpose before studying individual End Times events?
  2. What does God's redemptive plan before creation reveal about His sovereignty and foreknowledge?
  3. How is God transforming your life in preparation for eternity?
  4. Why must Jesus Christ remain the central focus of biblical prophecy?
  5. How should the promise of dwelling eternally with God influence the way you live today?

Conclusion

God's eternal plan for mankind was established before the foundation of the world. Throughout Scripture, He progressively reveals His purpose to redeem mankind through Jesus Christ, transform believers into His likeness, and prepare His people for eternal fellowship.

Jesus Christ remains the center of this eternal purpose. Through Him, we receive redemption, experience spiritual transformation, and look forward to the fulfillment of God's promises.

As we continue studying Biblical Prophecy and the End Times, we must never lose sight of God's ultimate purpose.

Our greatest hope is not merely understanding what will happen in the future, but living eternally in the presence of God and Jesus Christ.

Remember

Biblical prophecy is not merely a revelation of future events. It is a revelation of God's eternal purpose, centered in Jesus Christ, through whom mankind is redeemed, believers are transformed, and God's people will ultimately dwell with Him forever.

God is not simply preparing a place for His people; He is also preparing His people for that place.
