The Holiness of God

Primary Scripture: Isaiah 6:1–13   |   Additional readings: Hebrews 11:13, 39–40; 1 Corinthians 3:6–7; Hebrews 9:13–14

Objective: Understand God's holiness, Isaiah's need for cleansing, and how grace leads to humble obedience and faithful service.

Introduction — The Holy God Who Calls

Knowing God's attributes is more than learning facts about Him. It should shape our worship, trust, repentance, and obedience. Isaiah 6 shows us how a glimpse of God's holiness changed Isaiah's understanding of himself and prepared him to respond to God's call.

Central truth: God's holiness exposes our need for His grace. His grace calls forth faith, gratitude, and willing obedience. We are called to serve faithfully and leave the results in His hands.

Read first: Isaiah 6:1–13 slowly. Verses 1–8 describe Isaiah's vision, confession, cleansing, and response. Verses 9–13 reveal the difficulty of the assignment he received.

1. Observation — What Does Scripture Say?

Isaiah 6:1–4 — The vision of God's holiness

  • Isaiah sees the Lord seated upon a throne, high and lifted up.
  • Seraphim are present, proclaiming the Lord's holiness.
  • The temple is shaken and filled with smoke.
  • God's glory is described as filling the earth.
“Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts.” — Isaiah 6:3 (ESV)

Isaiah 6:5 — Isaiah's confession

Isaiah declares his distress, acknowledges that his lips are unclean, and identifies himself as living among a people of unclean lips. He sees himself differently when confronted with God's holiness.

Careful observation: The passage records Isaiah's confession and alarm; it does not explicitly say that he fell on his face.

Isaiah 6:6–7 — God's provision for cleansing

One of the seraphim takes a burning coal from the altar and touches Isaiah's mouth. The seraph announces that Isaiah's guilt has been taken away and his sin atoned for. The cleansing comes from God's provision, not Isaiah's ability to make himself pure.

Isaiah 6:8–13 — The call and the difficult commission

When the Lord asks whom He shall send, Isaiah responds willingly. The task that follows is difficult: many hearers will resist the message. The passage also points to a remnant and the imagery of a holy seed in the stump (verse 13).

“Here I am! Send me.” — Isaiah 6:8 (ESV)

2. Interpretation — What Does the Passage Mean?

A. God is perfectly holy

Holiness speaks of God's incomparable majesty and moral purity. He is set apart from all creation and free from sin. The threefold declaration of “holy” intensifies the emphasis on who God is.

B. God's holiness exposes human sin

Isaiah does not measure himself against the people around him. In the presence of God's holiness, he recognizes his own uncleanness. This is not a lesson in despair for its own sake, but a truthful recognition of humanity's need for God.

C. God graciously provides cleansing

Isaiah cannot remove his own guilt. The coal comes from the altar, and the announcement of atonement follows God's initiative. Within Christian theology, this foreshadows the larger biblical theme that God provides the cleansing sinners need; Hebrews 9:13–14 directs believers to Christ's superior and decisive cleansing.

D. Grace leads to willing service

Isaiah's “Here I am” follows his cleansing. His service does not purchase God's grace. Rather, his willingness to answer expresses a response to the God who has shown mercy.

E. Faithfulness is not measured only by visible acceptance

Isaiah 6:9–13 warns that his message will meet resistance. God gives him a commission, not a promise that every hearer will respond positively. Hebrews 11:13 and 39–40 describe faithful people who did not receive the complete fulfillment of God's promises during their lifetimes. Some witnessed remarkable deliverances; others endured suffering. Their faithfulness was not defined by outward success.

Before I prepare to explain holiness to someone else, am I willing to let this passage examine my own heart?

Reflect quietly: Do I approach God with reverence? When Scripture reveals sin in my life, do I confess it or excuse it? Am I serving God to receive recognition, or because I love and trust Him?

4. Application — How Should This Change My Life?

God is holy and worthy of reverence.Approach Him in worship with humility, awe, and gratitude.
His holiness exposes sin.Confess what His Word reveals rather than comparing myself with others.
God provides cleansing by grace.Depend on His mercy and Christ's saving work, not on personal merit.
God calls His people to obedience.Offer myself willingly, while seeking His guidance and strength.
Faithful service can meet resistance.Teach truth with love and patience, trusting God with the outcome.

Personal commitment — Faithful obedience

There was a time when I was deeply concerned about seeing results from my efforts. Through the years, I have learned that my responsibility is to remain faithful and obedient to God.

I cannot control the results, and I do not need to carry that burden. My responsibility is to study His Word, live according to what I learn, and serve Him with the strength and opportunities He provides. I will leave the outcome in His hands.

Important balance: Entrusting the outcome to God does not mean neglecting careful preparation, wise stewardship, or concern for the people we serve. It means the response of others does not determine our willingness to obey.

Christ-centered connection: Our hope is not that we can become holy enough by effort alone. Through Jesus Christ, God provides reconciliation and cleansing; by His Spirit, believers are called to grow in holiness and obedience (Hebrews 9:13–14; 1 Peter 1:15–16).

6. Weekly Assignment and Closing Prayer

Read: Isaiah 6:1–13 and Hebrews 11:32–40. Pay attention to the different ways faithful people responded to God in difficult circumstances.

Practice: Each day, identify one area where you need to submit to God's Word. Pray about it and take one specific step of obedience. Ask God to help you serve without needing to control the outcome.

Suggested memory verse: Isaiah 6:8.

Prayer

Holy Father, thank You for revealing Your holiness and showing mercy to sinners. Examine my heart through Your Word. Help me confess my sin, trust in the cleansing provided through Jesus Christ, and serve You willingly. Give me courage to obey even when I cannot see the results. Keep my heart humble and faithful. In Jesus' name, amen.

Lesson conclusion: God's holiness leads us to humility; His grace calls us to obedience; His faithfulness enables us to entrust the outcome to Him.
