Living by Faith

Trusting God Beyond What We See

Biblical faith is trusting God because of who He is and what He has revealed in Scripture, even when circumstances are uncertain and feelings change.

Primary Scriptures

Primary Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:7

Supporting Scriptures: Hebrews 11:1–6 • Romans 10:17 • Proverbs 3:5–6 • Mark 9:23–24
Central Teaching Truth

Biblical faith is trusting God because of who He is and what He has revealed in Scripture, even when circumstances are uncertain and feelings change.

Lesson Objective

Understand biblical faith, distinguish faith from feelings, and practice trusting God's Word through everyday obedience.

Introduction — When the Outcome Is Unclear

Living by faith becomes most difficult when the outcome is unclear. We may pray about a family problem, a financial concern, a health issue, or a difficult decision without seeing an immediate answer.

In those moments, faith does not mean pretending that uncertainty is easy. Biblical faith means trusting God because of who He is and because of what He has revealed in His Word.

Second Corinthians 5:7 teaches that believers walk by faith, not by sight. That means our confidence cannot depend only on what we can see, understand, or feel at the moment.

Faith looks beyond present circumstances and rests in the character, promises, wisdom, and faithfulness of God.

1. What Biblical Faith Really Is

Hebrews 11:1-6 and 2 Corinthians 5:7 give us a clear picture of biblical faith. Faith is not wishful thinking, positive thinking, or pretending that problems do not exist. Faith is confidence in God based on who He is and what He has revealed.

Hebrews 11 teaches that faith trusts what God has said even when the final outcome cannot yet be seen. Second Corinthians 5:7 reminds believers that we walk by faith, not by sight.

Biblical faith therefore rests on God's character, God's promises, and God's Word rather than on changing circumstances.

Teaching Truth: Faith is not confidence in ourselves. Faith is confidence in God.

2. Faith Is Rooted in God, Not in Feelings

Biblical faith always has an object: the living God revealed in Jesus Christ. Faith is not optimism, positive thinking, or confidence in our own ability.

Romans 10:17 teaches that faith grows through hearing the message concerning Christ. Biblical faith is strengthened as we listen to, understand, and trust what God has spoken in His Word.

Hebrews 11 shows people obeying God without knowing every detail of what would happen. Faith does not require us to understand everything before we obey. It requires us to trust the One who has spoken.

Faith does not eliminate questions, grief, fear, or uncertainty. It teaches us to trust God's character and follow His Word even when circumstances remain unclear.

Feelings are real, but they are changeable. They can inform us about what we are experiencing, but they cannot serve as our final authority.

Scripture teaches us to measure our thoughts, feelings, and responses against God's truth rather than treating emotional certainty as proof of God's presence or favor.

Teaching Truth: Faith is grounded in God's truth, not in the stability of our emotions.

3. Application — Putting Faith Into Practice

Trusting God does not mean pretending that pain, fear, or unanswered questions do not exist.

In Mark 9:24, a father brought both belief and weakness to Jesus.

Honest prayer and faithful obedience can coexist with uncertainty.

5. Encouraging Others Who Are Waiting on God

When someone is waiting on God, we should not assume that unanswered prayer means weak faith. Faithful believers may experience long seasons of waiting, uncertainty, grief, or unanswered questions.

We encourage others by reminding them of God's character, praying with them, pointing them back to Scripture, and walking beside them without giving shallow explanations for what God has not yet revealed.

Teaching Truth: Biblical encouragement does not condemn someone for struggling. It helps them continue trusting God while they wait.
