Lesson 3: Why Do We Need Salvation?

Foundational Scripture: Romans 3:23

Romans 3:23 (KJV)

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;”

Welcome — Why Does Salvation Matter?

You may wonder why Christians talk so much about being saved. Saved from what? And why would a person who tries to do good need salvation?

The Bible begins with an important truth: God created humanity for a relationship with Him. But sin has affected every one of us. The good news is that God has made a way for us to receive forgiveness and new life through Jesus Christ.

1. What Is Sin?

Sin is more than making a mistake. It includes our actions, thoughts, and attitudes that go against God’s good and holy ways. Sometimes we do what we know is wrong; other times we fail to do what is right (James 4:17).

Romans 3:23 says that all have sinned. No one can claim to have lived a perfectly sinless life, and none of us can make ourselves right with God by comparing ourselves to someone else.

Remember: Sin is a problem shared by all of us, not just by people we consider especially bad.

2. Why Does Sin Matter?

God is holy and just. Sin harms our relationship with Him, with others, and even with ourselves. Isaiah 59:2 describes how sin separates people from God.

Romans 6:23 says, “For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.” The consequence of sin is serious, but this verse also gives us hope.

Remember: God does not ignore sin, yet He offers us the gift of eternal life in Jesus Christ.

3. Can Good Works Save Us?

Doing good, helping others, and attending church can be valuable. But none of these things can erase our sins or earn salvation.

Ephesians 2:8–9 teaches that salvation is by God’s grace through faith, not something we can boast of achieving ourselves. Grace means God gives us what we could never earn.

We do not work to purchase God’s forgiveness. We receive His gift through faith in Jesus Christ.

4. What Has God Done to Save Us?

God’s love is shown in Jesus Christ. Romans 5:8 teaches that Christ died for us while we were still sinners. Jesus died for our sins, was buried, and rose again (1 Corinthians 15:3–4).

Because of Jesus, forgiveness and a right relationship with God are possible. Salvation is not a reward for already being good; it is good news for people who know they need a Savior.

Remember: The gospel is the good news of what Jesus Christ has done for us.

5. How Should I Respond?

Jesus calls people to repent and believe the gospel (Mark 1:15). To repent is to turn from sin toward God, rather than choosing to continue in our old ways.

Romans 10:9–10 connects confessing Jesus as Lord with believing in our hearts that God raised Him from the dead. Faith is more than knowing about Jesus; it means trusting Him.

If you are exploring faith, begin by reading these passages and asking God to help you understand who Jesus is. You do not need to pretend to have every answer.

Something to Think About

  1. What does Romans 3:23 teach about all of us?
  2. Why can good behavior not earn salvation?
  3. What does Romans 5:8 tell me about God’s love?
  4. What would it mean for me to repent and trust Jesus Christ?

Take your time with these questions. You are welcome to continue reading Scripture and asking questions as you learn.
