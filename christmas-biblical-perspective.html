Christmas: Have We Lost Sight of Jesus Christ?

A Biblical Conversation About Faith, Tradition, and Truth

1. A Personal Reflection on Christmas

When I think about Christmas today, I see a society that places tremendous emphasis on Santa Claus, Christmas trees, decorations, colorful lights, buying presents, and exchanging gifts.

We see families gathering together, people showing kindness, and individuals giving and receiving gifts from family and friends, also to those who are less fortunate. These are not necessarily bad things. Giving, loving others, and spending time with family can be wonderful expressions of kindness.

However, my concern is that somewhere along the way, the true identity and purpose of Jesus Christ have been overshadowed by the traditions and activities surrounding Christmas.

I understand why Christian parents want their children to experience the excitement of Christmas morning. My concern is not simply about gifts or family gatherings.

My concern is whether we are teaching our children and ourselves who Jesus Christ truly is and why He came into the world.

The Bible tells us about the birth of Jesus Christ, but it does not establish December 25 as the date of His birth.

Jesus did not come merely so humanity could commemorate His birth once a year. He came to fulfill God's redemptive purpose.

Matthew 1:21; Luke 2:10-11; John 1:14; John 3:16-17; 1 Timothy 1:15

2. Traditions of Mankind

Jesus warned against placing human traditions above the commandments of God.

Mark 7:8-9 (KJV)

"For laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men, as the washing of pots and cups: and many other such like things ye do.

And he said unto them, Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition."

In its context, Jesus was confronting religious leaders whose traditions displaced God's commands. The passage does not specifically address Christmas, nor does it condemn every human tradition.

Nevertheless, it challenges believers to ask whether any tradition has become more important than obedience to God's Word.

I believe that as Christians mature in faith, we should reconsider practices we have accepted simply because they were passed down to us.

Christmas is a tradition of mankind, but Jesus Christ is not a tradition. He is the eternal Son of God, our Lord and Savior.

3. The Historical Origins of Christmas

Why December 25?

Scripture does not identify the exact date of Jesus' birth or command Christians to celebrate His birthday annually.

December 25 became an established Christian feast date in fourth-century Rome. Historians discuss several possible explanations, including connections with Roman winter observances and early Christian calculations concerning the conception and birth of Jesus.

The evidence does not establish one universally accepted explanation for the choice of this date.

Where Did Santa Claus Come From?

The Santa Claus tradition developed partly from stories associated with Saint Nicholas, a fourth-century Christian bishop of Myra remembered for generosity.

Dutch Sinterklaas customs and later American literature and illustrations contributed to the development of the modern Santa Claus character.

The familiar story of a man traveling in a flying sleigh with reindeer and delivering presents around the world is not a biblical teaching.

Where Did Christmas Trees Originate?

Evergreen plants were used in various ancient winter customs. Decorated Christmas trees, more closely resembling those used today, developed in German-speaking Europe.

While some winter customs predate Christianity, the history of the modern Christmas tree is complex. Its use as decoration should not automatically be equated with worshiping an idol.

Scriptures to Examine:

Jeremiah 10:1-5; Exodus 20:3-5; Romans 14:5-6; Colossians 2:16

Jeremiah 10 describes the making of idols. We should examine its context carefully rather than claiming that it directly describes the modern Christmas tree.

Historical accounts help us understand traditions, but Scripture remains the authority for Christian faith and conduct.

4. Santa Claus, Children, and Truthfulness

Many children grow up believing that Santa Claus is a real person who delivers gifts on Christmas Eve. Later, they discover that the story was not literally true.

I recognize that many sincere Christian families celebrate Christmas while faithfully teaching their children about Jesus Christ.

However, I am concerned when Santa Claus and holiday excitement receive more attention than the Savior.

Parents should consider the difference between imaginative storytelling and presenting fiction as fact. Not every child will experience a loss of trust, but honesty matters in Christian parenting.

Scriptures:

Ephesians 4:25; Proverbs 12:22; Deuteronomy 6:6-7; Ephesians 6:4

If we teach children to believe in Jesus Christ, should we not also demonstrate truthfulness in the way we raise them?

5. Beyond the Manger: The Biblical Account

The Birth of Jesus — Luke 2:1-20

Luke records that Jesus was laid in a manger and that shepherds received the angelic announcement of His birth.

The announcement was not merely that a child had been born. The Savior, Christ the Lord, had entered the world.

The Wise Men — Matthew 2:1-12

Traditional nativity scenes often show the wise men beside the manger. However, Matthew 2:11 records that they entered a house and saw the young child with Mary.

Scripture does not specify exactly three wise men. It records three gifts: gold, frankincense, and myrrh. The precise date of their visit is not given.

King Herod — Matthew 2:13-18

Herod sought to destroy Jesus after learning of His birth. He ordered the killing of young boys in Bethlehem and its surrounding region.

God warned Joseph in a dream, and Joseph took Mary and Jesus into Egypt.

These events reveal that the birth of Christ was accompanied not only by rejoicing but also by opposition.

6. Who Is Jesus Christ, and Why Did He Come?

The birth of Jesus marked the beginning of His earthly life, not the beginning of His existence.

John 1:1 and John 1:14 reveal that the Word was God and became flesh.

Matthew 1:23 identifies Jesus as Emmanuel, meaning "God with us."

Jesus did not enter the world simply to become the center of an annual holiday.

He came to save sinners, reveal the Father, proclaim the kingdom of God, and accomplish the work of redemption.

Key Bible References
  • Matthew 1:21 — Saving His people from sin
  • Luke 19:10 — Seeking and saving the lost
  • John 3:16-17 — God's saving love
  • Philippians 2:5-11 — Christ's humility and deity
  • 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 — Death and resurrection
  • Hebrews 9:26-28 — Sacrifice and future appearing
  • Acts 1:9-11 — His ascension and promised return

We cannot fully understand the significance of the manger without understanding the cross, the resurrection, and the promise of Christ's return.

7. Christian Liberty and Spiritual Growth

Christians have different convictions about observing Christmas.

Romans 14:5-6 teaches that believers may regard particular days differently while seeking to honor the Lord.

Colossians 2:16 warns against allowing others to judge believers regarding religious observances.

These passages deserve careful examination alongside the warnings about human traditions.

My desire is not to condemn Christians who celebrate Christmas. Rather, I encourage believers to examine their practices as they mature in their understanding of Scripture.

Our relationship with Christ should never be measured simply by whether we celebrate December 25.

8. Why Does Knowing the Truth Matter?

Hosea 4:6 (KJV)

"My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge: because thou hast rejected knowledge, I will also reject thee..."

Hosea addressed Israel's unfaithfulness and the rejection of God's law and knowledge.

This does not mean someone will be condemned simply for not knowing the history of Christmas.

But it reminds us of the seriousness of rejecting God's truth. We should study Scripture and respond faithfully to what we learn.

Additional Scriptures: Acts 17:11; 2 Timothy 2:15; John 8:31-32; 2 Corinthians 5:10.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  1. Have traditions overshadowed the true identity of Jesus Christ?
  2. Are we teaching our children biblical truth or simply passing down customs?
  3. Could a tradition distract us from Christ, even when our intentions are good?
  4. Are we willing to reconsider practices that Scripture does not require?
  5. How can we honor Jesus Christ every day, rather than focusing on one holiday?

9. Beyond Christmas: Do You Truly Know Jesus Christ?

Throughout this study, we have examined Christmas traditions, historical origins, the birth of Jesus Christ, and the importance of knowing biblical truth.

But I would like to leave you with something far more important than whether you choose to celebrate Christmas.

Do you truly know Jesus Christ?

It is possible to know the Christmas story without personally knowing the Savior.

We can recognize the manger, shepherds, wise men, and star of Bethlehem. We can exchange gifts, sing songs, and attend church services.

Yet none of these activities alone establishes a saving relationship with Jesus Christ.

John 14:6 (KJV)

"I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me."

Jesus came to save sinners, reconcile us to God, and offer eternal life through His death and resurrection.

A Personal Invitation

If you have never placed your faith in Jesus Christ, I encourage you to examine His Word, recognize your need for salvation, repent of your sins, and trust Him as Lord and Savior.

If you already belong to Christ, continue growing in His Word, allowing the Holy Spirit to transform your heart, thinking, and life.

Further Study: John 3:3; Romans 3:23; Romans 5:8; Romans 10:9-10; 2 Corinthians 5:17; John 17:3.

Don't allow the traditions of one particular day to become more important than following Jesus Christ every day.

John 8:31-32 (KJV)

"Then said Jesus to those Jews which believed on him, If ye continue in my word, then are ye my disciples indeed;

And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free."

Continue Studying God's Word

We encourage you to examine the Scriptures and grow in your relationship with Jesus Christ.
