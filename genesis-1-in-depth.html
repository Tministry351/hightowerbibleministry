Old Testament In-Depth Study

Genesis 1 — In the Beginning, God

A deeper study of God as Creator, the order and goodness of creation, humanity made in God's image, and the foundation Genesis 1 lays for the rest of Scripture.

Purpose of This Study

Genesis 1 introduces us to the God who existed before creation and brought the heavens and the earth into being.

The chapter establishes foundational truths about God, creation, humanity, purpose, authority, order, and goodness.

Genesis 1 is not merely an account of how the biblical story begins. It tells us who God is, who we are, and why the world belongs to Him.

Big Idea: The sovereign God created all things by His word, ordered His creation according to His purpose, and made humanity in His image to represent Him within the world He created.

Before You Begin

Read Genesis 1 slowly in your preferred Bible translation.

As you read, pay attention to repeated words, commands, responses, patterns, evaluations, and distinctions.

Look especially for these repeated ideas:
  • God said
  • It was so
  • God saw that it was good
  • Evening and morning
  • According to their kinds
  • God blessed

These repetitions help us see that creation is not presented as accidental or chaotic. God speaks, creation responds, and everything develops according to His will.

Structure of Genesis 1

Study Outline

  • Genesis 1:6–8 — The Expanse
  • Genesis 1:9–13 — Land, Seas, and Vegetation
  • Genesis 1:14–19 — Lights in the Heavens
  • Genesis 1:20–23 — Creatures of Sea and Sky
  • Genesis 1:24–25 — Land Animals

The chapter moves from an unformed and unfilled earth toward an ordered and inhabited creation.

God forms environments and then fills those environments with life. The chapter reaches its high point with the creation of humanity in God's image.

Genesis 1:1–2 — God Before Creation

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

Genesis begins with God. Before anything else in the biblical story is described, God is already present.

God creates the heavens and the earth. The earth is described as formless and empty, darkness is over the surface of the deep, and the Spirit of God is present over the waters.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

Genesis does not begin by explaining where God came from. It begins with God as the eternal starting point of everything that exists.

The words "heavens and earth" describe the totality of creation. God is not part of creation. He is the Creator who exists before it and brings it into being.

Verse 2 presents creation before the ordering work described throughout the rest of the chapter.

The presence of the Spirit of God shows that God is actively involved from the beginning of creation.

Biblical Truth

God is eternal, sovereign, and the Creator of everything that exists.

Truth to Remember: The Bible begins with God because all reality ultimately begins with Him.

Application

If God is my Creator, then my life does not ultimately belong to me.

My identity, purpose, choices, and priorities should be shaped by the One who created me.

Christ-Centered Application

The New Testament reveals that Jesus Christ was present in God's work of creation.

John 1 and Colossians 1 teach that all things were created through Him. The Creator who made us is also the Savior who came to redeem us.

Genesis 1:3–5 — Light and Darkness

Observation

God speaks, light appears, and God sees that the light is good.

God separates light from darkness and names them day and night.

Interpretation

God's spoken word is effective. He commands, and creation responds.

God also establishes distinctions within creation. Light and darkness are separated, named, and placed within an ordered pattern.

Biblical Truth

God's word carries authority and power. What God commands is accomplished.

Truth to Remember: God brings order where there was disorder and light where there was darkness.

Application

Do I treat God's Word as the authoritative voice that should shape my thinking and life?

Christ-Centered Application

The New Testament describes Jesus as the true Light who enters a dark world.

The God who spoke light into creation is also able to bring spiritual light into human hearts through Jesus Christ.

Genesis 1:6–8 — The Expanse

Observation

God separates the waters, creating an expanse between them. God names the expanse heaven or sky.

Interpretation

Again we see God bringing structure and distinction to creation.

Genesis presents the world as ordered by God's command rather than governed by competing divine powers.

Biblical Truth

God is Lord over the world He created. Nothing in creation operates outside His authority.

Application

Because creation belongs to God, I should approach the world with humility, gratitude, and responsibility.

Genesis 1:9–13 — Land, Seas, and Vegetation

Observation

God gathers the waters together so that dry land appears.

He names the dry land earth and the gathered waters seas.

God then commands the land to produce vegetation, plants, and fruit-bearing trees.

Interpretation

God creates an environment capable of sustaining life.

The repeated phrase "according to their kinds" emphasizes order, distinction, and fruitfulness within creation.

Biblical Truth

God not only creates life; He provides what life needs to continue.

Truth to Remember: God's creation reflects order, purpose, provision, and fruitfulness.

Application

God's provision should lead us to gratitude rather than entitlement.

The resources of creation are gifts entrusted to us, not possessions independent of God.

Genesis 1:14–19 — Lights in the Heavens

Observation

God appoints lights in the heavens to separate day from night and to mark seasons, days, and years.

The greater light governs the day, the lesser light governs the night, and the stars are also created by God.

Interpretation

The sun, moon, and stars are not presented as gods. They are created objects placed within God's creation to serve His purposes.

Time itself is organized within the order God establishes.

Biblical Truth

The heavenly bodies belong to God and function under His authority.

Application

Creation should move us to worship the Creator, not the things He created.

Genesis 1:20–23 — Creatures of Sea and Sky

Observation

God fills the waters with living creatures and the sky with birds.

God sees that His work is good and blesses these creatures with the ability to be fruitful and multiply.

Interpretation

Life exists because God gives it. The variety and abundance of living creatures reflect His creative wisdom.

This is also the first explicit blessing recorded in Scripture.

Biblical Truth

God delights in life and gives His creatures the capacity to flourish within the world He made.

Application

The abundance and diversity of creation should deepen our appreciation for God's wisdom and generosity.

Genesis 1:24–25 — Land Animals

Observation

God commands the earth to bring forth living creatures: livestock, creatures that move along the ground, and wild animals.

They are produced according to their kinds, and God sees that His work is good.

Interpretation

The repeated pattern continues: God speaks, creation responds, and God evaluates what He has made.

Biblical Truth

The animal world is part of God's good creation and exists under His authority and care.

Genesis 1:26–28 — Humanity in God's Image

Observation

The creation of humanity is described differently from the creation of the animals.

God declares that mankind will be made in His image and likeness.

Humanity is created male and female, blessed by God, commanded to be fruitful and multiply, and given responsibility within creation.

Interpretation

Being made in God's image gives human beings a unique place within creation.

Humanity is created to represent God, live under His authority, exercise responsible stewardship, and reflect something of His character within the world.

Both male and female share equally in the dignity of being created in God's image.

Humanity receives authority over creation, but this authority is delegated. People remain accountable to the Creator whose world they oversee.

Biblical Truth

Every human life possesses dignity and value because humanity is created in God's image.

Truth to Remember: Human worth does not come from wealth, ability, status, race, age, achievement, or usefulness. Human dignity is rooted in being created in God's image.

Application

How should the image of God affect the way I view myself and the way I treat other people?

My life has purpose because I was created to know God, honor Him, and represent Him faithfully.

Christ-Centered Application

Sin later damages humanity's faithful reflection of God, but the New Testament presents Jesus Christ as the perfect image of God.

Through Christ, believers are being transformed increasingly into His likeness.

Genesis 1:29–31 — God's Provision and a Very Good Creation

Observation

God provides food for humanity and the creatures He made.

At the conclusion of the sixth day, God looks upon everything He has made and declares the whole creation very good.

Interpretation

Earlier in the chapter, individual parts of creation are repeatedly called good.

Now the completed creation is described as very good.

The world as God originally created it reflects His wisdom, goodness, order, and purpose.

Biblical Truth

God's original creation is good, and His provision demonstrates His care for the life He created.

Truth to Remember: Creation is good because it comes from a good Creator and exists according to His purpose.

Application

The goodness of creation should produce gratitude, worship, humility, and responsible stewardship.

Major Truths in Genesis 1

1. God existed before creation.

Everything begins with Him.

2. God created everything that exists.

Creation depends upon God; God does not depend upon creation.

3. God's Word is powerful and authoritative.

God speaks, and creation responds.

4. Creation reflects order and purpose.

God forms, separates, fills, names, blesses, and evaluates His creation.

5. Humanity is uniquely created in God's image.

Human beings possess dignity, responsibility, and a unique calling within creation.

6. Male and female share the dignity of God's image.

Both are created by God and both receive His blessing and calling.

7. God's creation was originally very good.

Sin and death are not introduced until later in the Genesis story.

How Genesis 1 Points Toward Jesus Christ

Genesis 1 introduces truths that become clearer as the biblical story unfolds.

God creates through His word, and the New Testament identifies Jesus Christ as the eternal Word through whom all things were made.

Humanity is created in God's image, while Jesus is later revealed as the perfect image of the invisible God.

The God who brings light into darkness also sends Jesus Christ as the Light of the world.

The Creator who gives physical life is also the One who provides eternal life through His Son.

Christ-Centered Truth: Genesis begins with creation, but the New Testament reveals that Jesus Christ is central both to God's work of creation and to His work of redemption.

Personal and Group Study Questions

  1. What does Genesis 1 teach us about God's authority?
  2. What repeated patterns do you notice throughout the chapter?
  3. What does the repeated statement that creation is good teach us about God?
  4. What does it mean for humanity to be created in God's image?
  5. How should the image of God affect the way we treat other people?
  6. What responsibilities does God give humanity within creation?
  7. How does Genesis 1 challenge the idea that human life has no greater purpose?
  8. How does the New Testament connect Jesus Christ with creation?

Putting Genesis 1 Into Practice

Read Genesis 1 once each day this week.

Each time you read it, identify one truth about God, one truth about humanity, and one truth about creation.

Then ask how that truth should affect the way you think, worship, treat others, and live.

Personal Question: If I truly believe that God is my Creator and that I am made in His image, what should change about the way I understand my identity, purpose, and daily life?
