John 3 — You Must Be Born Again

A closer study of the new birth, the Son of Man being lifted up, God's love for the world, the response of faith, and the supremacy of Jesus Christ.

Purpose of This Study

John 3 explains why religious knowledge and human effort are not enough to bring a person into God's kingdom. Jesus teaches that people need a new birth from God.

The chapter also reveals God's love in sending His Son, the necessity of believing in Christ, and the difference between coming to the Light and remaining in darkness.

Big Idea: Eternal life is God's gift to those who believe in Jesus Christ. We do not enter God's kingdom by religious effort; we need the new life that God gives through His Spirit.

Chapter Setting

John 2 ends by telling us that Jesus knew what was in mankind. John 3 immediately introduces Nicodemus, a respected Pharisee and teacher of Israel.

Nicodemus appears to have everything a religious person might value: knowledge, position, morality, and respect. Yet Jesus tells him that he still needs something only God can give: new spiritual life.

John 3:1–8 — You Must Be Born Again

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

Nicodemus comes to Jesus at night and acknowledges that Jesus has come from God because of the signs He performs.

Jesus immediately tells him that unless a person is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.

Nicodemus thinks in physical terms, but Jesus explains that He is speaking about spiritual birth.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

Jesus does not tell Nicodemus that he needs more religion. Nicodemus needs an entirely new beginning from God.

Physical birth produces physical life. Spiritual birth is the work of the Holy Spirit, giving a person new spiritual life.

Jesus' reference to water and Spirit likely recalls God's promise to cleanse His people, give them a new heart, and place His Spirit within them.

Application — How Should We Respond?

Church attendance, biblical knowledge, family background, morality, and religious activity cannot replace the new birth.

Every person must ask: Have I merely become religious, or have I received new life from God?

Supporting Scriptures: Ezekiel 36:25–27; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Titus 3:5
Truth to Remember: No amount of religion can replace the new life that only God can give through His Spirit.

John 3:9–15 — The Son of Man Must Be Lifted Up

Observation

Nicodemus struggles to understand Jesus' teaching. Jesus reminds him that as a teacher of Israel, he should recognize the biblical promise of spiritual renewal.

Jesus then speaks of the Son of Man and compares His coming work to Moses lifting up the serpent in the wilderness.

Interpretation

In Numbers 21, the Israelites were dying because of their sin. God provided a way of deliverance: those who looked in faith at the bronze serpent lived.

Jesus uses that event to point toward His own crucifixion. He would be lifted up so that those who believe in Him might receive eternal life.

The comparison emphasizes both humanity's desperate condition and God's gracious provision.

Application

Salvation requires more than recognizing that we have a problem. We must look in faith to God's provision — Jesus Christ.

Supporting Scriptures: Numbers 21:4–9; John 8:28; John 12:32–33; Isaiah 53:4–6
Gospel Truth: Just as the Israelites had to look toward God's provision for life, sinners must look in faith to Jesus Christ.

John 3:16–21 — God So Loved the World

Observation

God loved the world and gave His Son so that those who believe in Him would not perish but have eternal life.

The Son was sent not merely to announce condemnation, but to provide salvation.

Yet John also explains that people respond differently to the Light. Some come to the Light, while others remain in darkness because they do not want their deeds exposed.

Interpretation

God's love is demonstrated through action: He gave His Son.

Eternal life is connected to believing in Christ. Unbelief leaves a person under condemnation, not because God's provision is inadequate, but because the person rejects the One God has provided.

The contrast between light and darkness reveals that unbelief is not only an intellectual issue. The human heart may resist the Light because the Light exposes sin.

Application

God's love should never be separated from His truth. The same passage that announces God's love also calls people to believe in His Son and come into the Light.

We should ask whether we are allowing Christ's truth to expose and transform every area of our lives.

Truth to Remember: God's love is seen most clearly in giving His Son so that sinners may receive eternal life through faith in Him.

John 3:22–30 — He Must Increase

Observation

Jesus' ministry begins attracting more attention, and some of John's disciples become concerned.

John the Baptist refuses to compete with Jesus. He reminds them that his role was to prepare the way.

His response reaches its climax in the statement: “He must increase, but I must decrease.”

Interpretation

John understands that successful ministry is not measured by how much attention the messenger receives.

His joy is fulfilled when people turn toward Christ. He recognizes that Jesus is the Bridegroom, while John is simply the friend who rejoices in His arrival.

Application

Christian ministry should never become competition for attention, recognition, or influence.

Our goal should be that Christ becomes greater in people's understanding and affection, while we remain faithful servants.

Ministry Principle: Faithful ministry points people toward Christ, not toward the messenger.

John 3:31–36 — The Son Is Above All

Observation

The chapter closes by emphasizing the unique authority and heavenly origin of Jesus.

The Father loves the Son and has placed all things into His hands.

Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life, while rejecting the Son leaves a person under God's judgment.

Interpretation

Jesus is not merely one religious teacher among many. He comes from above and speaks with divine authority.

A person's response to Jesus therefore has eternal significance. Eternal life is not merely a future promise; it begins with believing in the Son.

Application

We must take Jesus seriously because of who He is. Receiving or rejecting Him is not a minor religious decision.

Trust the Son, submit to His authority, and build your life upon His words.

Truth to Remember: The Son is above all, and eternal life is found in Him.

Five Major Truths in John 3

1. We Need New Birth

Human religion and effort cannot produce spiritual life. The Holy Spirit must give new life.

2. Jesus Must Be Lifted Up

The cross is God's provision for sinners who cannot rescue themselves.

3. God Loves the World

God's love is demonstrated by giving His Son so that believers may receive eternal life.

4. Christ Must Increase

Christian ministry exists to make Christ known, not to glorify the messenger.

5. The Son Is Above All

Jesus possesses divine authority, and our response to Him has eternal consequences.

Personal and Group Application

  1. Am I trusting in Christ, or in religious activity and moral effort?
  2. What does being born again teach me about my dependence upon God?
  3. How does the bronze serpent in Numbers 21 help me understand Jesus being lifted up?
  4. What does John 3:16 teach me about both God's love and my need to believe?
  5. Is there any area of my life that I am keeping away from Christ's light?
  6. Does my service point attention toward Christ or toward myself?
  7. How should the authority of the Son affect the way I live today?

Gospel Connection

John 3 brings us directly to the heart of the Gospel.

Humanity needs more than improvement. We need new life.

We cannot rescue ourselves from sin, so God sent His Son. Jesus would be lifted up on the cross so that those who believe in Him might receive eternal life.

God's invitation is clear: come to the Light, believe in the Son, and receive the life only He can give.

The Gospel does not call us merely to become more religious. It calls us to receive new life through faith in Jesus Christ.
