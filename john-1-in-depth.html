John 1 — The Word Became Flesh

A closer study of the eternal Word, the true Light, the witness of John the Baptist, the Lamb of God, and the first disciples who began following Jesus Christ.

Purpose of This Study

John 1 introduces us to the identity and mission of Jesus Christ. Before John tells us what Jesus did, he first helps us understand who Jesus is.

The chapter presents Jesus as the eternal Word, Creator, Life, Light, Son of God, Lamb of God, Messiah, King of Israel, and Son of Man.

Big Idea: Jesus Christ is the eternal Son who entered His creation, revealed the Father, takes away sin, and calls people to follow Him.

Chapter Setting

John begins his Gospel before Bethlehem and before the birth of Jesus. He reaches back beyond human history to the beginning itself.

His purpose is to show that Jesus did not begin to exist when He was born. The One who became flesh already existed before creation and participated in God's work of creation.

John 1:1–3 — The Eternal Word and Creation

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

John says that the Word already existed “in the beginning.” The Word was with God, the Word was God, and everything that came into existence came into existence through Him.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

John's opening intentionally recalls Genesis 1:1. When creation began, the Word was already there.

The Word is personally distinct from the Father because He is described as being “with God,” yet John also identifies the Word as truly divine.

Verse 3 places the Word on the Creator side of the distinction between Creator and creation. Everything that was made came into existence through Him.

Application — How Should We Respond?

Jesus must not be reduced merely to a teacher, prophet, moral example, or created messenger. John introduces Him as the eternal divine Word.

Truth to Remember: Before creation existed, the Word already existed. Jesus Christ belongs to the identity of the Creator, not to the category of created things.
Supporting Scriptures: Genesis 1:1; John 17:5; Colossians 1:16–17; Hebrews 1:2–3

John 1:4–5 — Life and Light

Observation

John connects life with the Word and describes that life as the light of humanity. The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness does not overcome it.

Interpretation

Jesus is not merely someone who possesses life. Life has its source in Him.

Light in John's Gospel points toward truth, holiness, revelation, and the life of God breaking into the darkness of sin and unbelief.

Darkness opposes the light, but it cannot finally defeat it.

Application

We should bring every part of life into the light of Christ. His truth exposes darkness, but it also leads us toward life.

Teaching Truth: Jesus is the source of spiritual life and the Light that darkness cannot overcome.

John 1:6–8 — John the Baptist Bears Witness

Observation

John the Baptist is described as a man sent from God. His role is not to be the Light but to bear witness concerning the Light.

Interpretation

John understood that his ministry was not about drawing attention to himself. His responsibility was to point others toward Jesus.

A faithful witness does not become the center of the message. The witness directs people toward Christ.

Application

Christian ministry should follow the same pattern. Our teaching, testimony, service, and preaching should help people see Jesus more clearly rather than make ourselves the center of attention.

Ministry Principle: We are witnesses to the Light; we are not the Light.

John 1:9–13 — The True Light and Receiving Christ

Observation

The true Light comes into the world He created, yet the world does not recognize Him.

He comes to His own people, but many reject Him. Others receive Him and believe in His name.

Interpretation

John's Gospel introduces two responses to Jesus: rejection and reception.

Those who receive Christ are given the right to become children of God. This new relationship with God is not produced by ancestry, human determination, or natural birth. It comes from God.

Application

Knowing facts about Jesus is not the same as receiving Him. The Gospel calls for personal faith and trust in Christ.

Truth to Remember: Becoming a child of God is not inherited through family, nationality, or religion. It comes through receiving Jesus Christ by faith.
Supporting Scriptures: John 3:3–8; Romans 8:14–17; Galatians 3:26

John 1:14–18 — The Word Became Flesh

Observation

The eternal Word becomes flesh and lives among humanity. John says that His glory was seen and describes Him as full of grace and truth.

Interpretation

The One who existed before creation truly entered human history. Jesus did not merely appear to be human. He became flesh.

In Jesus, God's character is made known in a unique and personal way. Grace and truth are perfectly revealed in Him.

Verse 18 explains that the Son makes the Father known. If we want to understand what God is like, we must look carefully at Jesus as He is revealed in Scripture.

Application

Study the words, character, holiness, compassion, authority, sacrifice, and obedience of Jesus. Through Him we learn the character of the Father.

Christ-Centered Truth: The eternal Word entered our world so that God might be revealed and sinners might receive grace.
Supporting Scriptures: John 14:6–9; Colossians 1:15; Hebrews 1:1–3; Philippians 2:6–8

John 1:19–28 — John the Baptist's Testimony

Observation

Religious leaders question John about his identity. They ask whether he is the Christ, Elijah, or the Prophet.

John refuses titles that do not belong to him. He describes himself as a voice preparing the way of the Lord.

Interpretation

John understands both his calling and his limitations. He knows who he is, but equally important, he knows who he is not.

His baptism prepares people for the One who is already among them, One so great that John considers himself unworthy even to perform the work of a servant for Him.

Application

Spiritual maturity includes knowing our role. We should faithfully serve where God has placed us without seeking an identity or position that belongs to Christ.

Ministry Truth: Faithful servants prepare the way for people to encounter Christ.

John 1:29–34 — The Lamb of God

Observation

John sees Jesus approaching and identifies Him as “the Lamb of God” who takes away the sin of the world.

John also testifies that the Spirit remained upon Jesus and identifies Him as the Son of God.

Interpretation

The title “Lamb of God” directs attention toward sacrifice. The problem Jesus came to address is not merely suffering, political oppression, or lack of knowledge. Humanity's deepest problem is sin.

John's testimony begins revealing that Jesus' mission will involve giving Himself for sinners.

Application

We must recognize the seriousness of sin and the sufficiency of Christ. Forgiveness cannot be earned through moral effort. Our hope rests in God's provision through Jesus.

Gospel Truth: Jesus is God's provision for the problem of human sin.
Supporting Scriptures: Exodus 12:1–13; Isaiah 53:4–7; 1 Corinthians 5:7; 1 Peter 1:18–19

John 1:35–42 — The First Disciples Follow Jesus

Observation

John again identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God, and two of John's disciples begin following Jesus.

Andrew later finds his brother Simon and tells him that they have found the Messiah. He then brings Simon to Jesus.

Interpretation

Discipleship begins with responding to Jesus' invitation. Those who encounter Christ naturally begin telling others about Him.

Andrew does not attempt to answer every possible question. He simply brings his brother to Jesus.

Application

One of the simplest ways to share our faith is to help another person encounter Jesus through Scripture.

Discipleship Principle: People who begin following Jesus should also begin helping others come to Him.

John 1:43–51 — Philip and Nathanael

Observation

Jesus calls Philip to follow Him. Philip then finds Nathanael and tells him that they have found the One written about by Moses and the prophets.

Nathanael responds skeptically, but Philip simply tells him to come and see.

When Nathanael encounters Jesus, he discovers that Jesus already knows him in a way that surprises him.

Interpretation

Jesus knows Nathanael before Nathanael truly knows Jesus. The encounter moves Nathanael from skepticism toward confession.

Jesus then identifies Himself as the Son of Man and promises that the disciples will see greater things.

Application

Honest questions do not have to prevent someone from coming to Christ. Philip does not argue Nathanael into faith. He invites him to encounter Jesus personally.

Witnessing Principle: When someone is skeptical about Jesus, sometimes the most important invitation is simply: “Come and see.”

Major Titles of Jesus in John 1

The Word

Jesus is God's eternal self-revelation and existed before creation.

The True Light

Jesus reveals truth and brings spiritual life into darkness.

The Only Son

Jesus uniquely reveals the Father.

The Lamb of God

Jesus is God's provision for human sin.

The Messiah

Jesus is the promised Christ anticipated in Scripture.

The Son of God

Jesus possesses a unique relationship with the Father and divine authority.

The King of Israel

Jesus is God's promised King.

The Son of Man

Jesus is the promised ruler who brings heaven and earth together under God's authority.

What John 1 Reveals About Jesus

  • He existed before creation.
  • He is truly divine and personally distinct from the Father.
  • Everything created came into existence through Him.
  • He is the source of life and the true Light.
  • He entered human history and became flesh.
  • He perfectly reveals the Father.
  • He is the Lamb of God who deals with human sin.
  • He is the Messiah, Son of God, King, and Son of Man.
  • He calls ordinary people to become His disciples.

Personal and Group Application

  1. Is my understanding of Jesus shaped primarily by Scripture or by ideas I have heard from others?
  2. What does John 1 teach me about Jesus before His human birth?
  3. Where do I need Christ's light to expose darkness in my life?
  4. Have I merely learned about Jesus, or have I personally received Him and trusted Him?
  5. How should the example of John the Baptist affect the way I serve or teach others?
  6. What does the title “Lamb of God” teach me about my need for salvation?
  7. Who could I invite to “come and see” Jesus through Scripture?

Gospel Connection

John 1 begins with the eternal Word and ends with people beginning to follow Him.

The Creator entered His creation. The Light entered darkness. The Son revealed the Father. The Lamb of God came to deal with human sin.

The proper response is not simply admiration or religious interest. John calls us to receive Christ, believe in Him, and follow Him.

John 1 moves us from knowing who Jesus is to responding personally to Him.

HBM In-Depth Study Principle

Careful Bible study should help us see what the text says, understand what it means, recognize what it reveals about God, and respond faithfully to Jesus Christ.
