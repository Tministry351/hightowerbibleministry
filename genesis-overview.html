Pentateuch • Bible Book Overview

Genesis

The book of beginnings — creation, humanity, sin, judgment, covenant, promise, and God's unfolding plan of redemption.

Genesis at a Glance

Genesis is the first book of the Bible and lays the foundation for understanding the rest of Scripture. It introduces God as Creator, explains the entrance of sin into the world, and begins the story of God's covenant promises through which He would bring blessing to the nations.

Main Truth: Genesis reveals the sovereign Creator who judges sin, extends grace, makes covenant promises, and faithfully works through people to accomplish His purposes.

Book

Genesis

The name means “beginning” or “origins.”

Author

Traditionally attributed to Moses as part of the first five books of Scripture.

Chapters

50 chapters

Genesis moves from creation to the family of Israel in Egypt.

Purpose of Genesis

Genesis explains the beginnings of the world, humanity, marriage, sin, death, nations, and God's covenant relationship with His people. It shows why humanity needs redemption and begins to reveal how God intends to provide that redemption.

The book also explains the beginnings of Israel. Through Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph, God begins forming the family through whom His covenant promises will continue.

Major Structure of Genesis

Genesis 1–11 — The Beginnings of Humanity

  • Creation — Genesis 1–2
  • The Fall — Genesis 3
  • Cain and Abel — Genesis 4
  • The Flood and Noah — Genesis 6–9
  • The Nations and Babel — Genesis 10–11

Genesis 12–50 — The Beginnings of God's Covenant People

  • Abraham — Genesis 12–25
  • Isaac — Genesis 25–26
  • Jacob — Genesis 27–36
  • Joseph — Genesis 37–50

Major Themes

God the Creator

Genesis begins with God. He creates the heavens and the earth, gives life, establishes order, and declares His creation good.

Humanity Made in God's Image

Human beings are uniquely created in the image of God and are given responsibility within His creation.

Sin and Its Consequences

Genesis 3 records humanity's rebellion against God. Sin brings guilt, broken relationships, suffering, and death.

Judgment and Grace

Throughout Genesis, God judges evil while also showing mercy. This pattern appears in the Fall, the Flood, Babel, and throughout the lives of the patriarchs.

Covenant and Promise

God makes promises to Noah and later calls Abraham, promising him land, descendants, and blessing. Through Abraham's family, God promises that blessing will ultimately extend to the nations.

God's Providence

The story of Joseph shows God working through difficult and even sinful human circumstances to accomplish His good purposes.

Key People in Genesis

Adam and Eve — the first man and woman, created in God's image.

Noah — preserved by God through the Flood and recipient of God's covenant.

Abraham and Sarah — called by God and given covenant promises concerning descendants, land, and blessing.

Isaac and Rebekah — the covenant promise continues through their family.

Jacob — later named Israel; father of the twelve sons who became the foundation of the tribes of Israel.

Joseph — used by God to preserve his family during famine and bring them into Egypt.

Important Promises and Covenants

Genesis 3:15 — The First Promise of Victory

After humanity falls into sin, God announces that the offspring of the woman will ultimately defeat the serpent. This begins a biblical expectation of deliverance from the power of evil.

Genesis 9 — God's Covenant With Noah

God promises that He will never again destroy all life by a flood and gives the rainbow as the sign of His covenant.

Genesis 12, 15, and 17 — God's Covenant With Abraham

God promises Abraham descendants, land, and blessing. Through Abraham, all the families of the earth are to be blessed.

How Genesis Points Toward Jesus Christ

Genesis begins themes that continue throughout Scripture and find their fulfillment in Jesus Christ.

The promised offspring of the woman introduces the hope of victory over the serpent and the power of sin.

God's promise that the nations would be blessed through Abraham becomes part of the biblical story leading to Christ.

The New Testament identifies Jesus Christ as the One through whom God's promised blessing reaches people from every nation.

Christ-Centered Truth: Genesis begins the story of redemption. What humanity lost through sin, God begins restoring through His promises — promises that ultimately lead us to Jesus Christ.

Why Genesis Matters

Genesis answers foundational questions about God, humanity, sin, relationships, judgment, grace, faith, and God's purposes in history.

Without Genesis, many of the major themes of Scripture would be difficult to understand. Genesis gives us the beginning of the story that unfolds throughout the rest of the Bible.

Key Scriptures to Remember

Genesis 1:1 — God is the Creator.

Genesis 1:27 — Humanity is created in God's image.

Genesis 3:15 — God promises victory over the serpent.

Genesis 12:1–3 — God calls Abraham and promises blessing.

Genesis 15:6 — Abraham believes God.

Genesis 50:20 — God works through human evil to accomplish His good purposes.
