Bible Book Overview • New Testament

The Gospel According to Matthew

Jesus the Messiah, King, and Savior

Matthew presents Jesus Christ as the promised Messiah and King, the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecy, the One who announces the Kingdom of Heaven, gives His life for sinners, rises from the dead, and commands His followers to make disciples of all nations.

Book Overview

The Gospel According to Matthew introduces Jesus as the long-awaited Messiah promised throughout the Old Testament. Matthew repeatedly connects the life, ministry, teaching, death, and resurrection of Jesus with God's earlier promises to Israel.

Jesus is presented not merely as a teacher or miracle worker, but as the rightful King. He teaches with authority, demonstrates authority over sickness, nature, demons, sin, and death, and calls people to repent and follow Him.

Matthew also emphasizes discipleship. Those who follow Jesus are called to live under His authority, seek first God's kingdom, pursue righteousness from the heart, forgive others, serve humbly, endure faithfully, and proclaim the Gospel.

Central Teaching Truth:

Matthew presents Jesus Christ as the promised Messiah and King who brings God's kingdom, calls people to repent and follow Him, gives His life for sinners, rises from the dead, and sends His disciples to make disciples of all nations.

Author and Audience

Matthew

The Gospel has traditionally been associated with Matthew, also called Levi, who was a tax collector before Jesus called him to become one of His disciples.

Matthew's background makes his calling especially significant. Tax collectors were often despised in Jewish society, yet Jesus called Matthew to leave his former life and follow Him.

Primary Audience

Matthew appears especially concerned with helping Jewish readers understand that Jesus is the promised Messiah. This is seen in his frequent references to Old Testament prophecy, Jewish customs, the Law, the prophets, and the fulfillment of God's covenant promises.

At the same time, Matthew's message extends beyond Israel. The Gospel concludes with Jesus commanding His followers to make disciples of all nations.

Purpose of Matthew

Matthew seeks to show that Jesus is the promised Messiah, the Son of David, the Son of Abraham, and the rightful King.

The Gospel demonstrates that Jesus fulfills God's promises, teaches the true meaning of righteousness, announces the Kingdom of Heaven, confronts false religion, gives His life as a ransom, rises from the dead, and possesses all authority in heaven and on earth.

Key Emphasis:

Matthew continually points readers toward one central question: Who is Jesus, and how should we respond to Him?

Fulfillment — Jesus Is the Promised Messiah

One of Matthew's strongest themes is fulfillment. Again and again, Matthew connects events in Jesus' life with what God had previously spoken through the prophets.

Matthew wants his readers to understand that the coming of Jesus was not an unexpected interruption in God's plan. Jesus stands at the center of God's redemptive purpose.

Teaching Truth:

The Old Testament points forward to God's saving work, and Matthew presents Jesus as the One in whom those promises reach their fulfillment.

Major Themes

1. Jesus Is the Messiah

Matthew identifies Jesus as the promised Christ, descended from Abraham and David, and repeatedly demonstrates that His life fulfills God's promises.

2. Jesus Is King

From the visit of the Magi to the Great Commission, Matthew emphasizes the kingship and authority of Jesus.

3. The Kingdom of Heaven

Jesus announces that the Kingdom of Heaven has drawn near. The kingdom refers to God's rule and authority, and Jesus teaches what life looks like for those who submit to that rule.

4. True Righteousness Begins in the Heart

Jesus confronts outward religion that lacks inward transformation. In the Sermon on the Mount, He teaches that obedience involves the motives, desires, attitudes, and intentions of the heart.

5. Discipleship Requires Commitment

Following Jesus involves more than admiration. His disciples are called to deny themselves, take up their cross, obey His teaching, serve others, forgive, endure, and remain faithful.

6. Religious Hypocrisy Is Dangerous

Matthew records some of Jesus' strongest warnings against religious leaders who appeared righteous outwardly while their hearts remained far from God.

7. The Cross and Resurrection Are Central

Jesus repeatedly prepares His disciples for His death. His crucifixion is followed by His bodily resurrection, demonstrating His victory and authority.

8. The Gospel Is for All Nations

Matthew begins with strong connections to Israel, but concludes with a worldwide mission. Jesus commands His followers to make disciples of all nations.

What Matthew Teaches Us About Jesus

What Matthew Reveals What It Teaches Us
Jesus fulfills Old Testament prophecy. He is the promised Messiah.
Jesus teaches with authority. His Word demands obedience.
Jesus forgives sins. He possesses divine authority.
Jesus commands nature, demons, sickness, and death. His authority extends over creation and the spiritual realm.
Jesus receives worship. He is more than merely a human teacher.
Jesus gives His life as a ransom. His death is central to God's saving purpose.
Jesus rises from the dead. Death does not defeat the King.
Jesus possesses all authority in heaven and earth. Every disciple lives under His lordship.

Practical Application

Matthew does not merely ask us to learn facts about Jesus. It calls us to respond to Him.

If Jesus is truly the Messiah and King, then His words cannot be treated as optional suggestions. His followers are called to hear Him, trust Him, obey Him, and represent Him faithfully.

Living It Out:

We should examine whether our faith is merely outward or whether Christ is transforming our hearts, priorities, relationships, decisions, and daily obedience.

Matthew also reminds believers that discipleship includes mission. Christians are not only called to learn from Jesus, but to help others know Him and become His disciples.

HBM Study Rhythm

Read it • Examine it • Understand it • Believe it • Live it

As we study Matthew, we will pay close attention to what Jesus says, what Jesus does, how people respond to Him, and what each passage teaches us about faithful discipleship.

The goal is not merely to finish the Gospel of Matthew. The goal is to know Christ more clearly, trust Him more deeply, and live under His authority more faithfully.
