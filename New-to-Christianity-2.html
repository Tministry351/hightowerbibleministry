Lesson 2: Who Is Jesus Christ?

Foundational Scripture: John 1:1-14

John 1:14 (KJV)

"And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth."

Welcome! Let's Learn About Jesus Christ

Perhaps you have heard the name Jesus Christ throughout your life. You may know something about His birth, His miracles, His death on the cross, or His resurrection.

But have you ever stopped to consider who Jesus Christ really is?

Some people think of Jesus as a good teacher or prophet. The Bible reveals that He is much more.

Christianity begins with understanding who Jesus Christ is and why He came into the world.

1. Who Does the Bible Say Jesus Is?

The Bible teaches that Jesus Christ is the eternal Son of God who came into the world as a human being.

John 1:1 tells us that the Word was with God and that the Word was God. John 1:14 explains that the Word became flesh and lived among us.

This means Jesus was more than a good teacher or prophet. He came to reveal God to humanity and make a way for us to be saved.

Jesus experienced human life, including hunger, sorrow, and suffering, yet He lived without sin (Hebrews 4:15).

As we continue studying God's Word, we will learn more about His divine nature and why His identity is important to our salvation.

Remember: Jesus Christ came to reveal God to us and bring us the hope of salvation.

2. Why Did Jesus Come into the World?

Jesus explained His mission in Luke 19:10:

"For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost."

The Bible teaches that sin has separated humanity from a right relationship with God (Romans 3:23).

Yet God did not leave us without hope. John 3:16 teaches that God loved the world and gave His only begotten Son so that those who believe in Him may have everlasting life.

Jesus came because God loves humanity and desires to save those who are lost.

We cannot save ourselves through good behavior or religious activities. Salvation is God's gift through Jesus Christ (Ephesians 2:8-9).

3. What Did Jesus Accomplish Through His Death and Resurrection?

Jesus willingly gave His life for our sins.

Romans 5:8 tells us that Christ died for us while we were still sinners.

His death was not the end.

According to 1 Corinthians 15:3-4, Christ died for our sins, was buried, and rose again on the third day.

His resurrection confirms the hope of eternal life for those who place their faith in Him.

Jesus Christ is not merely a historical figure who lived and died. He is the risen Savior.

4. What Does Knowing Jesus Mean for My Life?

Knowing Jesus involves more than knowing facts about Him.

It means recognizing who He is, trusting Him as Savior, and learning to follow Him as Lord.

In John 14:6, Jesus declared:

"I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me."

Through faith in Christ, we can receive forgiveness and begin a new relationship with God.

As we grow through Scripture, prayer, and obedience, the Holy Spirit works within us to transform our lives.

Following Christ does not mean life will be free from difficulties. It means we can learn to trust Him through those difficulties.

5. Something to Think About

  1. Who have I understood Jesus Christ to be before reading this lesson?
  2. Why does the Bible say Jesus came into the world?
  3. What does His death and resurrection mean for me?
  4. Would I like to learn more about having a personal relationship with Jesus Christ?

You do not need to answer every question immediately. Take time to read the Scriptures and consider what they teach.
