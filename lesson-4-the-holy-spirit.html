Lesson 2 — The Nature and Attributes of God

Part 4 — God’s Omnipotence

God is almighty, able to accomplish His holy will. His power gives us confidence to trust, worship and obey Him.

Download / Print PDF

Lesson Foundation

Lesson question: What does it mean that God is almighty, and how should His power shape our faith?

Central truth: God possesses unlimited power to accomplish His holy will. His power never contradicts His character, and His people can trust Him even when circumstances appear impossible.

Primary passages: Jeremiah 32:17, 26–27; Job 42:1–6. Supporting Scriptures: Genesis 1:1–3; Isaiah 40:25–31; Matthew 19:16–26; Ephesians 1:19–23; 3:20–21.

Why This Lesson Matters

Knowing God involves more than acknowledging His existence. In Parts 1–3 we considered the value of knowing Him, His eternal nature and His holiness. Now we consider His power. God is not dependent upon human strength, resources or permission to accomplish His purposes.

Understanding His power helps us pray with confidence without assuming that our desires determine His will.

Jeremiah 32:17, 26–27 — Nothing Is Too Hard for God

Jeremiah prayed while Jerusalem was under siege. God had instructed him to purchase a field, a sign of future restoration despite the approaching judgment (Jeremiah 32:6–15). The circumstances appeared to contradict the promise.

Jeremiah acknowledged that the Lord made heaven and earth by His great power. God answered by asking whether anything was too hard for Him. The same God who could bring judgment could also restore His people.

Key Biblical Truth: God’s ability to fulfill His promises does not depend upon favorable circumstances.

What Omnipotence Means

Omnipotence means that God is almighty. Job 42:2 affirms that no purpose of God can be thwarted. Psalm 115:3 declares that God does what He pleases. His power is not borrowed, acquired or exhausted.

This does not mean that God can contradict His own nature. Titus 1:2 teaches that God cannot lie, and 2 Timothy 2:13 teaches that He cannot deny Himself. His inability to act wickedly is not weakness; it reflects His perfect character.

God’s sovereignty describes His supreme authority to rule; omnipotence describes His unlimited power to accomplish His will. These truths work together.

God’s Power in Creation and Preservation

Genesis 1:1–3 introduces God as Creator. Psalm 33:6–9 describes creation coming into existence at His command. Unlike human makers, God is not dependent upon preexisting materials or assistance (Hebrews 11:3).

God also sustains what He has created. Colossians 1:16–17 teaches that all things were created through Christ and hold together in Him. Hebrews 1:3 describes the Son upholding all things by His powerful word.

Isaiah 40:28–31 connects the Creator’s inexhaustible strength with His care for weary people. Our weakness does not diminish His ability to sustain us.

God’s Power to Deliver — Exodus 14 and Daniel 3

At the Red Sea, Israel faced a situation it could not resolve by its own strength. God opened a way of escape (Exodus 14:13–31). His deliverance displayed His authority and faithfulness.

In Daniel 3:16–18, the three Hebrews confessed that God was able to deliver them from the furnace. Yet they refused to worship the image even if He did not deliver them. Their faith rested in God Himself, not in a guaranteed outcome.

Key Biblical Truth: Faith recognizes what God is able to do while submitting to what He chooses to do.

Matthew 19:16–26 — The Power of God to Save

Jesus’ conversation with the rich young ruler revealed the danger of trusting in possessions. The disciples asked who then could be saved. Jesus answered that what is impossible with men is possible with God.

In context, this passage concerns salvation, not a promise that every personal ambition will succeed. Human effort cannot earn salvation. God provides salvation through His grace in Jesus Christ (Ephesians 2:8–10).

The Resurrection and the Power of Christ

Ephesians 1:19–23 directs believers to the greatness of God’s power demonstrated in raising Jesus from the dead and seating Him above every authority. The resurrection reveals that death itself does not have the final word.

Jesus exercised authority over disease, nature and death during His earthly ministry (Mark 4:35–41; John 11:38–44). John 1:1–3 and Colossians 1:16–17 connect His identity with the divine work of creation.

Romans 8:11 teaches that the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus dwells in believers. Christian hope rests in God’s power, not our ability to overcome death.

God’s Power and Our Weakness

In 2 Corinthians 12:7–10, Paul asked repeatedly for his thorn to be removed. God’s answer emphasized sufficient grace and power made perfect in weakness. Paul did not receive the particular relief he requested, yet God was at work.

God may deliver us from a difficulty or sustain us through it. Neither the presence of suffering nor a delayed answer proves that God lacks power. We should pray, seek appropriate help and continue faithfully in the responsibilities He has given us.

Common Misunderstandings

“God can do anything, so He must do what I ask.” Scripture encourages confident prayer according to His will (1 John 5:14–15). Faith does not give us control over God.

“If I suffer, my faith must be inadequate.” Paul’s experience and the testimony of Hebrews 11:35–40 show that faithful people may endure hardship.

“God’s power makes human obedience unnecessary.” Philippians 2:12–13 connects our obedience with God’s work within us. His power is a reason to obey, not an excuse for passivity.

Living Out the Biblical Truth

Bring a difficult situation before God in prayer. Acknowledge His ability to act, ask specifically for help, and submit the outcome to His wisdom.

Read Ephesians 3:14–21. Notice that Paul prays for inner strength, love and spiritual maturity, not merely changed circumstances. Ask God to strengthen your character as well as meet your needs.

Continue serving faithfully even when results are not immediately visible. God gives the increase (1 Corinthians 3:6–7). Give Him the glory rather than boasting in your own strength.

Questions for Deeper Understanding

1. What is the difference between God’s omnipotence and His sovereignty?

2. How does Jeremiah’s purchase of a field demonstrate trust in God’s power?

3. Why is God’s inability to lie not a limitation of His divine perfection?

4. What does Matthew 19:26 teach in its original context?

5. How do Daniel 3 and 2 Corinthians 12 help us understand unanswered prayer?

6. What does the resurrection reveal about God’s power?

7. How should your understanding of God’s power change your daily decisions and service?

Remember

God is almighty, but His power is never separated from His holiness, wisdom and love. We can trust His ability without demanding our preferred outcome. Faith rests in who God is, not merely in what we hope He will do.

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for revealing Yourself as the Almighty God. Help us trust Your power and submit to Your wisdom. Strengthen us through Your Holy Spirit when we are weak, and teach us to serve faithfully regardless of circumstances. May our lives glorify You through Jesus Christ. Amen.
