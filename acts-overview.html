Bible Book Overview

The Acts of the Apostles

The Risen Christ Continues His Work Through the Holy Spirit and His Witnesses

Acts records the continuation of Jesus Christ's work after His resurrection and ascension. Through the power of the Holy Spirit, the apostles and early believers carry the message of Christ from Jerusalem into Judea, Samaria, and outward toward the nations.

Introduction to Acts

The Book of Acts continues the story begun in the Gospel of Luke. Luke explains how the risen Jesus prepares His disciples, promises the Holy Spirit, ascends to heaven, and sends His followers into the world as witnesses.

Acts shows the birth and growth of the early church, the spread of the Gospel, the ministry of the Holy Spirit, the courage of the apostles, and the way God works through ordinary believers.

Central Theme:

Jesus Christ continues His work through Spirit-empowered witnesses who proclaim the Gospel to the world.

Author and Background

Acts was written by Luke, the same author who wrote the Gospel of Luke.

Luke addresses both writings to Theophilus and presents Acts as a continuation of the account of Jesus' life and ministry.

The book begins in Jerusalem and follows the movement of the Gospel outward through the Roman world.

Key Verse

Acts 1:8

Jesus tells His disciples that they will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon them and that they will be His witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.

This verse provides a basic outline for the movement of the entire book.

Major Themes in Acts

The Risen Christ

Acts begins with Jesus alive after His resurrection and continues to show that He remains active in directing His church.

The Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit empowers believers, guides the church, gives boldness, and advances the mission of the Gospel.

Witness

The followers of Jesus are repeatedly called to testify about His death, resurrection, and saving work.

The Growth of the Church

The Gospel moves across cultural, geographic, and social boundaries.

Prayer

Prayer repeatedly appears before important moments, decisions, and movements of God.

Opposition and Perseverance

The early believers face persecution, rejection, imprisonment, and suffering, yet the Gospel continues to advance.

Movement of the Book

Acts can be followed through the geographical pattern Jesus gives in Acts 1:8.

Jerusalem

The church begins in Jerusalem with the coming of the Holy Spirit and the preaching of the apostles.

Judea and Samaria

Persecution causes believers to scatter, and the Gospel begins moving beyond Jerusalem.

Toward the Nations

Through Peter, Paul, and many other believers, the Gospel reaches Gentiles and spreads throughout the Roman world.

What We Should Watch for While Studying Acts

As we study Acts, pay attention to how often prayer appears, how the Holy Spirit directs believers, how the apostles explain the Gospel, and how the church responds to opposition.

Also watch how God continually moves the Gospel beyond familiar boundaries and brings different groups of people into the growing church.

Study Question:

How does the mission of the early church challenge believers today to depend upon the Holy Spirit and faithfully make Christ known?
