The Authority of Scripture

Why Is the Bible Our Final Authority?

The Bible is God's inspired Word, given to reveal His truth, guide our beliefs, correct our conduct, and equip us to live lives that glorify Him.

Download / Print PDF

Lesson Foundation

Lesson Question: Why is the Bible our final authority?

Central Truth: The Bible is the inspired Word of God, completely trustworthy, fully authoritative, and sufficient to guide our faith and life.

Main Passage: 2 Timothy 3:14–17

Supporting Scriptures: 2 Peter 1:20–21; Psalm 19:7–11; Psalm 119:105; Isaiah 40:8; Matthew 4:4; John 17:17; Hebrews 4:12

Why This Lesson Matters

Every believer must decide what will shape his or her faith, beliefs, and daily living.

Some people rely mainly on human opinion, culture, tradition, feelings, or personal preference.

But the Christian life must be grounded in something stronger and more certain.

God has given us His written Word so that we may know truth, recognize error, grow in righteousness, and live in a way that honors Him.

Key Truth: If the Bible is truly God’s Word, then it must be received as our final authority in doctrine, conduct, and Christian living.

What Scripture Says About Itself

In 2 Timothy 3:14–17, Paul reminds Timothy to continue in the truths he has learned.

He points Timothy back to the “Holy Scriptures,” which are able to make a person wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus.

Paul then declares that all Scripture is given by inspiration of God.

This means Scripture is not merely human reflection about God. It is God’s Word given through human writers.

Paul also teaches that Scripture is profitable for:

  • teaching
  • reproof
  • correction
  • instruction in righteousness

The result is that the servant of God may be complete and equipped for every good work.

The Bible Is Inspired by God

2 Timothy 3:16 teaches that Scripture is “God-breathed.”

This does not mean that the writers were passive machines. God used real men, with real personalities and writing styles, to communicate His truth without error in what He intended to reveal.

2 Peter 1:20–21 adds that prophecy did not come by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit.

The Bible, therefore, has a divine source.

Doctrinal Truth: Because Scripture comes from God, it carries God’s truth, God’s authority, and God’s trustworthiness.

The Bible Is Our Final Authority

If God is the source of Scripture, then Scripture stands above every human opinion.

Jesus Himself appealed to Scripture as final authority. In Matthew 4:4, 7, and 10, He answered temptation by saying, “It is written.”

Jesus did not treat God’s Word as optional or secondary. He submitted to it fully and used it accurately.

John 17:17 says, “Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth.”

Because God’s Word is truth, it must govern what we believe about God, salvation, the church, sin, holiness, and Christian living.

The Bible Is Sufficient for Spiritual Life and Growth

Scripture not only tells us what is true; it also equips us to live faithfully.

According to 2 Timothy 3:16–17, God’s Word teaches us, exposes error, corrects what is wrong, and trains us in righteous living.

Psalm 19:7 says, “The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul.”

Psalm 119:105 says, “Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.”

The believer does not outgrow the need for Scripture. We grow by it, are corrected by it, and are strengthened through it.

Common Errors to Avoid

There are several mistakes people make when it comes to the authority of Scripture:

  • placing personal feelings above God’s Word
  • treating cultural opinion as more reliable than Scripture
  • using only favorite verses while ignoring the whole counsel of God
  • depending on religious tradition without testing it by Scripture
  • reading the Bible casually but not submitting to what it says

A faithful believer does not stand over Scripture as judge. He stands under Scripture as a servant and learner.

Practical Christian Living

If the Bible is our final authority, then we must do more than own it—we must read it, believe it, obey it, and live by it.

This means:

  • we test teaching by Scripture
  • we make decisions in light of Scripture
  • we allow Scripture to correct our thinking
  • we use Scripture in discipleship, ministry, and daily life
  • we teach others that God’s Word can be trusted completely
Practical Challenge: Ask yourself, “Am I allowing God’s Word to shape my life, or am I letting my feelings and surroundings shape me more than Scripture?”

Questions for Deeper Understanding

  1. Why does the divine origin of Scripture matter for Christian faith?
  2. What does 2 Timothy 3:16–17 teach about the usefulness of Scripture?
  3. How did Jesus show the authority of Scripture in His own life?
  4. What are some modern ways people place other authorities above the Bible?
  5. How can believers grow in submitting more fully to God’s Word?
  6. Why is Scripture necessary for sound doctrine and Christian maturity?

Remember

The Bible is not merely a helpful religious book.

It is the inspired Word of God.

It is true, trustworthy, and authoritative.

Because it comes from God, it must shape what we believe and how we live.

God’s people grow strong when they stand firmly on God’s Word.

Closing Prayer

Father, give us humble hearts to receive Your Word, wisdom to understand it rightly, courage to obey it, and love to share its truth with others.

Help us glorify You in both what we teach and how we live. In Jesus' name, amen.
