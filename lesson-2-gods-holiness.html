Lesson 2 — The Nature and Attributes of God

Part 3 — God’s Holiness

God is uniquely holy, perfectly pure and righteous in all His ways. He calls His people to reflect His character.

Download / Print PDF

Lesson Foundation

Lesson question: What does it mean that God is holy, and how should His holiness shape our lives?

Central truth: God is uniquely holy, perfectly pure and righteous in all His ways. Through Jesus Christ, He calls His people to belong to Him and to reflect His character in everyday life.

Primary passage: Isaiah 6:1–8. Supporting Scriptures: Exodus 3:1–6; 1 Samuel 2:2; Leviticus 19:2; 1 Peter 1:13–19; Hebrews 10:10–14; Revelation 4:8–11.

Why This Lesson Matters

In Part 2 we learned that God has always existed and that His character never changes. Now we consider the holiness of that character. God is not simply a greater version of ourselves. He is the Creator, unequaled in majesty and entirely free from sin.

A correct understanding of holiness changes how we worship, how we understand sin, and how we appreciate the grace of Jesus Christ. It also challenges us to examine whether our daily conduct reflects the God we profess to know.

Biblical Background

Isaiah 6 opens in the year King Uzziah died. An earthly throne had lost its king, but Isaiah saw the Lord seated upon His throne. The vision directs attention away from human power and toward the majesty of the eternal King.

Isaiah 6:1–4 — Holy, Holy, Holy

“Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory.” — Isaiah 6:3 (KJV)

The seraphim cover their faces and feet in the presence of the Lord. Even these heavenly servants display reverence. The threefold declaration intensifies the proclamation of God’s holiness; Revelation 4:8 echoes it in heavenly worship.

Holiness includes God’s unique majesty and His complete moral purity. He is distinct from creation, not because creation is necessarily evil, but because no created being shares His divine perfection. His power, love, justice and mercy are never corrupted by sin.

Key Biblical Truth: God’s holiness is not a temporary condition. It belongs to who He is.

Exodus 3:1–6 — Holy Ground

When God calls Moses from the burning bush, He commands him to remove his shoes because the ground is holy. The ground is not inherently divine; God’s presence sets the place apart. Moses hides his face in reverence.

God’s invitation to Moses does not eliminate the distinction between Creator and creature. Likewise, our access to God through Christ should produce gratitude and reverence, not careless familiarity. Hebrews 12:28–29 connects acceptable worship with reverence and awe.

1 Samuel 2:2 — None Holy Like the Lord

Hannah’s prayer declares that there is none holy like the LORD. God has no rival in holiness. First John 1:5 teaches that God is light and in Him is no darkness at all. James 1:13 adds that God cannot be tempted with evil and does not tempt anyone to evil.

Isaiah 6:5–7 — Conviction and Cleansing

“Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips...” — Isaiah 6:5 (KJV)

Isaiah’s encounter with God exposes his own uncleanness and that of his people. He does not compare himself with others to establish his righteousness. In the light of God’s holiness, he recognizes his need for cleansing.

A seraph touches Isaiah’s lips with a coal from the altar and announces that his iniquity is taken away. The cleansing is provided to Isaiah; it is not something he achieves through personal merit. This scene illustrates the need for divine cleansing, while the New Testament explains how forgiveness is secured through Christ.

Key Biblical Truth: The holiness of God exposes sin, but His grace provides cleansing.

Jesus Christ — Our Holy Savior

Hebrews 4:15 teaches that Jesus was tempted as we are, yet without sin. His sinlessness is essential to understanding His saving work. First Peter 1:18–19 describes His precious blood in terms of a lamb without blemish or spot.

At the cross, God does not overlook sin as though it were insignificant. Romans 3:25–26 connects Christ’s sacrifice with God’s righteousness and His justification of those who have faith in Jesus. Divine holiness and saving love are not opposing qualities.

Hebrews 10:10–14 teaches that believers are sanctified through Christ’s offering once for all. The finished work of Christ is the foundation of our acceptance before God, not our record of religious performance.

Isaiah 6:8 — Cleansed for Service

After cleansing comes the call to serve. Isaiah responds, “Here am I; send me.” Grace does not make obedience unnecessary; it prepares and motivates us for service. Ephesians 2:8–10 similarly connects salvation by grace with good works.

Leviticus 19:2 and 1 Peter 1:13–16 — Be Holy

“Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy.” — 1 Peter 1:16 (KJV)

Leviticus calls Israel to holiness because the LORD is holy. The chapter applies that calling to ordinary life: honesty, justice, care for the poor, and love of neighbor. Holiness is not confined to a worship gathering.

Peter applies the call to Christian believers. They are to reject former sinful desires and reflect God’s character in all their conduct. The reason is their relationship with the God who called them, not an attempt to earn salvation.

Key Biblical Truth: Biblical holiness is belonging to God and increasingly reflecting His character in conduct.

Sanctification — A Life of Spiritual Growth

Sanctification refers to being set apart for God and growing in holy living. First Corinthians 6:11 speaks of believers as washed, sanctified and justified. First Thessalonians 4:3–7 also calls them to ongoing purity and obedience.

The Holy Spirit enables this growth. Galatians 5:16–25 contrasts the works of the flesh with the fruit of the Spirit. Spiritual maturity is expressed through love, faithfulness, self-control and the other qualities the Spirit produces.

Believers still need confession and repentance (1 John 1:8–9). A struggle with temptation is not the same as surrendering to it. Our response should be to seek God’s help, turn away from sin and continue walking with Him.

Common Misunderstandings

Holiness is not merely outward appearance: Jesus warns against outward righteousness that conceals inward corruption (Matthew 23:25–28).

Holiness is not isolation from everyone who sins: Jesus calls His followers to live in the world without belonging to its sinful ways (John 17:15–18).

Holiness is not earning salvation: Ephesians 2:8–10 teaches that good works follow salvation by grace.

Holiness is not permission to look down on others: Galatians 6:1 calls believers to restore others gently while watching themselves.

Practical Christian Living

Worship God with reverence and gratitude (Hebrews 12:28). Examine your motives, words and conduct in light of His Word (Psalm 139:23–24). Confess sin rather than excusing it (1 John 1:9). Depend on the Holy Spirit to help you resist temptation and practice obedience (Galatians 5:16). Treat others with honesty, justice, mercy and love (Leviticus 19:11–18).

This week, read Isaiah 6:1–8 and 1 Peter 1:13–19. Identify one area where your conduct needs to reflect God’s character more faithfully. Pray, choose a specific act of obedience, and seek accountability from a mature believer if helpful.

Questions for Deeper Understanding

1. What does Isaiah’s vision teach about God’s uniqueness and majesty?

2. Why did Isaiah recognize his own uncleanness in God’s presence?

3. How does Christ’s sacrifice reveal both God’s holiness and His grace?

4. What is the difference between being set apart for God and earning salvation?

5. How does the Holy Spirit help believers grow in holiness?

6. How should holiness affect our relationships, speech and decisions?

Remember

Key Biblical Truth: God is holy in all His ways. His holiness exposes our need for grace, and His grace calls us into a life of obedience. Through Jesus Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit, we are called to honor Him in every part of our lives.

Closing Prayer

Holy Father, thank You for revealing Your character through Your Word and through Jesus Christ. Help us recognize sin, receive Your cleansing with gratitude, and walk in obedience through the power of Your Holy Spirit. Teach us to worship You with reverence and to reflect Your holiness in our thoughts, words and actions. In Jesus’ name, amen.
