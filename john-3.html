Verse-by-Verse Study

John 3

“You must be born again.” — John 3:7

John 3 explains the necessity of the new birth, reveals God’s love in sending His Son, and shows the difference between coming to the Light and remaining in darkness.

Chapter Overview

John 3 centers on Jesus’ conversation with Nicodemus and explains what it means to be born again. The chapter also contains one of Scripture’s clearest statements about God’s love and salvation, followed by John the Baptist’s final testimony that Jesus must increase while he must decrease.

John 3:1–8 — You Must Be Born Again

Observation Nicodemus, a Pharisee and ruler of the Jews, comes to Jesus at night. Jesus tells him that unless a person is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.
Interpretation Jesus is speaking about spiritual birth, not physical birth. Entrance into God's kingdom requires a work of the Holy Spirit that gives new life.
Application Religion, knowledge, position, and good works cannot replace spiritual rebirth. Every person must personally receive the new life that comes from God.

John 3:9–21 — God So Loved the World

Observation Jesus explains heavenly truths to Nicodemus and points to the Son of Man being lifted up. He then declares that God loved the world and gave His only Son so that those who believe in Him would not perish but have eternal life.
Interpretation Salvation is God's gracious provision through Jesus Christ. Christ came not merely to condemn the world, but to save. Those who believe come into the light, while unbelief keeps people in darkness.
Application God's love calls for a personal response of faith. We are invited to leave spiritual darkness, trust Christ, and live openly in the light of God's truth.

John 3:22–36 — He Must Increase

Observation John's disciples become concerned that more people are going to Jesus. John responds by reminding them that he is not the Christ and that his role is to point others to Him.
Interpretation John understands that Jesus must become greater while he becomes less. True ministry is not about personal recognition but about directing attention to Christ.
Application Christian service should make Jesus more visible, not ourselves. A faithful life says with John, “He must increase, but I must decrease.”

Key Truths

  • Every person must be born again spiritually.
  • The new birth is a work of the Holy Spirit.
  • God loved the world and gave His Son.
  • Salvation is received through faith in Jesus Christ.
  • Unbelief keeps people in spiritual darkness.
  • True ministry points people to Christ, not to ourselves.

Practical Application

John 3 calls us to examine whether we have truly been born again, whether we are trusting Christ personally, and whether our lives are bringing attention to Him rather than to ourselves.

Discussion Questions

  1. What did Jesus mean when He said a person must be born again?
  2. Why could Nicodemus not rely on his religious position?
  3. What does John 3:16 teach us about God's love?
  4. What is the difference between coming to the Light and remaining in darkness?
  5. Why did John the Baptist say, “He must increase, but I must decrease”?
  6. How can your life point more clearly to Jesus?
