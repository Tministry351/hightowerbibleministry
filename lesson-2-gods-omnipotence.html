Biblical Truths & Sound Doctrine · Lesson 2 · Part 4

The Omnipotence of God

God's Unlimited Power — Trusting His Promises, Depending on Christ, and Growing in Obedience

Primary Scriptures: Genesis 18:9–15 · Jeremiah 32:16–27

Lesson purpose

Knowing the All-Powerful God

Omnipotence means that God is all-powerful. He has the power to accomplish everything consistent with His holy nature and will. God's power is never defeated by human limitations or circumstances. Yet Scripture does not teach that God promises to grant everything we desire.

Central truth: Because God is all-powerful and faithful, we can trust His promises, obey His Word, and leave the timing and results in His hands.

Our study method: Observation → Interpretation → Meditation → Application.

1 · Observation

What Does Scripture Say?

Genesis 18:9–15 — Abraham and Sarah

The Lord announced that Sarah would bear a son. Sarah was beyond her childbearing years and laughed to herself at the prospect. God's question in verse 14 focuses on His ability, not her physical limitations:

“Is anything too hard for the LORD?” — Genesis 18:14 (ESV)

In Genesis 21:1–2, God fulfilled the promise at the appointed time. Abraham was 100 years old when Isaac was born (Genesis 21:5); Sarah was approximately 90.

Jeremiah 32:16–27 — A Promise Amid Crisis

Jeremiah prayed while Jerusalem faced Babylonian siege. God had directed him to purchase a field despite the approaching disaster, pointing ahead to restoration (Jeremiah 32:6–15, 36–44). Jeremiah affirmed that God created heaven and earth by His great power (verse 17). God answered in verse 27:

“Is anything too hard for me?” — Jeremiah 32:27 (ESV)

Both accounts confront human expectations with God's power to carry out what He has said.

2 · Interpretation

What Do These Passages Mean?

God's ability to fulfill His Word does not depend on what appears possible to us. Sarah's initial laughter did not nullify the promise; Hebrews 11:11 later connects her story with faith in the One who promised. Jeremiah's circumstances looked hopeless, yet God's plan extended beyond the immediate crisis.

Important distinction: Believing that God can do something recognizes His power. Trusting that God will do what He has promised rests on His faithfulness. Neither permits us to decide God's timing or treat our wishes as guaranteed promises.

God's omnipotence does not mean He acts against His nature. Scripture teaches that God does not lie (Titus 1:2). His power is always in harmony with His holiness, wisdom, truth, and purposes.

God's Power at Work Within Believers

Philippians 2:12–13 calls believers to live out their salvation with reverence because God works in them to shape both willing and doing. We do not earn salvation by our efforts; we respond to God's gracious work through obedient living.

Our Dependence on Jesus Christ

Jesus calls Himself the vine and His disciples the branches (John 15:1–8). Fruitfulness comes from remaining in Him, not from religious activity performed independently of Him. Prayer, Scripture study, and obedience are practices through which we seek and respond to Christ, not substitutes for His grace.

3 · Meditation

Chewing on God's Word

God is able.

When my circumstances appear impossible, do I remember the character and power of the One I worship?

God is faithful.

Am I relying on His actual promises, or demanding an outcome He has not promised?

God works in me.

Am I responding in faith and obedience, or using “waiting on God” as an excuse to avoid a clear responsibility?

Christ sustains me.

Am I remaining attentive to Christ through prayer, His Word, fellowship, and obedience?

Why Christian Fellowship Matters

Hebrews 10:24–25 urges believers to encourage one another toward love and good works, while Ephesians 4:11–16 describes how Christ equips His people through the Church. Learning from mature believers, welcoming correction, and encouraging one another are part of healthy spiritual growth.

An illustration: Water that remains stagnant can become unhealthy. Likewise, deliberate neglect of prayer, Scripture, fellowship, and obedience can foster spiritual complacency. This is an illustration, not an image used in those passages. A person unable to attend gatherings because of illness or hardship should not be confused with someone deliberately withdrawing from Christian fellowship.

4 · Application

How Should This Truth Change My Life?

What Scripture revealsMy faithful response
God has power to fulfill His promises.Read His promises in context; trust Him even while waiting.
God's purposes are greater than my circumstances.Pray honestly without requiring my preferred outcome.
God works within His people.Continue growing in obedience, not passivity (Philippians 2:12–13).
Christ is the source of spiritual fruitfulness.Abide in Him through faith, prayer, Scripture, and obedience (John 15).
Christ builds up His Church.Seek fellowship, sound teaching, mutual encouragement, and service.
A principle for faithful ministry: We faithfully study, obey, teach, and serve. We entrust the results and their timing to God. “God gave the growth” (1 Corinthians 3:6, ESV).

Trusting God's power is not inactivity. It is a continuing willingness to do what Scripture makes clear, while accepting that growth can take time and that visible results are not ours to control.

5 · Personal reflection and discussion

Questions for the Student

  1. Why did Sarah laugh at the promise in Genesis 18? How did God redirect her attention?
  2. What is the difference between trusting God's power and assuming He has promised an outcome we want?
  3. How do Philippians 2:12–13 and John 15:4–5 hold together God's work and our responsibility?
  4. How can fellowship and sound biblical teaching guard against spiritual complacency?
  5. What is one responsibility God has made clear in Scripture that I can obey this week, regardless of visible results?
6 · Weekly study and prayer

Continue in the Word

Reading assignment: Read Genesis 18:9–15, Genesis 21:1–7, Jeremiah 32:16–27, Philippians 2:12–13, John 15:1–8, and Hebrews 10:24–25 over the coming week. Write down what each passage actually promises and what response it calls for.

Memory verse: Genesis 18:14 — “Is anything too hard for the LORD?” (ESV)

Prayer: Almighty God, thank You that Your power is never limited by our weakness. Teach us to trust what You have promised, remain close to Jesus Christ, and obey Your Word while leaving the results in Your hands. Strengthen us through Your Spirit and encourage us through fellowship with Your people. In Jesus' name, amen.

Closing thought: God's unlimited power should lead us to trust Him, depend on Christ, remain obedient to His Word, and grow together with other believers.
