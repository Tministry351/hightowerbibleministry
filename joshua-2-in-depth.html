Joshua 2

Rahab and the Spies

Central Theme

God can use unexpected people who respond to Him in faith, and true faith becomes visible through courageous action.

Joshua 2:1 — Joshua Sends Two Spies

Commentary

Joshua sends two men secretly from Shittim to spy out the land, especially Jericho.

Jericho was the first major fortified city Israel would face after crossing the Jordan River.

Joshua had already received God's promise that the land would be given to Israel, but he still used wisdom and preparation.

Sending spies did not necessarily show unbelief. Joshua trusted God's promise, but he also gathered information before moving forward.

There is an important balance here.

Faith in God does not mean refusing to plan. Trusting God and preparing wisely can work together.

The two spies enter the house of Rahab, a woman identified as a harlot.

From the beginning of the chapter, God is already working through someone most people would not have expected.

Key Truth:

God can use unexpected people and unexpected circumstances to accomplish His purposes.

Application:

Believers should not confuse faith with carelessness.

Wise preparation can be part of faithful obedience.

Rahab also reminds us that a person's past does not prevent God from working in their life when they respond to Him in faith.

Joshua 2:2–7 — Rahab Hides the Spies

Commentary

News quickly reaches the king of Jericho that Israelite men have entered the city.

The king sends men to Rahab and commands her to bring out the spies.

Rahab had already hidden them.

She tells the king's men that the spies had been there, but claims they had already left.

She then sends the king's men in the wrong direction while the spies remain hidden on her roof beneath stalks of flax.

Rahab takes a tremendous risk.

If her actions had been discovered, she could have been accused of helping the enemy and placed her life in serious danger.

Her decision shows that she had already begun choosing the God of Israel over the security of her own city.

Rahab's Deception

Rahab's lie raises an important moral question.

The passage records what Rahab did, but it does not directly command believers to imitate deception.

What Scripture later commends about Rahab is her faith and the way she received and protected the messengers.

Her actions took place in a dangerous situation during war, but the central emphasis of the biblical story is her faith in God.

Key Truth:

Rahab's faith began to affect her decisions before she had seen Israel enter the land.

Application:

True faith eventually requires choices.

Sometimes trusting God means taking a stand that may cost us comfort, security, or acceptance.

Faith becomes visible when what we believe begins shaping what we do.

Joshua 2:8–11 — Rahab Confesses What She Knows About God

Commentary

Before the spies go to sleep, Rahab comes to them on the roof.

She tells them that she knows the LORD has given Israel the land.

This is remarkable.

Israel had not yet crossed the Jordan. Jericho had not yet fallen.

But Rahab already believed that God's promise would be fulfilled.

She explains that the people of Jericho had heard what God had done.

They knew about the parting of the Red Sea.

They also knew about Israel's victories over Sihon and Og.

The people were terrified because of what they had heard.

Rahab then makes one of the strongest statements in the chapter: the LORD, Israel's God, is God in heaven above and on earth beneath.

Rahab was living among people who worshiped other gods, yet she recognized that the God of Israel was the true God.

Key Truth:

Rahab's faith was based on what she had heard about God's power and faithfulness.

Application:

Faith often begins by hearing what God has done.

Believers should never underestimate the power of testimony.

When we tell others what God has done, we may be planting seeds of faith in people whose hearts God is already preparing.

Joshua 2:12–14 — Rahab Asks for Mercy

Commentary

Rahab now asks the spies to show kindness to her and her family.

She had shown kindness to them by protecting their lives.

Now she asks for assurance that when Israel enters Jericho, her family will be spared.

Rahab is not only concerned about herself.

She asks for protection for her father, mother, brothers, sisters, and everyone belonging to them.

The spies agree.

They promise that if she does not reveal their mission, they will deal kindly and faithfully with her when God gives Israel the land.

Key Truth:

Rahab's faith moved beyond concern for herself and included concern for her family.

Application:

A person who begins trusting God often becomes concerned about the well-being of others.

Faith should not make us self-centered.

It should increase our concern for the people around us.

Joshua 2:15–21 — The Scarlet Cord and the Agreement

Commentary

Rahab's house was built into the city wall.

She lets the spies down through a window using a rope.

She tells them to escape toward the hills and remain hidden for three days until the king's men return.

The spies then establish specific conditions for their agreement.

Rahab must tie a scarlet cord in the window.

Her family must remain inside the house.

If anyone leaves the house, responsibility for that person's death would not fall on the spies.

Rahab agrees to these conditions and ties the scarlet cord in the window.

The Scarlet Cord

The scarlet cord becomes the visible sign identifying Rahab's house.

The text itself presents it as the agreed sign of protection.

Christians have often seen symbolic parallels between the scarlet cord and themes of redemption, including the blood of the Passover and ultimately the saving work of Christ.

Those comparisons can be spiritually meaningful, but we should be careful not to claim more than the passage itself directly states.

In Joshua 2, the scarlet cord primarily functions as a visible sign identifying the household that is to be spared.

Key Truth:

Rahab's faith was accompanied by obedience. She believed the promise and acted according to the instructions she was given.

Application:

Faith is not merely agreeing that something is true.

Faith responds.

When God gives clear instruction, genuine trust is demonstrated through obedience.

Joshua 2:22–24 — The Spies Return to Joshua

Commentary

The spies follow Rahab's advice and hide in the hills for three days.

After the king's men return to Jericho, the spies make their way back to Joshua.

They report everything that happened.

Their final statement is important.

They tell Joshua that the LORD has surely delivered the entire land into Israel's hands.

They also report that the inhabitants of the land are terrified.

This report is very different from the report given by ten of the twelve spies during Moses' time.

Earlier, fear caused most of the spies to focus on the strength of the enemy.

Here, these two spies recognize the fear of the enemy and the faithfulness of God.

Key Truth:

Faith sees circumstances through the promises and power of God rather than allowing circumstances to become greater than God in our thinking.

Application:

The way we interpret circumstances matters.

Two people can face the same difficulty and respond very differently.

Fear asks, “How can we possibly overcome this?”

Faith asks, “What has God already promised, and how should we respond?”

Major Lessons From Joshua 2

1. God Can Use Unexpected People

Rahab's background did not prevent God from working through her.

God saw a woman who responded to what she had heard about Him.

2. Faith Produces Action

Rahab did more than acknowledge God's power.

She acted on what she believed.

3. Testimony Matters

Rahab's faith began because she had heard what God had done.

4. Faith Can Require Courage

Rahab took a serious risk when she protected the spies.

Trusting God may sometimes require us to make difficult decisions.

5. Faith Should Affect How We See the Future

Rahab believed that God had already given Israel the land before Jericho ever fell.

6. God Honors Faith

Rahab's story does not end in Joshua 2.

She is later remembered in Scripture as a woman of faith.

Her story demonstrates that God can redeem a person's life and give them a new place in His purposes.

Final Application

Joshua 2 challenges us to ask:

What have I heard and learned about God?

Do I actually believe it?

If I believe it, how should that belief affect my decisions?

Is there an area where fear is preventing me from acting in faith?

Am I concerned only about myself, or am I also concerned about the people around me?

Rahab's story reminds us that faith is not defined by where a person started.

What matters is how that person responds when they recognize who God truly is.

Remember

God can reach people in unexpected places.

God can use people others might overlook.

Faith begins by recognizing who God is.

Faith grows through trust.

Genuine faith becomes visible through courageous obedience.
