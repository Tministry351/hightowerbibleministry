Verse-by-Verse Study

John 1

“In the beginning was the Word...” — John 1:1

The Word became flesh. This chapter introduces Jesus Christ as the eternal Word, the true Light, the Lamb of God, and the Son of God.

Chapter Overview

John 1 lays the foundation for the entire Gospel. It reveals who Jesus is, what He came to do, and how people are called to respond to Him. This chapter moves from eternity past to the calling of the first disciples.

John 1:1–5 — The Word and Creation

Observation John begins before creation itself. The Word already existed, was with God, and was God. Everything that was created came into existence through Him.
Interpretation Jesus did not begin His existence at Bethlehem. John presents Him as eternal and divine. He is distinct from the Father, yet fully shares the nature of God.
Application Our faith rests in more than a great teacher. Jesus is the eternal Lord through whom life itself exists. He deserves our trust, worship, and obedience.

John 1:6–13 — The Witness and the True Light

Observation John the Baptist was sent by God to testify about the Light. Jesus came into the world He created, yet many did not recognize or receive Him.
Interpretation Spiritual privilege, ancestry, or human effort cannot make a person a child of God. Those who receive Christ and believe in His name are given the right to become children of God.
Application Each person must personally respond to Jesus Christ. Knowing about Him is not the same as receiving Him.

John 1:14–18 — The Word Became Flesh

Observation The eternal Word became flesh and lived among humanity. John says the disciples saw His glory, full of grace and truth.
Interpretation Jesus truly became human without ceasing to be divine. In Christ, God made Himself known in a personal and visible way.
Application Because Jesus entered our world, Christianity is not merely a philosophy or set of rules. God came near to reveal Himself and bring grace and truth.

John 1:19–34 — John the Baptist Points to Jesus

Observation John refuses to draw attention to himself. He identifies himself as a voice preparing the way of the Lord and identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God.
Interpretation The title “Lamb of God” points to Jesus as the One who deals with humanity’s sin. John’s ministry was not about building his own reputation but directing people to Christ.
Application Christian ministry should point people toward Jesus rather than toward ourselves. Our message should make Christ greater, not the messenger.

John 1:35–51 — The First Disciples

Observation Several of Jesus’ first disciples begin following Him through personal testimony. Andrew brings Simon Peter, and Philip invites Nathanael to come and see.
Interpretation The Gospel spreads relationally. People encounter Christ, become convinced of who He is, and then invite others to meet Him.
Application You do not need to know everything before telling someone about Jesus. Like Philip, sometimes the most powerful invitation is simply: “Come and see.”

Key Truths

  • Jesus Christ existed before creation.
  • Jesus is fully divine.
  • Jesus is the Creator and source of life.
  • Jesus became truly human.
  • Jesus is the Lamb of God who deals with sin.
  • Receiving Christ brings a person into God’s family.

Practical Application

John 1 calls us to believe, follow, worship, and proclaim Jesus Christ. He is not presented as merely a religious teacher. He is the eternal Word who became flesh and made the Father known.

Discussion Questions

  1. What does John 1:1–3 teach about Jesus before creation?
  2. What does it mean that the Word became flesh?
  3. Why does John call Jesus the Lamb of God?
  4. What is the difference between knowing about Jesus and receiving Him?
  5. What can we learn from Andrew and Philip about sharing our faith?
