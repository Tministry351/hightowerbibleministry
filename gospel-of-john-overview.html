Gospel of John Overview — Jesus Christ, the Son of God

The Gospel of John presents Jesus Christ as the eternal Word, the Son of God, the Messiah, and the source of eternal life. John records selected signs, teachings, conversations, the death and resurrection of Jesus, so that readers may believe in Him and have life in His name.

John at a Glance

The Gospel of John is one of the four New Testament Gospels, but its presentation differs in important ways from Matthew, Mark, and Luke.

John gives particular attention to the identity of Jesus Christ, His relationship with the Father, His signs, His extended teachings, and the necessity of believing in Him for eternal life.

Rather than attempting to record everything Jesus said and did, John explains that he selected particular signs so that readers might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God.

Main Purpose — John 20:30–31: John tells us why he wrote his Gospel: so that people may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing they may have life in His name.

Book

The Gospel According to John

The fourth Gospel of the New Testament.

Author

Traditionally identified as John, the son of Zebedee and one of the twelve apostles.

Chapters

21 chapters

The Gospel moves from the eternal Word to the risen Christ commissioning His followers.

Purpose of the Gospel of John

John does not leave the purpose of his book uncertain. Near the end of the Gospel, he explains that Jesus performed many other signs that were not recorded in the book.

The signs John selected were written so that readers might recognize who Jesus is and respond to Him in faith.

Therefore, the Gospel of John is not merely a biography of Jesus. It is a carefully arranged testimony calling people to believe in Jesus Christ and receive eternal life through Him.

Purpose Truth: John wants the reader to answer the question:

Who is Jesus Christ, and will I believe in Him?

Major Structure of John

John 1:1–18 — The Prologue: The Eternal Word

John begins before Bethlehem. Jesus is presented as the eternal Word who was with God, was God, created all things, and became flesh.

John 1:19–12:50 — Jesus Revealed Through Signs and Teaching

Jesus begins His public ministry. John records signs, conversations, conflicts, and teachings that reveal His identity.

Some believe in Him, while opposition continues to grow.

John 13–17 — Jesus Prepares His Disciples

As the cross approaches, Jesus spends extended time with His disciples.

He teaches them about love, service, the Holy Spirit, abiding in Him, persecution, prayer, and their future mission.

John 18–20 — The Cross and Resurrection

Jesus is arrested, tried, crucified, buried, and raised from the dead.

The risen Christ appears to His disciples, including Thomas, leading to one of the Gospel's clearest confessions concerning Jesus.

John 21 — The Epilogue

The risen Jesus appears again to His disciples, restores Peter, and directs him toward faithful service.

Major Themes in John

Jesus Christ Is the Son of God

John repeatedly presents Jesus as uniquely related to the Father and sharing divine glory.

Belief and Unbelief

People repeatedly encounter Jesus and must respond to what they see and hear.

Some believe. Others reject Him despite the evidence before them.

Eternal Life

Eternal life is one of John's central themes.

It is not presented merely as something that begins after physical death. Eternal life is connected with knowing God and believing in Jesus Christ.

Light and Darkness

Jesus is presented as the true Light.

Light represents truth, revelation, and life, while darkness is connected with unbelief and resistance to God.

Witness and Testimony

John's Gospel contains many witnesses who testify concerning Jesus.

These include John the Baptist, the works of Jesus, the Father, Scripture, the disciples, and the Holy Spirit.

Jesus and the Father

Jesus repeatedly explains His relationship with the Father.

He speaks the Father's words, does the Father's works, carries out the Father's will, and reveals the Father.

The Holy Spirit

John gives important teaching concerning the Holy Spirit, especially in chapters 14–16.

Jesus promises another Helper who will teach, remind, testify, convict, guide, and glorify Christ.

Seven Signs in John's Gospel

John uses selected signs to reveal the identity and glory of Jesus. The following seven are commonly recognized as the major signs recorded during His public ministry.

  1. Water turned to wine — John 2:1–11
    Jesus reveals His glory at Cana.
  2. Healing the official's son — John 4:46–54
    Jesus demonstrates authority even from a distance.
  3. Healing at Bethesda — John 5:1–9
    Jesus restores a man who had been disabled for many years.
  4. Feeding the five thousand — John 6:1–14
    Jesus provides food for a great multitude.
  5. Walking on the sea — John 6:16–21
    Jesus demonstrates authority over creation.
  6. Healing the man born blind — John 9:1–7
    Jesus gives sight and exposes spiritual blindness.
  7. Raising Lazarus — John 11:1–44
    Jesus demonstrates His authority over death.
Why the Signs Matter: The signs are not merely displays of power. They reveal who Jesus is and call people to believe in Him.

The “I AM” Statements of Jesus

John records several major statements in which Jesus uses vivid images to explain His identity and saving work.

  • “I am the bread of life” — John 6:35
  • “I am the light of the world” — John 8:12
  • “I am the door” — John 10:7, 9
  • “I am the good shepherd” — John 10:11, 14
  • “I am the resurrection and the life” — John 11:25
  • “I am the way, and the truth, and the life” — John 14:6
  • “I am the true vine” — John 15:1

John also records other significant uses of the expression “I am,” including Jesus' declaration in John 8:58.

Key People in the Gospel of John

John the Baptist — testifies that Jesus is the Lamb of God and the Son of God.

Nicodemus — a Pharisee who comes to Jesus at night and hears Jesus teach about the new birth.

The Samaritan Woman — encounters Jesus at the well and becomes a witness to her community.

Martha, Mary, and Lazarus — friends of Jesus whose story becomes central to the raising of Lazarus in John 11.

Peter — one of Jesus' closest disciples, who later denies Him and is restored in John 21.

Thomas — moves from doubt to the confession, “My Lord and my God.”

Pontius Pilate — the Roman governor who questions Jesus and authorizes His crucifixion.

How John Presents Jesus Christ

John's Gospel presents Jesus from many complementary perspectives.

  • The eternal Word
  • The Creator
  • The true Light
  • The Lamb of God
  • The Messiah
  • The Son of God
  • The Bread of Life
  • The Light of the World
  • The Good Shepherd
  • The Resurrection and the Life
  • The Way, the Truth, and the Life
  • The True Vine
  • The crucified and risen Lord
Christ-Centered Truth: The entire Gospel of John calls us to see Jesus for who He truly is, believe in Him, follow Him, and receive the eternal life that only He can give.

Why the Gospel of John Matters

John addresses the most important question a person can face: Who is Jesus Christ?

The Gospel does not allow Jesus to remain merely a moral teacher, miracle worker, prophet, or religious leader.

John presents Him as the eternal Word, the Son sent by the Father, the promised Christ, the Savior, and the source of eternal life.

John's Gospel also teaches believers how to live in continuing relationship with Christ — abiding in Him, loving one another, depending upon the Holy Spirit, obeying His Word, and bearing witness to Him.

Key Scriptures to Remember

John 1:1 — The Word existed in the beginning and shares the divine identity.

John 1:14 — The Word became flesh and dwelt among us.

John 1:29 — Jesus is identified as the Lamb of God.

John 3:16 — God's love is revealed in the giving of His Son.

John 8:58 — Jesus makes a striking declaration concerning His existence before Abraham.

More Key Scriptures

John 10:30 — Jesus speaks of His unity with the Father.

John 11:25 — Jesus is the Resurrection and the Life.

John 14:6 — Jesus is the Way, the Truth, and the Life.

John 20:28 — Thomas confesses Jesus as “My Lord and my God.”

John 20:30–31 — John states the purpose of his Gospel.
