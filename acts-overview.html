Bible Book Overview

The Book of Acts

The Risen Christ Continues His Work Through the Holy Spirit and His Witnesses

Main Theme

The Book of Acts shows how the risen Jesus Christ continued His work through His followers by the power of the Holy Spirit.

Beginning in Jerusalem, the gospel spread outward through Judea, Samaria, and eventually toward the wider Roman world.

Acts demonstrates that Christ builds His church through Spirit-empowered witnesses who proclaim the gospel, endure opposition, pray, serve, and remain faithful to His mission.

Where Acts Fits in the Bible

Acts comes immediately after the four Gospels.

The Gospels record the earthly ministry, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ.

Acts begins with the risen Jesus preparing His disciples for the work they would continue after His ascension.

The book then records the growth of the early church and the spread of the gospel.

Simple Connection:

The Gospels show what Jesus began to do during His earthly ministry.

Acts shows how His work continued through His followers by the power of the Holy Spirit.

Author

The Book of Acts was written by Luke, the same author who wrote the Gospel of Luke.

Acts begins by referring back to Luke's earlier account of the life and ministry of Jesus.

Luke presents an orderly account of the early church and the spread of the gospel.

Purpose of Acts

Acts records how the message of Jesus Christ moved from Jerusalem into the surrounding regions and eventually throughout much of the Roman world.

The book shows:

The power of the Holy Spirit

The birth and growth of the early church

The courage of the apostles

The spread of the gospel

The conversion of both Jews and Gentiles

The importance of prayer, unity, teaching, fellowship, and mission

Key Verse

Acts 1:8

“But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

Acts 1:8 provides a simple outline for much of the book.

The witness begins in Jerusalem and steadily moves outward.

Major Sections of Acts

Acts 1–7 — The Church in Jerusalem

Jesus ascends to heaven, the Holy Spirit comes at Pentecost, the apostles begin preaching, and the early church grows in Jerusalem.

Acts 8–12 — The Gospel Spreads Beyond Jerusalem

Persecution scatters believers, the gospel reaches Samaria, Philip ministers to the Ethiopian, Saul is converted, and Peter witnesses God's work among Gentiles.

Acts 13–20 — Paul's Missionary Journeys

Paul and his companions travel through many regions preaching Christ, planting churches, teaching believers, and enduring opposition.

Acts 21–28 — Paul Goes Toward Rome

Paul is arrested, gives testimony before rulers, appeals to Caesar, survives a dangerous journey, and eventually reaches Rome.

Major Themes in Acts

1. The Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit empowers believers for witness, gives direction, strengthens the church, and works through God's people.

2. The Resurrection of Jesus

The apostles repeatedly proclaim that Jesus died and rose again.

The resurrection stands at the center of their message.

3. Witness

Believers are called to testify about Jesus Christ.

Their witness spreads from one place to another.

4. Prayer

The early church repeatedly turns to God in prayer for guidance, boldness, wisdom, strength, and direction.

5. The Growth of the Church

God adds people to the church as the gospel is proclaimed.

Growth includes both increasing numbers and spiritual maturity.

6. Opposition and Persecution

The early believers face threats, imprisonment, rejection, and violence.

Yet opposition does not stop the spread of the gospel.

7. The Gospel Reaches the Gentiles

Acts shows that the message of salvation through Jesus Christ is not limited to one nation or people.

The gospel continues outward to people from many backgrounds.

Important People in Acts

Peter

Peter becomes one of the primary leaders during the early chapters of Acts.

He preaches, performs miracles, faces persecution, and helps the church understand God's work among the Gentiles.

Stephen

Stephen faithfully witnesses about Christ and becomes the first recorded Christian martyr in Acts.

Philip

Philip carries the gospel into Samaria and later shares the truth of Christ with an Ethiopian official.

Paul

Saul begins as a persecutor of Christians.

After encountering Christ, he becomes Paul, one of the most influential missionaries in the early church.

Barnabas

Barnabas becomes known as an encourager and plays an important role in Paul's early ministry.

What Acts Teaches About the Church

The Book of Acts gives us an important picture of the early church.

The church was centered on Jesus Christ.

The church depended upon the Holy Spirit.

The church was devoted to the apostles' teaching.

The church prayed.

The church shared life together.

The church cared for people in need.

The church proclaimed the gospel.

The church endured persecution.

The church sent believers out to make Christ known.

Acts and the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is central throughout the Book of Acts.

Jesus tells the disciples to wait for the Spirit's power.

At Pentecost, the Spirit comes upon the believers.

Throughout the book, the Holy Spirit:

Gives power for witness

Provides direction

Gives courage

Guides ministry

Strengthens believers

Works through the church

Acts reminds us that the work of God cannot be accomplished through human strength alone.

Practical Truth

Christ still calls His people to be witnesses.

We may not serve in the same locations or circumstances as Peter, Paul, or the other believers in Acts.

But every believer has been placed somewhere where Christ can be represented.

We need God's Word.

We need prayer.

We need Christian fellowship.

We need courage.

And we need the power and guidance of the Holy Spirit.

Questions to Keep in Mind While Studying Acts

1. What does this chapter teach me about Jesus Christ?

2. What does this chapter teach me about the Holy Spirit?

3. How did the early believers respond to opposition?

4. What role did prayer play in the life of the church?

5. How did the gospel continue spreading?

6. What can today's church learn from their example?

7. What should I personally apply to my life?

Begin the Study

Begin with Acts 1, where Jesus prepares His disciples for the mission ahead.

He promises the power of the Holy Spirit, calls them to be His witnesses, ascends to heaven, and prepares the early believers for what comes next.

Open Acts 1 Commentary

Remember

Acts is not simply the story of great apostles.

It is the story of the risen Christ continuing His work through ordinary people empowered by the Holy Spirit.

The message they carried is still the message the church carries today.

Jesus Christ is Lord.

He died and rose again.

Salvation is found in Him.

And His followers are called to make Him known.
