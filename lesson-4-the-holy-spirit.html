Lesson 4 — The Holy Spirit

The Person and Ministry of the Holy Spirit

Central Truth

The Holy Spirit is not merely a force or influence.

He is personal, divine, and active in the life of every believer.

He reveals the truth of God, glorifies Jesus Christ, dwells within believers, guides them, strengthens them, and helps transform their lives.

Key Scriptures

John 14:16–17
John 14:26
John 16:13–14
Acts 5:3–4
1 Corinthians 2:10–11
Romans 8:9–16
1 Corinthians 6:19
Galatians 5:16–25
Ephesians 4:30
Ephesians 5:18
Matthew 28:19

1. Who Is the Holy Spirit?

The Holy Spirit is part of the Godhead:

God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit.

The Holy Spirit is not merely an impersonal spiritual force.

He is personal, divine, and active.

The Holy Spirit reveals the mind and truth of God and works within the hearts and minds of believers.

He teaches. He guides. He convicts. He strengthens. He helps believers grow spiritually.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit is God, personally present and active in the believer, revealing God's truth and shaping the believer's life from within.

2. The Holy Spirit Is a Person

Scripture describes the Holy Spirit in personal ways.

Jesus speaks of the Spirit as He and describes Him as One who teaches, guides, speaks, and reveals truth.

John 16:13 says the Spirit will guide believers into truth and will speak what He hears.

The Holy Spirit can also be grieved.

Ephesians 4:30

“And grieve not the holy Spirit of God...”

An impersonal force cannot experience grief.

Scripture shows the Holy Spirit performing actions that belong to a person.

He speaks. He teaches. He guides. He reveals. He intercedes. He can be grieved.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit is not simply spiritual power. Scripture presents Him as a personal being who speaks, teaches, guides, and can be grieved.

3. The Holy Spirit Is God

Acts 5:3–4 provides one of the clearest biblical examples.

Peter tells Ananias that he has lied to the Holy Spirit.

Then Peter says that Ananias has not lied merely to men, but to God.

Peter connects lying to the Holy Spirit with lying to God.

The Holy Spirit is not presented as a lesser spiritual being or merely a force used by God.

He is divine.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit is not only personal. He is divine. Scripture identifies Him with God.

4. Other Scriptures Showing the Deity of the Holy Spirit

The biblical teaching about the Holy Spirit does not depend upon only one passage.

Several Scriptures together give us a fuller picture.

Hebrews 3:7–11

Hebrews introduces a quotation by saying: “Wherefore as the Holy Ghost saith...”

The quotation comes from Psalm 95, where God is the One speaking.

The writer of Hebrews attributes those words to the Holy Spirit.

Hebrews 10:15–17

Again Scripture says: “Whereof the Holy Ghost also is a witness to us...”

The passage then quotes words originally spoken by the LORD in Jeremiah 31.

1 Corinthians 2:10–11

The Spirit searches the deep things of God and knows the things of God.

This is not the description of an impersonal force.

The Spirit possesses knowledge and understanding.

Psalm 139:7

David asks: “Whither shall I go from thy spirit?”

God's Spirit is present wherever David goes.

Matthew 28:19

Jesus commands His disciples to baptize:

“In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost.”

The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are placed together under the one divine name.

Teaching Truth:

The deity of the Holy Spirit is not built on one isolated verse.

Scripture repeatedly presents the Holy Spirit as personal, possessing divine knowledge and presence, speaking the words of God, and being identified with God Himself.

5. The Holy Spirit Dwells in Believers

One of the most important truths for a Christian to understand is that the Holy Spirit does not merely work around believers.

He dwells within them.

Romans 8:9 teaches that anyone who belongs to Christ has the Spirit.

Romans 8:11 speaks of the Spirit dwelling in believers and giving life.

First Corinthians 6:19 says the believer's body is the temple of the Holy Spirit.

This means the Christian life is not lived by human strength alone.

God has given His Spirit to live within believers.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit lives within every true believer, giving spiritual life, strengthening faith, and helping the believer grow in obedience to God.

6. The Holy Spirit Teaches Believers

Jesus told His disciples in John 14:26 that the Holy Spirit would teach them and bring His words to their remembrance.

The Holy Spirit helps believers understand spiritual truth.

This does not mean that every thought or feeling we have comes from the Holy Spirit.

What we believe must still agree with Scripture.

The Holy Spirit will never guide a believer in a way that contradicts the Word of God.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit helps believers understand God's truth, but His guidance will always agree with Scripture.

7. The Holy Spirit Guides Into Truth

John 16:13 teaches that the Spirit guides believers into truth.

He does not speak independently from God.

He communicates what is true and reveals what God wants His people to understand.

The Spirit's guidance is never meant to draw believers away from Christ.

Instead, it leads them deeper into truth.

8. The Holy Spirit Glorifies Jesus Christ

John 16:14 gives an important principle.

Jesus said the Holy Spirit would glorify Him.

The Spirit does not draw attention to Himself apart from Christ.

His ministry points believers toward Jesus.

He helps us understand who Christ is.

He helps us understand what Christ has done.

He helps us live in a way that honors Christ.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit glorifies Jesus Christ, reveals truth about Him, and helps believers live faithfully as His followers.

9. The Holy Spirit Convicts

Jesus taught in John 16 that the Holy Spirit would convict the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment.

Conviction is different from simple guilt.

Conviction brings awareness that something is wrong before God.

The Holy Spirit exposes sin and points people toward truth.

In the believer's life, conviction should lead toward repentance and restoration rather than hopelessness.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit reveals sin so that people can respond to God in repentance and truth.

10. The Holy Spirit Helps Us Resist Sin

Galatians 5 teaches believers to walk by the Spirit.

The Christian life contains a struggle between sinful desires and the life God wants us to live.

The Holy Spirit gives believers power to resist the desires of the flesh.

This does not mean temptation disappears.

It means the believer is no longer left to fight temptation alone.

Teaching Truth:

Walking by the Spirit means learning to depend on God's power instead of continually surrendering to sinful desires.

11. The Fruit of the Spirit

Galatians 5:22–23 describes the fruit produced by the Spirit.

Love
Joy
Peace
Patience
Kindness
Goodness
Faithfulness
Gentleness
Self-control

These qualities are evidence of spiritual growth.

The fruit of the Spirit is not produced simply by trying harder.

It grows as believers remain surrendered to God and allow the Holy Spirit to shape their character.

Teaching Truth:

Spiritual maturity is seen not only in what a believer knows, but also in the character the Holy Spirit produces in their life.

12. The Holy Spirit Gives Gifts for Ministry

First Corinthians 12 teaches that the Holy Spirit gives spiritual gifts to believers.

These gifts are not given so that believers can compete with one another.

They are given for service.

Different believers receive different gifts so that the entire body of Christ can function together.

Teaching Truth:

Spiritual gifts are given by the Holy Spirit so believers can serve others and help strengthen the body of Christ.

13. The Holy Spirit Helps Us Pray

Romans 8 teaches that believers do not always know how to pray as they should.

The Spirit helps us in our weakness.

This is encouraging because there will be times when we feel overwhelmed, confused, or unable to express what is happening inside us.

God understands.

The Holy Spirit helps believers even in those moments.

14. Do Not Grieve the Holy Spirit

Ephesians 4:30 warns believers not to grieve the Holy Spirit.

The surrounding verses deal with things such as anger, bitterness, corrupt speech, and sinful behavior.

Because the Holy Spirit dwells within believers, the way we live matters.

Our choices can either honor His work in us or resist it.

Teaching Truth:

Because the Holy Spirit lives within believers, Christians should desire to live in a way that honors His presence.

15. Be Filled With the Spirit

Ephesians 5:18 tells believers to be filled with the Spirit.

This means believers should continually allow the Holy Spirit to influence and direct their lives.

Being filled with the Spirit affects:

Our worship
Our speech
Our relationships
Our attitudes
Our obedience
Our service

The Christian life is not meant to be lived independently from God.

16. Walking With the Holy Spirit

Walking with the Holy Spirit is not about constantly searching for unusual experiences.

It is about learning to live daily in dependence upon God.

A believer grows by:

Reading Scripture
Praying
Obeying what God reveals
Turning away from sin
Responding to conviction
Allowing the Spirit to produce Christlike character
Teaching Truth:

Walking by the Spirit is a daily life of dependence, obedience, and surrender to God.

A Simple Way to Understand the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is a Person.

The Holy Spirit is God.

The Holy Spirit lives within believers.

The Holy Spirit works to glorify Jesus and transform believers.

Questions for Reflection

1. Why is it important to understand that the Holy Spirit is a Person and not merely a force?

2. What does Acts 5:3–4 teach about the Holy Spirit?

3. What does it mean for the Holy Spirit to dwell within a believer?

4. How does the Holy Spirit help believers understand truth?

5. Why is John 16:14 important for understanding the ministry of the Holy Spirit?

6. What is the fruit of the Spirit?

7. How can a believer grieve the Holy Spirit?

8. What does it mean to walk by the Spirit?

9. What area of your life do you most need the Holy Spirit to help you change?

This Week's Practical Step

Read:

John 14:16–17
John 16:13–14
Romans 8:9–16
Galatians 5:16–25

As you read, write down:

What does this passage teach me about who the Holy Spirit is?

What does it teach me about what the Holy Spirit does?

What should change in my life because the Holy Spirit lives within me?

Then pray and ask God to help you live each day in greater dependence upon the Holy Spirit.

Remember

The Holy Spirit is not an impersonal force.

He is personal.

He is divine.

He teaches.

He guides.

He convicts.

He strengthens.

He lives within believers.

He produces spiritual fruit.

He gives gifts for service.

His ministry continually points believers toward Jesus Christ.

The Christian life was never meant to be lived by human strength alone.

God has given His Spirit to live within His people and help them become more like Christ.
