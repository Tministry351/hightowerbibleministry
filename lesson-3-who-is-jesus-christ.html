Lesson 3 — Jesus Christ

Part 1 — Who Is Jesus Christ?

Before we study what Jesus accomplished for our salvation, we must first understand who Scripture reveals Him to be.

Primary Scriptures

Primary Passages: John 1:1–18 • Colossians 1:15–20 • Philippians 2:5–11 • Hebrews 1:1–4

Supporting Scriptures: Matthew 16:13–17 • John 8:58 • John 20:28 • 1 Corinthians 8:6
Central Teaching Truth

Jesus Christ is the eternal Son of God, the Word who became flesh, the promised Messiah, the Creator and Lord of all, and the One who perfectly reveals the Father.

Introduction — The Question Every Person Must Answer

During His ministry, Jesus asked His disciples what people were saying about Him. Several answers were being given, but Jesus made the question personal: Who do you say that I am?

That question remains central to Christianity. Jesus cannot be understood merely as a wise teacher, moral example, prophet, or religious leader.

Scripture presents Him as the eternal Son, the promised Christ, the Word who became flesh, and the Lord through whom God created and sustains all things.

1. Jesus Christ — The Eternal Word and Son of God

Scripture teaches that Jesus Christ did not begin His existence when He was born in Bethlehem. Before His earthly birth, He already existed with the Father.

John 1:1 declares, “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” John identifies Jesus as the eternal Word. He was with God, showing a distinction between the Son and the Father, yet Scripture also declares that the Word was God.

John 1:3 goes even further by telling us that all things were made through Him. Jesus therefore cannot simply be another part of creation, because Scripture presents Him as the One through whom creation came into existence.

Then John 1:14 tells us that “the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.” The eternal Son entered human history. Jesus did not cease to be who He was; He took on true human nature and lived among mankind.

Key Truth

Jesus Christ is the eternal Son of God. His life did not begin at Bethlehem. The eternal Word became flesh and entered His own creation so that God might be revealed to us and salvation accomplished.

Jesus Christ and Creation — Colossians 1:15–17

Colossians 1 describes Jesus as “the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature.” The word “firstborn” does not mean that Jesus was the first being God created.

The verses that follow explain what Paul means. Colossians 1:16 says that by Christ all things were created—things in heaven, things on earth, visible and invisible. Everything was created through Him and for Him.

Verse 17 then says that Christ is “before all things,” and that by Him all things consist. In other words, Jesus existed before creation and continues to sustain what He created.

In this passage, “firstborn” speaks of Christ's supremacy, authority, and position over all creation. He is not presented as part of creation, but as the One who stands above it.

This is important because Scripture does not describe Jesus as merely a great teacher, prophet, or created heavenly being. Paul presents Him as the One through whom all creation came into existence and the One for whom it exists.

The Word Became Flesh — John 1:14

John 1:14 teaches that “the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.” This means that the eternal Son of God truly entered human life. He did not merely appear to be human. He became fully human while remaining who He eternally was.

This truth is essential to the Christian faith because Jesus had to truly enter our human condition in order to represent us, suffer for us, die for our sins, and become our faithful High Priest.

The same Jesus who existed before creation stepped into His own creation. The Creator became man without ceasing to be the eternal Son.

Why This Matters

Jesus Christ is not simply a religious teacher who came to tell people about God. He is the eternal Son who came from the Father, entered human history, revealed God to us, and came to accomplish salvation.

A. The Son Shares the Glory and Nature of God

Scripture does more than tell us that Jesus existed before Bethlehem. It describes Him in ways that reveal His unique relationship with God and His divine glory.

Hebrews 1:2–3 says that God has spoken to us through His Son, whom He appointed heir of all things and through whom He made the worlds. The Son is described as “the brightness of his glory” and “the express image of his person.”

This means that Jesus does not merely tell us something about God. He perfectly reveals God's character and nature. In the Son, we see the holiness, righteousness, love, truth, authority, and glory of God made known.

Hebrews also says that the Son upholds all things by the word of His power. Jesus is therefore not presented as a powerless messenger or created servant. He is shown as the One through whom God created and continues to sustain all things.

Key Teaching

Jesus Christ is the perfect revelation of God. He shares the Father's glory, carries divine authority, and reveals to us what God is truly like.

Scripture presents Jesus Christ as the One who uniquely makes God known. John 1:18 teaches that the Son has made the Father known. Colossians 1:15 calls Jesus “the image of the invisible God,” and Hebrews 1:3 describes Him as the brightness of God's glory and the exact representation of His nature.

This does not mean that Jesus and the Father are the same Person. Scripture distinguishes the Son from the Father. Jesus prays to the Father, speaks of being sent by the Father, and speaks of returning to the Father.

Yet Jesus reveals the Father perfectly because He shares the divine nature. His words, character, works, holiness, compassion, authority, righteousness, and truth reveal to us what God is like.

Jesus said in John 14:9, “He that hath seen me hath seen the Father.” He was not saying that He is the Father, but that in seeing the Son, people were seeing the perfect revelation of the Father's character.

Key Teaching

We cannot rightly understand God while ignoring Jesus Christ. The Son reveals the Father to us, and through Christ we come to understand God's character, truth, holiness, love, and saving purpose.

C. Jesus Is Creator and Lord

John 1 and Colossians 1 place Jesus on the Creator side of the Creator-creature distinction.

All things came into being through Him, and creation continues to depend upon Him. Jesus is therefore not merely one important figure within creation. He is Lord over creation.

Understanding "Firstborn of All Creation" — Colossians 1:15–17

Colossians 1:15 describes Jesus Christ as "the firstborn of every creature" (KJV). Does this mean Jesus was the first being God created? To answer this question, we must examine the surrounding verses and the biblical meaning of "firstborn."

In Scripture, "firstborn" can refer to position, authority, and preeminence rather than simply the order of birth. In Psalm 89:27, God says of the Davidic king, “I will make him my firstborn,” and then explains this as being “higher than the kings of the earth.” Here, “firstborn” clearly emphasizes royal rank, authority, and supremacy rather than the order of birth.

Colossians 1:16–17 explains that all things were created through Christ and for Christ, including things in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible. Paul also declares that Christ existed before all things and that all things hold together in Him.

Therefore, in its context, "firstborn of all creation" emphasizes Christ's supremacy over creation. Jesus is not presented as a created being, but as the eternal Son through whom all created things came into existence.

Doctrinal Truth: Jesus Christ is Creator and Lord over all creation. He is supreme over everything that has been made.

D. The Eternal Son Became Truly Human

John 1:14 teaches that “the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.” The eternal Son of God truly entered human life. Jesus did not merely appear to be human; He became genuinely human while remaining the eternal Son.

Philippians 2:5–8 explains that Christ humbled Himself, took the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men. He willingly entered the limitations and experiences of human life and became obedient even unto death on the cross.

Jesus experienced real human needs and emotions. He became hungry, tired, sorrowful, and physically weak. He experienced suffering, temptation, rejection, pain, and death. Yet Scripture teaches that He remained without sin.

Hebrews 4:15 tells us that Jesus was tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin. Because He truly became human, He is able to understand our weakness. Because He remained sinless, He was able to offer Himself as the perfect sacrifice for sin.

Why the Humanity of Christ Matters

Jesus had to truly become human in order to represent humanity, suffer and die for our sins, and become our faithful High Priest. The One who saves us is not distant from human suffering. He entered our condition, experienced our weakness, and overcame without sin.

Jesus Christ Is Superior to the Angels — Hebrews 1:1–14

Hebrews chapter 1 reveals the unique identity and authority of Jesus Christ as the Son of God. The writer distinguishes the Son from the angels and demonstrates His superiority over them.

Hebrews 1:3:
Jesus is described as the brightness of God's glory and the express image of His person. He upholds all things by the word of His power. This places the Son far above a created messenger. He perfectly reveals God's glory and exercises divine power in sustaining creation.

Hebrews 1:5:
God asks, “For unto which of the angels said he at any time, Thou art my Son?” The point is that no angel is given the unique identity and relationship that belongs to the Son. Jesus is not presented as one angel among many, but as the Son who stands in a completely different position.

Hebrews 1:6:
God commands the angels to worship the Son. The angels are not presented as equals of Christ. They are commanded to honor Him. This demonstrates the exalted position and authority of Jesus over the entire angelic order.

Hebrews 1:7:
Scripture describes the angels as ministering servants who carry out God's commands. Angels serve God and fulfill the purposes He gives them. This creates an important contrast with the Son, whose authority and eternal throne are described in the verses that follow.

Hebrews 1:8: The Father addresses the Son: "Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever." This passage is significant in understanding the divine identity of Jesus Christ.

Hebrews 1:8:
The Father addresses the Son with the words, “Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever.” This is one of the strongest statements in Hebrews concerning the identity of Jesus Christ. The Son is not described as a servant angel, but as the eternal King whose throne endures forever.

Hebrews 1:10–12:
The Son is associated with the creation of the heavens and the earth. Creation will grow old and change, but the Son remains. His years will not fail. These verses emphasize both His role as Creator and His unchanging, enduring nature.

Hebrews 1:13: God distinguishes the Son from the angels by inviting Him to sit at His right hand. This position reflects Christ's unique authority.

Doctrinal Truth: Hebrews 1 presents Jesus Christ as the divine Son who is superior to the angels. His identity and authority cannot be reduced to those of a created heavenly messenger.

Reflection Question: If Jesus were merely an angel, how should we understand the distinctions made between the Son and the angels throughout Hebrews chapter 1?

E. Jesus Is the Promised Christ

The title Christ means Messiah or Anointed One. In the Old Testament, God promised that a coming King and Deliverer would arise from the line of David and fulfill His saving purposes.

In Matthew 16:16, Peter confesses, “Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.” Peter was not simply calling Jesus a religious teacher. He was identifying Him as the promised Messiah whom God had sent.

The Old Testament prepares us for this promise. Isaiah 9:6–7 speaks of a coming Son whose government and kingdom will have no end. Micah 5:2 points to a ruler who would come from Bethlehem. Isaiah 53 describes the suffering Servant who would bear the sins of many.

Jesus fulfills these promises in a way that brings together both kingship and suffering. He is the promised King, but He also came first to suffer, die, and accomplish redemption.

This helps us understand why the title “Christ” is so important. It declares that Jesus is the One in whom God's promises of redemption, kingship, and salvation come together.

Key Teaching

Jesus is the promised Messiah. He fulfills God's Old Testament promises, brings salvation through His suffering and death, and will reign as the promised King.

Teaching Sentence: Jesus Christ is not merely a teacher who tells us about God; He is the eternal Son, the Creator and Lord, the promised Messiah, who became truly human, revealed the Father, accomplished redemption, and will reign as the promised King.

3. Biblical Truth — What Must We Believe?

A biblical understanding of Jesus must begin with what Scripture says about Him, not with human opinion, religious tradition, or personal preference.

Scripture reveals Jesus as eternal, divine, truly human, Creator, Lord, Messiah, and Son of God.

These truths are not secondary details. Who Jesus is determines how we understand His words, His works, His death, His resurrection, His authority, His saving work, and the worship and obedience He deserves.

Biblical Truth: Our faith must rest in the Jesus revealed in Scripture, not in a Jesus shaped by human opinion.

4. Application — How Should This Change My Life?

If Jesus is truly the eternal Son, Creator, Lord, Messiah, and Savior revealed in Scripture, then He deserves more than admiration or intellectual agreement.

He deserves our trust, worship, obedience, allegiance, and surrender. To know who Jesus is should change the way we think, the way we live, the way we worship, and the way we respond to His Word.

Following Christ means recognizing His authority over every area of our lives. We do not simply learn facts about Jesus; we are called to trust Him, obey Him, and become more like Him.

The question Jesus asked His disciples is therefore personal: Who do I believe Jesus is, and does my life reflect that confession?
