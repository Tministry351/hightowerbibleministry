Lesson 2 — The Nature and Attributes of God

Part 2 — God's Eternal Nature

God has always existed, depends on nothing outside Himself, and remains eternally faithful.

Download / Print PDF

Lesson Foundation

Lesson question: What does it mean that God is eternal, and how does this truth shape our faith?

Central truth: God has no beginning or end. He is uncreated, self-existent and unchanging in His divine character.

Primary passage: Psalm 90:1–4. Supporting Scriptures: Genesis 1:1; Exodus 3:13–15; Isaiah 40:28; Malachi 3:6; John 1:1–3; John 17:3; Hebrews 13:8; Revelation 1:8.

Why This Lesson Matters

In Part 1 we learned that knowing God is greater than trusting human wisdom, strength or riches. Now we begin to examine who God is. Everything we depend on in this world can change or come to an end. God alone is the eternal foundation of our faith.

Knowing His eternal nature helps us distinguish the Creator from everything He created. It also gives us confidence that His promises are not limited by our lifetime or circumstances.

Biblical Background

Psalm 90 is identified in its heading as a prayer of Moses. It contrasts the everlasting God with the brevity and frailty of human life. Moses does not begin with human achievement but with God as the dwelling place of His people throughout generations.

Psalm 90:1–4 — From Everlasting to Everlasting

“Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever thou hadst formed the earth and the world, even from everlasting to everlasting, thou art God.” — Psalm 90:2 (KJV)

The mountains appear permanent to us, yet they had a beginning. God existed before the earth and before every generation that has lived upon it. The words “from everlasting to everlasting” express His existence without beginning or end.

Verse 4 compares a thousand years in God’s sight to yesterday when it is past and to a watch in the night. The comparison emphasizes the difference between His perspective and our limited lifespan; it is not a formula for calculating prophetic dates.

Key Biblical Truth: God is not merely older than creation. He is the eternal Creator upon whom creation depends.

Genesis 1:1 — God Before Creation

“In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.” — Genesis 1:1 (KJV)

Scripture begins with God already present. The beginning described here is the beginning of the created world, not the beginning of God. Everything created owes its existence to Him. Asking who created God confuses the uncreated Creator with the things He made.

Acts 17:24–25 teaches that God does not need human hands to supply anything He lacks. He gives life and breath to all. His existence is not sustained by creation, worship or any outside power. This is sometimes called divine self-existence or aseity.

Key Biblical Truth: God depends on nothing outside Himself; everything else depends on Him.

Exodus 3:13–15 — I AM

“I AM THAT I AM” — Exodus 3:14 (KJV)

When Moses asks what name he should give Israel, God identifies Himself as I AM and as the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. The context connects His name with His presence and faithfulness to His covenant people (Exodus 3:12, 15).

God is not defined by human opinions or dependent on changing circumstances. His promise to be with Moses rests on who He is. Christians have long understood this revelation as consistent with His self-existence and eternal faithfulness, though the name is not merely an abstract philosophical definition.

Isaiah 40:28–31 — The Everlasting Creator

“...the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary...” — Isaiah 40:28 (KJV)

Isaiah addresses people who feel forgotten by God (verse 27). The answer is not that their difficulties are imaginary but that the Creator does not grow tired or lack understanding. He gives strength to the weary. Even the young may become exhausted, but those who wait upon the LORD are called to renewed hope.

Key Biblical Truth: The eternal God does not become exhausted by the needs of His people.

Malachi 3:6 — God’s Unchanging Character

“For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.” — Malachi 3:6 (KJV)

God’s eternity and immutability are related but distinct truths. Eternity concerns His existence; immutability concerns His unchanging divine nature and character. In Malachi, His covenant faithfulness explains why Israel has not been consumed. James 1:17 likewise points to the Father’s constancy.

God acts within history and responds to people, but He does not become less holy, less loving or less truthful. His dealings with people may differ as they repent or rebel without His character becoming inconsistent.

John 1:1–3, 14 — The Eternal Word

“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” — John 1:1 (KJV)

John distinguishes the Word from the Father while affirming the Word’s deity. All created things came into being through the Word. The Word is therefore not part of the created order. Verse 14 identifies the Word who became flesh, Jesus Christ.

The incarnation is not the beginning of the Son’s existence. Jesus speaks of the glory He had with the Father before the world existed (John 17:5). Hebrews 13:8 declares His enduring constancy. Together these passages support the Christian confession that the Son is eternal and truly divine.

Key Biblical Truth: Jesus Christ entered human history in the incarnation, but His divine existence did not begin at Bethlehem.

Revelation 1:8 — The Alpha and the Omega

The first and last letters of the Greek alphabet express the comprehensive sovereignty of the Lord God, who is, who was, and who is to come. Revelation 1:17–18 also presents the risen Christ as the First and the Last, the One who died and is alive forevermore. God’s eternal rule gives believers hope beyond the present age.

God’s Eternity and Our Eternal Life

God possesses eternal existence by nature. Human beings are created and have a beginning. Eternal life is God’s gift through Jesus Christ (Romans 6:23), not an attribute we possess independently.

John 17:3 describes eternal life in terms of knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom He sent. Eternal life is not simply an endless extension of earthly existence; it is life in relationship with God through Christ.

Key Biblical Truth: We receive eternal life from God; we do not become eternal in the same sense that God is eternal.

Common Misunderstandings

God had a beginning: Genesis 1:1 and Psalm 90:2 distinguish God from creation.

God is simply an immortal creature: God is uncreated and self-existent, not a being whose life is sustained by another.

God’s unchanging nature means He is inactive: Scripture shows Him creating, judging, saving and answering prayer while remaining faithful to His character.

Eternal life makes believers equal with God: eternal life is a gift, not independence from the Creator.

Practical Christian Living

Trust His promises when circumstances change (Hebrews 10:23). Pray with patience, remembering that God’s perspective is not limited to our timetable (2 Peter 3:8–9). Invest in what has eternal value rather than building your identity around temporary possessions (Matthew 6:19–21). Worship God for who He is, not merely for what He gives.

This week, read Psalm 90 and write down one way God’s eternity changes your response to uncertainty. Pray over that concern and choose one concrete act of obedience.

Questions for Deeper Understanding

1. How does Psalm 90 distinguish God from His creation?

2. What is the difference between eternal existence and eternal life?

3. What does Exodus 3 reveal about God’s identity and faithfulness?

4. How does John 1:1–3 support the eternal nature of Christ?

5. How can God act in history without changing His divine character?

6. What temporary things compete with your trust in the eternal God?

Remember

Everything created has a beginning. God has none. Generations pass, circumstances change, and earthly security fades, but the eternal God remains faithful. Build your life upon Him.

Closing Prayer

Eternal Father, thank You that You have always existed and that Your character never changes. Teach us to trust Your Word, rest in Your faithfulness, and live for what has eternal value. Help us know You more deeply through Jesus Christ and glorify You in our daily lives. In Jesus’ name, amen.
