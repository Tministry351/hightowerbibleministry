Historical Books • Joshua

Joshua 1:1–9

Be Strong and Courageous

Central Truth

God calls His people to move forward with courage, remain grounded in His Word, and trust His presence while carrying out what He has called them to do.
Key Passage: Joshua 1:1–9

Observation

Joshua 1:1–5 — God Commissions Joshua

Moses has died.

Joshua is now being called to lead Israel.

God tells Joshua that the time has come to cross the Jordan River and enter the land He had promised to Israel.

Joshua is stepping into a tremendous responsibility.

He had seen how God used Moses. He had also seen Israel's stubbornness, fear, complaining, and rebellion.

Joshua may have felt fearful, apprehensive, and overwhelmed by the responsibility of leading so many people.

But God reminds Joshua that the same presence that was with Moses would also be with him.

Joshua 1:5

“As I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.”

Joshua was not being called to depend on himself.

He was being called to depend on God.

Interpretation

Joshua's Courage Was Rooted in God's Presence

Joshua had a difficult assignment, but God did not tell him that the assignment would be easy.

Instead, God promised to be with him.

Joshua's confidence was not supposed to be:

“I am strong enough to handle this.”

His confidence was to be:

“God is with me.”

Teaching Truth:

Biblical courage is not the absence of fear. It is moving forward in obedience because we trust that God is present and faithful.

Joshua 1:6–8 — Courage and Obedience

God repeatedly tells Joshua to be strong and courageous.

In verses 6, 7, and 9, Joshua receives direct encouragement from God.

Later, in verse 18, the people also tell Joshua to be strong and courageous.

This repeated encouragement shows how serious the assignment was.

Joshua would need strength, courage, and dependence on God.

But God also connects courage with obedience.

Joshua is told not to turn from God's Law to the right or to the left.

He must remain faithful to what God has already revealed.

Teaching Truth:

Courage without obedience can become self-confidence. Biblical courage remains submitted to God's Word.

Joshua 1:8 — Stay Grounded in God's Word

God tells Joshua that the Book of the Law should not depart from his mouth.

Joshua is to meditate on it day and night.

He is to observe carefully everything written in it.

God's Word had to remain central to Joshua's leadership.

Joshua could not lead Israel successfully by depending only on experience, intelligence, leadership ability, or military strategy.

He needed continual fellowship with God through His Word.

Teaching Truth:

God's Word is essential for our daily spiritual life. It gives us direction, wisdom, correction, and strength so that we can faithfully accomplish what God has called us to do.

Just as the body needs physical food, believers need spiritual nourishment.

Jesus later expressed the same principle in Matthew 4:4 when He taught that man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from God.

Know the Word, Meditate on the Word, Obey the Word

Joshua 1:8 gives us an important pattern:

Know the Word.

Meditate on the Word.

Obey the Word.

Walk faithfully in what God has called you to do.

Biblical meditation is not simply reading quickly and moving on.

It involves thinking deeply about what God has said and allowing His truth to shape the way we think and live.

What Does Biblical Success Mean?

Joshua 1:8 speaks about prosperity and success.

In this context, God is not primarily promising Joshua wealth, comfort, or everything he personally desires.

The focus is Joshua successfully carrying out the assignment God gave him.

Joshua's success would be measured by faithfulness to God.

Teaching Truth:

True spiritual success is not measured by how much we accomplish for ourselves. It is measured by how faithfully we obey what God has called us to do.

Joshua 1:9 — Courage Rooted in God's Presence

Joshua 1:9

“Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.”

God does not merely suggest courage.

He commands it.

Joshua was not supposed to pretend that the task was easy.

The task was difficult. The people were difficult. The future was uncertain.

But God gave Joshua the reason he could move forward:

The LORD his God was with him.

Do Not Be Afraid

Fear can cause us to hesitate, retreat, or refuse to obey.

Do Not Be Dismayed

Discouragement can cause us to lose heart and give up.

Teaching Truth:

Biblical courage is not confidence in ourselves. It is confidence in God's presence, God's promises, and God's faithfulness.

Application

1. God May Call Us Into Difficult Assignments

God's calling does not always mean the assignment will be easy.

Sometimes obedience requires us to step into situations where we feel inadequate.

Joshua was not chosen because the task was easy.

He was chosen because God intended to work through him.

2. Do Not Depend Only on Your Own Strength

Joshua could not lead Israel in his own strength.

Neither can believers successfully carry out God's work by depending only on themselves.

When God calls us to something difficult, He does not expect us to carry the assignment in our own strength.

Our courage comes from trusting His presence, His Word, and His faithfulness.

Stay Grounded in God's Word

We can become so busy doing things for God that we stop spending time with God.

That creates spiritual danger.

Without God's Word, we can become confused, spiritually weak, discouraged, worn out, or ready to quit.

When we separate God's work from God's Word and God's presence, we become vulnerable to discouragement and burnout.

But when we remain rooted in Him, He gives us strength to continue and use our lives for His glory.

Do Not Let Failure Stop the Journey

There may be times when we become discouraged or spiritually stuck.

The answer is not to give up.

We return to God.

We repent where necessary.

We get back up.

We continue moving forward.

Spiritual growth is not perfection.

It is continuing to trust, learn, obey, repent, and move forward with God.

Remember Who Is With You

Joshua's courage did not come from believing everything would go according to his plans.

His courage came from knowing that God was with him.

That same truth should shape the way believers face difficult assignments today.

When God has clearly called us to obey Him, fear and discouragement should not become greater in our minds than the God we trust.

Questions for Reflection

1. What do you think Joshua may have felt when God called him to take Moses' place?

2. Why did Joshua need repeated encouragement to be strong and courageous?

3. What is the difference between biblical courage and self-confidence?

4. Why was God's Word so important to Joshua's leadership?

5. What does Joshua 1:8 teach us about true spiritual success?

6. What happens when believers try to do God's work without remaining grounded in God's Word?

7. Is there an area of your life where fear or discouragement has caused you to hesitate?

8. What would it look like for you to move forward while trusting God's presence?

Practical Step

Identify one responsibility, assignment, or situation where you feel discouraged, afraid, or spiritually stuck.

Read God's Word about the situation.

Pray and ask God for wisdom and courage.

Take one step of obedience instead of remaining stuck.

Remember

Joshua was not told to be strong because he was naturally capable of handling everything ahead of him.

He was told to be strong because God was with him.

Stay in God's Word.

Trust His presence.

Obey what He has revealed.

Keep moving forward with courage.
