Matthew In-Depth Study • Chapter 3

Matthew 3 — Prepare the Way of the Lord

Repentance, the Coming Messiah, and the Baptism of Jesus

Matthew 3 introduces John the Baptist as the forerunner of the Messiah, calls people to genuine repentance, points to the greater One who is coming, and records the baptism of Jesus where the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are revealed together.

Study Overview

Primary Passage: Matthew 3:1–17

Supporting Scriptures: Isaiah 40:3; Malachi 3:1; Malachi 4:5–6; Mark 1:1–11; Luke 3:1–22; John 1:19–34.
Central Teaching Truth:

Matthew 3 teaches that God prepares people to receive the Messiah through repentance, reveals Jesus as the greater One who baptizes with the Holy Spirit, and publicly identifies Him as the beloved Son in whom the Father is well pleased.

1. Matthew 3:1–2 — John Calls People to Repent

Observation

John the Baptist appears in the wilderness of Judea preaching a clear message: “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.”

Interpretation

Repentance involves more than feeling sorry. It is a turning of the mind and heart toward God that results in a changed direction of life.

John's message announces urgency. God's kingdom is drawing near, and people must prepare themselves to receive the King.

Teaching Truth:

Preparation for the Messiah begins with repentance.

Application

We should never reduce repentance to words alone. True repentance acknowledges sin, turns toward God, and begins producing a changed life.

2. Matthew 3:3 — John Fulfills the Prophecy of Isaiah

Observation

Matthew identifies John as the voice spoken of by Isaiah: one crying in the wilderness, preparing the way of the Lord.

Interpretation

John's ministry is not accidental. He appears as part of God's prophetic plan.

His assignment is to prepare people for the arrival of Jesus.

The wording from Isaiah is especially significant because it speaks of preparing the way of the Lord. Matthew applies this preparation directly to the coming of Jesus.

Biblical Truth:

John prepares the way for Jesus, showing that the coming of Christ is the fulfillment of God's promised redemptive work.

3. Matthew 3:4–6 — People Respond With Confession

Observation

John lives simply in the wilderness, wearing clothing made of camel's hair and eating locusts and wild honey.

People from Jerusalem, Judea, and the surrounding region come to him.

They confess their sins and are baptized in the Jordan River.

Interpretation

John's ministry draws attention not because of luxury, status, or appearance, but because of the truth of his message.

The people's confession shows that repentance includes an honest recognition of sin.

John's baptism serves as a public expression of repentance and preparation for the coming Messiah.

Teaching Truth:

Genuine repentance refuses to hide sin and instead brings it honestly before God.

4. Matthew 3:7–10 — Repentance Must Produce Fruit

Observation

John sees many Pharisees and Sadducees coming and confronts them strongly.

He tells them to produce fruit consistent with repentance.

He also warns them not to depend upon their physical descent from Abraham.

Interpretation

Religious heritage cannot substitute for personal repentance.

Being connected to Abraham physically did not automatically mean that a person's heart was right with God.

John insists that repentance should become visible. Changed hearts should eventually produce changed lives.

Teaching Truth:

Genuine repentance produces fruit. Religious identity alone cannot replace a transformed heart.

Application

We should examine whether our faith is resting merely on church attendance, family background, religious knowledge, or outward appearance.

God desires truth in the heart that becomes visible in the way we live.

5. Matthew 3:10 — God's Judgment Is Real

Observation

John describes the axe as already lying at the root of the trees.

Trees that do not produce good fruit are cut down and thrown into the fire.

Interpretation

John's message includes both grace and warning.

The invitation to repent is gracious, but refusing God's call has consequences.

Scripture does not present repentance as an optional religious improvement. It is part of a serious response to God's rule.

Biblical Truth:

God's patience should never be confused with the absence of judgment.

6. Matthew 3:11 — The Greater One Is Coming

Observation

John explains that he baptizes with water for repentance.

But One more powerful than John is coming.

John says he is not worthy even to carry His sandals.

The coming One will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire.

Interpretation

John understands that his ministry is temporary and subordinate to Jesus.

Water baptism symbolizes repentance, but Jesus brings a deeper work associated with the Holy Spirit.

John's statement emphasizes the superiority and authority of Christ.

Teaching Truth:

John prepares people for Jesus, but Jesus alone possesses the authority to give the Holy Spirit.

7. Matthew 3:12 — The Messiah Brings Separation and Judgment

Observation

John uses the picture of a threshing floor.

The wheat is gathered into the barn, while the chaff is burned.

Interpretation

The coming of Jesus brings more than comfort. It also brings division between those who belong to Him and those who reject God's rule.

Jesus is Savior, but He is also Judge.

Teaching Truth:

The Messiah offers salvation, but His coming also confronts every person with the necessity of responding to Him.

8. Matthew 3:13–15 — Jesus Comes to Be Baptized

Observation

Jesus comes from Galilee to the Jordan to be baptized by John.

John initially objects, recognizing that he himself needs what Jesus can provide.

Jesus tells John to permit it in order to fulfill all righteousness.

Interpretation

Jesus does not come for baptism because He needs to repent of sin.

Rather, His baptism identifies Him publicly with the people He came to save and marks the beginning of His public ministry.

Jesus willingly submits to the Father's purpose and fulfills what is right in God's plan.

Teaching Truth:

Jesus identifies with sinners without Himself being sinful.

9. Matthew 3:16–17 — The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit

Observation

After Jesus is baptized, He comes up from the water.

The heavens open, the Spirit of God descends like a dove and rests upon Him, and a voice from heaven speaks.

The Father declares Jesus to be His beloved Son in whom He is well pleased.

Interpretation

This is one of the clearest scenes in Scripture where the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit are simultaneously distinguished.

Jesus the Son is being baptized. The Spirit descends upon Him. The Father speaks from heaven.

The passage does not present three gods. Scripture teaches one God, while here revealing personal distinction between Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

Biblical Truth:

At Jesus' baptism, the Father speaks, the Son stands in the water, and the Holy Spirit descends. The passage gives an important biblical foundation for understanding the triune nature of God.

10. The Father Identifies Jesus as His Beloved Son

Interpretation

The Father's declaration establishes Jesus' identity before His public ministry fully unfolds.

Jesus does not become the Son of God because of His ministry, miracles, popularity, or accomplishments.

The Father identifies Him as His beloved Son before Matthew records a single miracle performed during His public ministry.

Teaching Truth:

Jesus' identity comes before His public works. He ministers as the beloved Son of the Father.

What Matthew 3 Teaches Us About Jesus

Jesus is the Lord whose way John prepares.
John fulfills prophecy by preparing people for His coming.

Jesus is greater than John.
John openly confesses his unworthiness before Him.

Jesus gives the Holy Spirit.
His ministry reaches beyond outward cleansing to inward transformation.

Jesus is righteous.
He willingly fulfills the Father's will.

Jesus is the beloved Son of God.
The Father publicly declares His identity.

Three Questions for Reflection

1. Is there any area of my life where I need genuine repentance rather than merely regret?

2. What fruit in my life demonstrates that my relationship with God is producing real change?

3. What does Jesus' baptism teach me about the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit?

Chapter Summary

Matthew 3 prepares the way for the public ministry of Jesus. John the Baptist calls people to repentance, warns against empty religious confidence, and announces that Someone far greater than himself is coming.

When Jesus arrives, He willingly submits to baptism in fulfillment of the Father's purpose.

The chapter reaches its climax as the Holy Spirit descends upon Jesus and the Father declares Him to be His beloved Son.

Remember:

Prepare the heart through repentance, recognize the greatness of Jesus, and listen to the Father's testimony: Jesus is His beloved Son.
