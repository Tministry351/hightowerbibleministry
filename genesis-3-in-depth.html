Genesis In-Depth Study • Chapter 3

Genesis 3 — The Fall, Sin, Judgment, Grace, and the Promise of Redemption

What Happens When Humanity Rejects God's Word?

Genesis 3 records humanity's rebellion against God and reveals the devastating consequences of sin. Yet even in judgment, God seeks the guilty, exposes sin, provides covering, and announces a promise that points toward the ultimate defeat of the serpent.

Study Overview

Primary Passage: Genesis 3:1–24

Supporting Scriptures: Romans 5:12–19; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22; 1 Corinthians 15:45–49; 2 Corinthians 11:3; Revelation 12:9; Revelation 20:2.
Big Idea: Sin begins with rejecting God's truth and authority. It brings guilt, shame, broken relationships, suffering, separation, and death. Yet Genesis 3 also reveals that God does not abandon His redemptive purpose. Even while judging sin, He begins revealing His promise of victory.

HBM In-Depth Study Rhythm

Read it • Examine it • Understand it • Believe it • Live it

Genesis 3 is familiar to many people, but familiarity can cause us to move through it too quickly. We want to observe carefully how temptation develops, how humanity responds, how God confronts sin, and how grace appears even within judgment.

Genesis 3:1 — The Serpent Questions God's Word

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

The serpent is introduced as crafty and begins the conversation by questioning what God had commanded.

The attack begins not with an immediate command to rebel, but with a question concerning God's Word.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

The serpent's strategy introduces doubt. God's clear command from Genesis 2 is brought into question.

Temptation often begins when God's Word is treated as uncertain, unreasonable, or open to human revision.

The New Testament identifies the ancient serpent with Satan and the devil in passages such as Revelation 12:9.

Biblical Truth: One of the enemy's oldest strategies is to create doubt about what God has said.

Application

When God's Word is questioned, distorted, or minimized, believers must return to what Scripture actually says rather than allowing feelings, culture, or temptation to redefine God's truth.

Genesis 3:2–3 — The Woman Responds

Observation

The woman correctly recognizes that God permitted them to eat from the trees of the garden, while one tree was forbidden.

Her response, however, does not repeat God's command exactly as it appears in Genesis 2:16–17.

Interpretation

The conversation has now shifted attention from God's generous provision to the one prohibition.

Temptation can cause us to focus so strongly on what God has forbidden that we overlook everything He has graciously provided.

Teaching Truth: God's commands should always be understood in the context of His character, goodness, authority, and generous provision.

Genesis 3:4–5 — The Serpent Contradicts God

Observation

The serpent moves from questioning God's Word to directly contradicting God's warning.

He also suggests that God is withholding something desirable from humanity.

Interpretation

The temptation now attacks both God's truthfulness and God's goodness.

The serpent presents disobedience as a path to greater freedom, knowledge, and independence.

The underlying temptation is to distrust God and decide that human beings can determine for themselves what is good and evil.

Question God's Word

“Did God really say?”

Deny the Consequence

The serpent contradicts God's warning concerning death.

Question God's Goodness

God is portrayed as withholding something desirable.

Offer Independence

Humanity is tempted to define good and evil apart from God's authority.

Genesis 3:6 — Desire Becomes Disobedience

Observation

The woman sees that the tree appears good for food, attractive to the eyes, and desirable for gaining wisdom.

She takes the fruit and eats. She also gives some to her husband, who is with her, and he eats.

Interpretation

Sin moves from thought and desire into deliberate action.

Adam and Eve choose the serpent's word over God's Word.

The central issue is not simply eating fruit. The deeper issue is rebellion against God's authority and distrust of His command.

Biblical Truth: Sin is more than breaking a rule. Sin is rebellion against God, rejecting His authority, and choosing our will over His will.

Genesis 3:7 — Shame Enters Human Experience

Observation

After eating, the man and woman immediately become aware of their nakedness.

They attempt to cover themselves.

Interpretation

Genesis 2 ended with the man and woman naked and unashamed.

Genesis 3:7 presents the immediate contrast: innocence has been replaced by shame.

Their first response is not confession. Their first response is self-covering.

Teaching Truth: Sin creates guilt and shame, and human beings naturally attempt to cover the problem themselves.

Genesis 3:8–10 — Hiding From God

Observation

The man and woman hear the presence of the LORD God in the garden and hide among the trees.

God calls to the man, asking where he is.

Adam explains that he was afraid and hid because he was naked.

Interpretation

Sin has damaged humanity's relationship with God.

The people who previously lived openly before God now respond with fear and hiding.

God's question does not indicate that He lacks knowledge of Adam's location. The question draws Adam out and begins the confrontation with sin.

Biblical Truth: Sin causes people to hide from God, but God confronts sinners and calls them to account.

Genesis 3:11–13 — Excuses and Blame

Observation

God questions Adam concerning his disobedience.

Adam points to the woman and even refers to God as the One who gave her to him.

The woman then points to the serpent, saying that she was deceived.

Interpretation

Neither Adam nor Eve initially responds with a simple confession of personal responsibility.

Sin has now damaged not only the relationship with God, but also human relationships.

Blame becomes part of fallen human behavior.

Application Truth: Genuine repentance requires us to stop shifting responsibility and honestly acknowledge our own sin before God.

Genesis 3:14 — Judgment Upon the Serpent

Observation

God begins His judgments with the serpent.

The serpent is cursed and humiliated among the creatures.

Interpretation

The serpent appeared to offer advancement, wisdom, and freedom.

Instead, rebellion against God results in judgment.

Biblical Truth: Evil does not escape God's judgment. God remains sovereign even when rebellion enters His creation.

Genesis 3:15 — The Promise of Victory

Observation

God announces continuing hostility between the serpent and the woman, and between their offspring.

The passage describes a future conflict in which the serpent will strike the offspring, yet the serpent will ultimately be crushed.

Interpretation

This verse introduces a promise of future victory in the very chapter where humanity falls into sin.

Christians have historically recognized Genesis 3:15 as an early biblical promise that anticipates the ultimate defeat of Satan.

The full meaning of that victory becomes clearer as God's redemptive plan unfolds throughout Scripture and reaches fulfillment in Jesus Christ.

Christ-Centered Truth: Human rebellion does not defeat God's purposes. Even in the midst of judgment, Scripture begins pointing toward One who will ultimately defeat the serpent and the power of evil.

Genesis 3:16 — Sin Affects Human Relationships

Observation

The consequences of the Fall now affect childbirth and the relationship between husband and wife.

Interpretation

Genesis 2 presented marriage as a relationship of unity, companionship, and one flesh.

Genesis 3 shows that sin introduces pain, struggle, and disorder into human relationships.

The verse describes the effects of the Fall; it should not be read as though sinful domination is God's ideal for marriage.

Biblical Truth: Sin damages relationships that God originally created for unity, love, faithfulness, and mutual good.

Genesis 3:17–19 — The Ground Is Cursed

Observation

Because Adam listened to another voice instead of obeying God's command, the ground is cursed.

Work becomes painful and frustrating. The earth produces thorns and thistles.

Human life will now end in physical death: humanity returns to the dust from which it was formed.

Interpretation

Work itself was not the curse. Genesis 2:15 already showed Adam working before sin entered the world.

The Fall changes the experience of work. Productivity is now accompanied by frustration, difficulty, decay, and eventual death.

Biblical Truth: Sin affects not only the human heart but the entire human experience, including work, suffering, creation, and death.

Genesis 3:20 — Adam Names His Wife Eve

Observation

Adam names his wife Eve because she would become the mother of all living.

Interpretation

The verse comes after the announcement of death, yet it speaks about continuing life.

God's judgment is real, but humanity is not immediately destroyed. Human history will continue, and so will God's purposes.

Genesis 3:21 — God Provides a Covering

Observation

God makes garments for Adam and Eve and clothes them.

Interpretation

Earlier, Adam and Eve attempted to cover themselves.

Now God Himself provides a more adequate covering.

The verse should not be forced to say more than the text states, but it clearly demonstrates God's care for guilty human beings even while they experience the consequences of sin.

Grace in the Midst of Judgment: God judges sin, yet He does not cease showing mercy, provision, and care toward fallen humanity.

Genesis 3:22–24 — Expelled From the Garden

Observation

Adam and Eve are expelled from Eden.

Access to the tree of life is guarded.

Humanity can no longer remain in the garden as before.

Interpretation

Sin brings separation.

Humanity loses the privileged garden environment and direct access to the tree of life.

The chapter that began with temptation ends with humanity outside Eden.

Biblical Truth: Sin promises freedom, but ultimately brings bondage, loss, separation, and death.

What Genesis 3 Teaches Us About God

What God Does What It Reveals
God confronts Adam and Eve. God is holy and holds humanity accountable.
God judges the serpent. God opposes evil.
God announces consequences for sin. God is just and His warnings are true.
God announces future victory over the serpent. God's redemptive purpose continues.
God clothes Adam and Eve. God shows mercy and provision even toward the guilty.
God guards the tree of life. God remains sovereign over life and His creation.

Christ-Centered Connection

Jesus Christ and the Defeat of the Serpent

Genesis 3:15 begins a biblical expectation that evil will not have the final victory.

The rest of Scripture progressively reveals how God's redemptive plan will unfold.

Jesus Christ ultimately defeats the power of Satan, sin, and death through His saving work.

Adam and Christ

Romans 5:12–19 contrasts Adam with Jesus Christ.

Through Adam's disobedience, sin and death entered human history. Through the obedience of Jesus Christ, God provides righteousness and life for those who belong to Him.

First Corinthians 15 also contrasts Adam and Christ, especially in relation to death and resurrection.

Christ-Centered Truth: Genesis 3 explains why humanity needs a Savior.

Adam's rebellion brings sin, condemnation, and death into the human story.

The New Testament reveals Jesus Christ as the One who obeys where humanity failed, bears the consequence of sin, defeats death through His resurrection, and brings eternal life to those who trust in Him.

Personal Application — How Should This Change My Life?

  1. Do I trust what God has said, or do I allow culture, feelings, or personal desires to redefine His Word?
  2. Where am I tempted to believe that God's boundaries are keeping something good from me?
  3. When I sin, do I confess honestly, or do I hide, excuse, minimize, or blame others?
  4. Do I understand that sin affects my relationship with God, other people, and the way I experience life?
  5. Am I trying to cover my guilt through my own effort rather than coming honestly before God?
  6. How does God's promise of redemption strengthen my faith in Jesus Christ?
Personal Application Truth: Sin should never be treated lightly. It separates, deceives, damages, and destroys.

Yet God calls us to stop hiding, face the truth about our sin, and look to the redemption He provides through Jesus Christ.

Study and Reflection Questions

  1. What was the serpent's first approach toward God's command?
  2. How does temptation move from questioning God's Word to directly contradicting it?
  3. What does Genesis 3:6 reveal about desire and disobedience?
  4. What changed immediately after Adam and Eve sinned?
  5. Why did Adam and Eve hide from God?
  6. How did Adam and Eve respond when God confronted them?
  7. What hope appears in Genesis 3:15?
  8. How did sin affect marriage, work, creation, and human life?
  9. What does God's provision of clothing reveal about His character?
  10. How does Romans 5 contrast Adam with Jesus Christ?

Key Truths to Remember

Genesis 3:1 — Temptation begins by questioning God's Word.

Genesis 3:4–5 — The serpent attacks God's truthfulness and goodness.

Genesis 3:6 — Desire becomes deliberate disobedience.

Genesis 3:7 — Sin immediately produces shame.

Genesis 3:8–10 — Sin causes humanity to hide from God.

Genesis 3:12–13 — Fallen humanity shifts blame.

Genesis 3:15 — God announces a future victory over the serpent.

Genesis 3:19 — Physical death becomes part of the human condition.

Genesis 3:21 — God provides covering for Adam and Eve.

Genesis 3:23–24 — Humanity is expelled from Eden.

Preparing for the Rest of Genesis

Genesis 3 explains the central problem that shapes the rest of the biblical story: humanity is now fallen, sinful, separated from God, and subject to death.

Yet God's promise has also begun.

As Genesis continues, we will see sin spreading through humanity, but we will also see God preserving His purposes, extending grace, making covenant promises, and moving history toward redemption.

Watch the story from this point forward: Sin will spread.

Judgment will come.

Grace will continue.

God's promise of redemption will move forward.
