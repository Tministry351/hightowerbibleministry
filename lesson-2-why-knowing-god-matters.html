Lesson 2 — The Nature and Attributes of God

Part 1 — Why Knowing God Matters

Before we study what God is like, we must understand why knowing Him is more valuable than human wisdom, strength, possessions, or achievement.

Download / Print PDF

Lesson Foundation

Lesson question: Why is knowing God more valuable than anything we can achieve or possess?

Central truth: True wisdom, security, and spiritual maturity begin with knowing the living God through His revelation in Scripture and through Jesus Christ.

Primary passage: Jeremiah 9:23–24

Supporting Scriptures: John 17:3; Philippians 3:7–10; Colossians 1:9–10; Hosea 6:6; Psalm 19:1–4; 2 Timothy 3:16–17.

Why This Lesson Matters

Before studying God’s attributes, we must understand why knowing Him matters. Our view of God shapes our worship, decisions, priorities, and response to suffering. A distorted view of God can lead to misplaced confidence, fear, or presumption. Scripture calls us to know God as He reveals Himself, rather than as we imagine Him to be.

Biblical Background

Jeremiah ministered to Judah amid spiritual unfaithfulness and impending judgment. The people could not secure their future through human ability or resources. In Jeremiah 9, the LORD redirects their confidence toward knowing Him and understanding His character.

Jeremiah 9:23 — What We Must Not Boast In

“Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom, neither let the mighty man glory in his might, let not the rich man glory in his riches:” (KJV)

Human wisdom: Education, intelligence and experience are useful, but limited. Proverbs 3:5–6 calls us to trust the LORD rather than rely on our own understanding.

Human strength: Physical ability, influence and authority can accomplish much, but cannot guarantee security. Psalm 20:7 contrasts confidence in military power with confidence in the LORD.

Human riches: Possessions can meet needs and support ministry, but cannot purchase eternal life or secure our future. First Timothy 6:17 warns against trusting uncertain riches.

These gifts are not inherently evil. The problem arises when they become the foundation of our identity, confidence or worship.

Jeremiah 9:24 — What We Should Boast In

“But let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and knoweth me, that I am the LORD which exercise lovingkindness, judgment, and righteousness, in the earth: for in these things I delight, saith the LORD.” (KJV)

Lovingkindness: God’s steadfast love and mercy reveal His faithfulness to His people.

Judgment: God acts justly; He does not ignore wrongdoing or judge by favoritism.

Righteousness: God’s character and actions are consistently right. His standards do not depend on human approval.

Knowing God includes understanding what He delights in and allowing those truths to shape our conduct. We cannot claim to honor His character while deliberately rejecting His ways.

Key biblical truth: Our highest reason for confidence is not what we know, what we can do, or what we possess, but that we know the living God.

More Than Knowing About God

A person may know biblical facts without personally trusting God. Knowledge about God is important, but Scripture calls us beyond information toward faith, worship, obedience and fellowship. Hosea 6:6 confronts religious activity that is disconnected from steadfast love and knowledge of God.

This does not mean doctrine is unimportant. Sound doctrine helps us recognize the true God and distinguish His revelation from human speculation. A living relationship with God and accurate biblical teaching belong together.

John 17:3 — Knowing God and Eternal Life

Jesus connects eternal life with knowing the Father, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom He sent. Eternal life is more than endless existence: it includes fellowship with God through Christ.

John 14:6–9 teaches that Jesus is the way to the Father and reveals Him. We do not establish this relationship by our own wisdom, strength or achievements. Salvation is God’s gift received through faith (Ephesians 2:8–9).

Philippians 3:7–10 — The Surpassing Value of Christ

Paul had religious credentials and accomplishments, yet counted them loss compared with knowing Christ. His confidence rested not in his own righteousness but in the righteousness received through faith.

Paul desired to know Christ more deeply, including the power of His resurrection and fellowship in His sufferings. Knowing Christ is not merely an intellectual achievement or a promise of an easy life. It involves a growing walk of faith and obedience.

Colossians 1:9–10 — Growing in the Knowledge of God

Paul prays that believers would be filled with knowledge of God’s will, walk worthily of the Lord, bear fruit in every good work and increase in the knowledge of God. Spiritual growth connects understanding with conduct.

Key biblical truth: Knowing God is the foundation of Christian maturity, not a subject we eventually outgrow.

How God Makes Himself Known

Creation displays God’s glory and power (Psalm 19:1–4), but Scripture gives us the revealed truth needed to understand His character, purposes and the gospel. Jesus Christ reveals the Father (John 1:18; Hebrews 1:1–3). The Holy Spirit helps believers understand and receive spiritual truth (1 Corinthians 2:10–12).

Common Misunderstandings

Knowing God means knowing everything about Him. God is infinite; our knowledge is real but limited (Romans 11:33–36).

Knowing God guarantees prosperity. Jeremiah does not promise earthly wealth to the faithful. Our confidence rests in God even when circumstances are difficult.

Religious activity proves we know God. Attendance, service and biblical knowledge matter, but cannot replace faith and obedience (1 John 2:3–6).

Practical Christian Application

Read Scripture regularly with the purpose of learning who God is. Pray for understanding and compare what you hear with His Word. Examine where you place your confidence when facing difficulties. Practice lovingkindness, justice and righteousness in your relationships. Let your growing knowledge of God lead you to worship and obedience, not pride.

Questions for Deeper Understanding

1. Why does Jeremiah warn against boasting in wisdom, strength and riches?

2. What do lovingkindness, judgment and righteousness reveal about God?

3. How is knowing about God different from personally knowing Him?

4. What does John 17:3 teach about eternal life?

5. What did Paul value above his accomplishments?

6. Where do you tend to place your confidence, and how can Scripture reshape it?

Remember

Knowing God is more valuable than anything we possess or accomplish. As we grow in understanding His character, our faith, worship and daily lives should increasingly reflect His truth.

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for revealing Yourself through Your Word and through Jesus Christ. Help us to place our confidence in You rather than in our own abilities or possessions. Give us wisdom to understand Your character and strength to live in a way that honors You. May our knowledge of You grow into faithful obedience, love and worship. In Jesus’ name, amen.
