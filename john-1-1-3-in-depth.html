John 1:1-3 — The Eternal Word and Creation

A closer look at what John is saying about Jesus Christ before creation, His relationship with the Father, and His role in bringing all things into existence.

Why John Begins Here

John does not begin his Gospel with Bethlehem, Mary, Joseph, or even the ministry of John the Baptist. He begins before creation itself.

His opening words intentionally take us back to Genesis 1:1: “In the beginning…” Genesis tells us that God created the heavens and the earth. John tells us that when that beginning took place, the Word was already there.

Central truth: Jesus Christ did not begin to exist at Bethlehem. John presents Him as existing before the world was created.

John 1:1 — “In the beginning was the Word”

What does “the Word” mean?

John introduces Jesus as “the Word.” He does not immediately use the name Jesus because he first wants us to understand who this Person is. Later, in John 1:14, he tells us that “the Word became flesh.” Then in John 1:17 he names Jesus Christ directly.

The idea of “the Word” fits the biblical picture of God making Himself known. In Genesis, God speaks and creation responds. Throughout Scripture, God's Word reveals His will, communicates His truth, and accomplishes His purpose. John presents Jesus as God's supreme self-revelation — the One through whom God is made known personally.

What John wants us to see: Jesus is not merely someone who brought a message from God. He is the eternal Word through whom God has revealed Himself.

John 1:1 — “The Word was with God”

John now tells us that the Word was with God. This shows that the Word is personally distinguishable from the Father. The Word is not simply another name for God the Father.

There is relationship here. Before creation, the Word was already in fellowship with God. Later in John's Gospel, Jesus speaks about the glory He shared with the Father before the world existed (John 17:5).

Important distinction: The Father and the Son are not the same Person, yet their relationship existed before creation.

John 1:1 — “The Word was God”

John has just said that the Word was with God, showing personal distinction. Now he says that the Word was God, showing His divine nature.

John is therefore holding two truths together at the same time:

What John SaysWhat It Shows
The Word was with GodThe Word is personally distinct from the Father.
The Word was GodThe Word is fully divine.

John is not teaching that there are two separate Gods. He is showing us that the Word is personally distinct from the Father while sharing the divine identity. This becomes one of the foundational passages for the Christian understanding of the Trinity.

Careful conclusion: Jesus is not the Father, but John does not place Jesus outside the identity of God. He presents the Word as truly divine.

John 1:2 — “He was in the beginning with God”

Verse 2 may sound like John is simply repeating verse 1, but the repetition is important. Before John begins talking about creation, he reinforces the truth that the Word was already there with God.

John wants the reader to understand that the Word does not belong to the category of created things. The Word existed before creation and was already in relationship with God.

This agrees with Jesus' own words later in the Gospel when He speaks about the glory He had with the Father before the world existed (John 17:5).

John 1:3 — “All things were made through Him”

John now moves from who the Word is to what the Word did. He tells us that all things came into existence through Him.

Then John makes the statement even stronger: apart from Him, nothing came into existence that has come into existence.

The implication is significant: If everything that was created came into existence through the Word, then the Word Himself cannot be one of the created things.

John is presenting two categories:

The WordCreation
Already existing in the beginningEverything that came into existence
The One through whom creation cameThe things that were made

In other words, John places the Word on the Creator side, not the creature side.

John 1:4 — “In Him was life”

John now moves from creation itself to something even more personal. The Word is not only the One through whom life was created; John says that life is found in Him.

Throughout the Gospel of John, life becomes one of the major themes. Jesus does not merely point people toward life. He is presented as the source and giver of life.

Later Jesus says that just as the Father has life in Himself, He has granted the Son also to have life in Himself (John 5:26). Jesus also declares, “I am the resurrection and the life” (John 11:25), and “I am the way, and the truth, and the life” (John 14:6).

Important truth: The life John speaks about is more than physical existence. John will repeatedly connect Jesus with eternal life — life that comes from God and is found in His Son.

John 1:4 — “The life was the light of men”

John now connects two major themes in his Gospel: life and light.

Light reveals what darkness hides. It allows people to see what is true, exposes what is wrong, and shows the way forward. John uses this picture to help us understand what Jesus does spiritually.

Jesus reveals God, exposes the true condition of the human heart, and shows people the way to life. Later Jesus says, “I am the light of the world” (John 8:12).

What John is showing us: The One who gives life is also the One who brings God's truth and revelation into a dark world.

John 1:5 — “The light shines in the darkness”

John introduces another major contrast that will continue throughout his Gospel: light and darkness.

Darkness represents a world separated from God — marked by sin, spiritual blindness, unbelief, and resistance to truth. Yet John does not say the light once shone. He says the light shines.

The coming of Jesus brings God's truth into a world that desperately needs it. Darkness may oppose the light, but darkness does not have the power to extinguish it.

The encouragement: The darkness is real, but it is not stronger than the light. Jesus Christ remains the Light that darkness cannot overcome.

John 1:5 — “The darkness did not overcome it”

This statement can carry the idea that the darkness did not overcome, master, or defeat the light. Some translations also emphasize that the darkness did not fully comprehend or grasp it.

Both ideas fit themes that appear throughout John's Gospel. People living in spiritual darkness often fail to understand Jesus, and darkness also opposes Him. But neither misunderstanding nor opposition succeeds in defeating the Light.

This becomes especially powerful when we read the rest of John's Gospel. Jesus will be rejected and crucified, yet even death will not extinguish the Light. He rises again.

Putting it together: Darkness may resist Christ, reject Christ, and misunderstand Christ, but darkness cannot ultimately defeat Christ.

Putting John 1:4-5 Together

Verse 4 — Life is found in the Word.

Verse 4 — That life brings light to humanity.

Verse 5 — The Light shines into the darkness.

Verse 5 — The darkness cannot ultimately overcome the Light.

John is gradually building his picture of Jesus. The eternal Word through whom creation came into existence is also the source of life and the Light who enters a spiritually dark world.

How John 1 Fits With Genesis

The connection with Genesis is important. Genesis 1 begins with God creating. John 1 begins by telling us that the Word was already present when creation began.

Genesis repeatedly describes God creating by speaking: “And God said…” John shows that creation came into existence through the Word.

John is not rewriting Genesis. He is helping us understand more fully who was present and active in God's work of creation.

Why This Matters for Understanding Jesus

John wants us to understand who Jesus is before we begin reading about what Jesus does. The One who later walks through Galilee, speaks with Nicodemus, heals the sick, raises Lazarus, washes His disciples' feet, dies on the cross, and rises from the dead is the same One John introduces here as the eternal Word.

Then John 1:14 gives us the remarkable conclusion: the Word became flesh.

The One through whom creation came into existence entered His own creation and lived among us.

Important Cross-References

  • Genesis 1:1-3 — God creates in the beginning and speaks creation into existence.
  • John 1:14 — the Word became flesh and lived among us.
  • John 17:5 — Jesus speaks of the glory He shared with the Father before the world existed.
  • Colossians 1:16-17 — all things are described as created through Christ and for Him.
  • Hebrews 1:2-3 — God made the ages through the Son, who reveals His glory.

Putting the Truth Together

Verse 1 — The Word already existed in the beginning, was with God, and was God.

Verse 2 — The Word existed in relationship with God before creation.

Verse 3 — Everything that came into existence came into existence through the Word.

John is preparing us to understand Jesus Christ as far more than a teacher, prophet, or moral example. He presents Jesus as the eternal Word who existed before creation, shares the divine identity, and participated in bringing creation into existence.

Questions for Deeper Study

  1. Why do you think John begins his Gospel by taking us back to “the beginning”?
  2. What does “the Word was with God” tell us about the relationship between Jesus and the Father?
  3. What does “the Word was God” tell us about the identity of Jesus?
  4. Why is John 1:3 important when considering whether Jesus was created?
  5. How does John 1:14 help us understand who “the Word” is?
  6. How should knowing who Jesus is affect the way we listen to His words and respond to Him?
