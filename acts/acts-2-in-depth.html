Acts In-Depth Study • Chapter 2

Acts 2 — The Holy Spirit Comes With Power

Pentecost, Peter's Message, Repentance, and the Birth of the Early Church

Acts 2 records the fulfillment of Jesus' promise that His followers would receive power from the Holy Spirit. The Spirit comes at Pentecost, Peter boldly proclaims the risen Christ, thousands respond to the Gospel, and a new community of believers begins to grow.

Study Overview

Primary Passage: Acts 2:1–47

Supporting Scriptures: Acts 1:4–8; Joel 2:28–32; Psalm 16:8–11; Psalm 110:1; John 14:16–17; John 16:13–14.
Central Teaching Truth:

Acts 2 shows that the promised Holy Spirit empowers the followers of Jesus, the Gospel centers on the death and resurrection of Christ, genuine conviction leads to repentance, and Spirit-filled believers grow together through teaching, fellowship, prayer, worship, generosity, and witness.

1. Acts 2:1–4 — The Holy Spirit Comes at Pentecost

Observation

The believers are together in one place when the day of Pentecost arrives.

Suddenly, a sound like a powerful rushing wind fills the house.

Something resembling tongues of fire appears and rests upon them.

They are filled with the Holy Spirit and begin speaking in other languages as the Spirit gives them ability.

Interpretation

What Jesus promised in Acts 1 is now fulfilled.

The disciples had been told to wait for the Father's promise. They did not create this event, manufacture it, or determine when it would happen.

God acted according to His own promise.

The wind, fire, and Spirit-enabled speech demonstrate that something extraordinary and divinely initiated is taking place.

Teaching Truth:

God's mission begins with God's power. The disciples are equipped by the Holy Spirit to do what they could not accomplish through human strength alone.

2. Acts 2:5–13 — The Mighty Works of God Are Heard

Observation

Jewish people from many nations are in Jerusalem.

When they hear the sound, a crowd gathers.

They are amazed because each person hears the believers speaking in his own native language.

The content of their speech concerns the mighty works of God.

Some people are amazed and ask what the event means. Others mock the believers and accuse them of being drunk.

Interpretation

The Spirit enables the disciples to communicate God's works across language barriers.

The miracle points beyond itself. The purpose is not simply to produce amazement.

It prepares the crowd to hear the Gospel proclaimed.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit draws attention to the mighty works of God and prepares people to hear the message about Jesus Christ.

3. Acts 2:14–21 — Peter Explains What Is Happening

Observation

Peter stands with the eleven and addresses the crowd.

He first corrects the accusation that the believers are drunk.

Peter then turns to Scripture and quotes the prophet Joel.

Joel spoke of a time when God would pour out His Spirit upon people.

Interpretation

Peter does not explain Pentecost by personal opinion.

He interprets what is happening through the Word of God.

The coming of the Spirit demonstrates that God's promised work is unfolding.

Teaching Truth:

Spiritual experiences must be understood in light of Scripture, not merely by emotion, appearance, or human explanation.

4. Acts 2:22–24 — Jesus Was Crucified and Raised

Observation

Peter turns the crowd's attention directly to Jesus of Nazareth.

He reminds them that Jesus was publicly demonstrated through miracles, wonders, and signs.

Jesus was handed over according to God's determined purpose and foreknowledge.

Yet human beings were still responsible for crucifying Him.

God raised Jesus from the dead because death could not hold Him.

Interpretation

Peter holds together God's sovereign purpose and human responsibility.

The cross was not an accident. At the same time, those who rejected and crucified Jesus were morally accountable.

The resurrection demonstrates that death did not defeat Christ.

Central Gospel Truth:

Jesus was crucified, but God raised Him from the dead. The resurrection stands at the center of the apostolic message.

5. Acts 2:25–32 — David Spoke of the Resurrection

Observation

Peter quotes Psalm 16.

David spoke of one who would not be abandoned to the grave and whose body would not experience corruption.

Peter explains that David himself died and was buried.

Therefore, David was speaking prophetically about the Messiah.

Peter then declares that God raised Jesus, and the apostles are witnesses of His resurrection.

Interpretation

Peter again uses Scripture to explain the identity and work of Jesus.

The resurrection is not presented as a new idea disconnected from God's earlier revelation.

Peter shows that God's promises find their fulfillment in Christ.

Teaching Truth:

The resurrection of Jesus fulfills God's promises and confirms that He is the promised Messiah.

6. Acts 2:33–36 — Jesus Is Exalted as Lord and Christ

Observation

Peter explains that Jesus has been exalted to the right hand of God.

Jesus received the promised Holy Spirit from the Father and poured out what the crowd was now seeing and hearing.

Peter also quotes Psalm 110 and concludes by declaring that God has made this Jesus both Lord and Christ.

Interpretation

Pentecost points back to Jesus.

The coming of the Spirit is evidence that the risen Christ has been exalted.

Peter's message does not center on the experience of Pentecost itself. It centers on who Jesus is.

Teaching Truth:

The work of the Holy Spirit directs attention toward the exalted Christ.

7. Acts 2:37 — Conviction Reaches the Heart

Observation

After hearing Peter's message, the people are cut to the heart.

They ask Peter and the other apostles, “What shall we do?”

Interpretation

The crowd does more than hear information.

The truth about Jesus confronts them personally.

Genuine conviction recognizes that a response is necessary.

Teaching Truth:

Biblical preaching does not merely inform the mind. Through God's work, truth reaches the heart and calls for a response.

8. Acts 2:38–41 — Repentance and Response to the Gospel

Observation

Peter calls the people to repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ.

He speaks of forgiveness of sins and the gift of the Holy Spirit.

Peter continues urging them to respond to God's saving work.

Those who receive his message are baptized, and about three thousand people are added that day.

Interpretation

Repentance involves a genuine turning of heart and direction.

The people who had rejected Jesus are now called to recognize Him as Lord and Christ.

Baptism publicly identifies them with the Jesus they previously rejected.

Teaching Truth:

The Gospel calls people not merely to admire Jesus, but to repent, believe, and publicly identify with Him.

9. Acts 2:42 — The Priorities of the New Believers

Observation

The new believers devote themselves to four important practices:

the apostles' teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread, and prayer.

Interpretation

Conversion is not presented as the end of the Christian journey.

These believers immediately begin a life of learning, relationship, worship, and prayer.

Teaching Truth:

Healthy Christian growth requires continuing devotion to God's truth, fellowship with believers, worship, and prayer.

10. Acts 2:43–45 — Awe, Generosity, and Care

Observation

A sense of awe comes upon the believers.

Signs and wonders are performed through the apostles.

The believers share their possessions and provide for people according to their needs.

Interpretation

Their generosity flows from a transformed community.

They are not merely attending meetings together. Their relationship with Christ affects the way they treat one another.

Material possessions are no longer viewed only through the lens of personal ownership, but also through the needs of fellow believers.

Teaching Truth:

Genuine Christian fellowship expresses itself through practical care and generosity toward others.

11. Acts 2:46–47 — Worship, Fellowship, and Witness

Observation

The believers continue meeting together.

They share meals with glad and sincere hearts.

They praise God and enjoy favor with the people.

The Lord continues adding to their number those who are being saved.

Interpretation

The early church demonstrates both public and personal fellowship.

Their faith is visible in worship, relationships, generosity, and daily life.

Growth ultimately belongs to the Lord.

Teaching Truth:

The church faithfully teaches, worships, prays, serves, and witnesses, but God is the One who brings spiritual growth.

What Acts 2 Teaches Us About the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit fulfills God's promise.
Jesus had told the disciples to wait, and God fulfilled what He promised.

The Holy Spirit gives power for witness.
The fearful disciples become bold proclaimers of Christ.

The Holy Spirit points people toward Jesus.
Pentecost leads directly into the proclamation of the crucified and risen Christ.

The Holy Spirit forms a new community.
The believers begin living together in teaching, fellowship, prayer, worship, and generosity.

What Acts 2 Teaches Us About Gospel Preaching

Peter's message is rooted in Scripture.

It explains who Jesus is.

It speaks clearly about His crucifixion.

It proclaims His resurrection.

It announces His exaltation as Lord and Christ.

It calls the hearers to respond.

HBM Teaching Principle:

Biblical preaching should explain Scripture, exalt Jesus Christ, speak honestly about sin, proclaim the resurrection, and call people to respond to God's truth.

Seven Marks of a Growing Church

1. Biblical Teaching

The believers devote themselves to the apostles' teaching.

2. Fellowship

They intentionally share life together.

3. Prayer

Prayer remains central to the life of the church.

4. Worship

They praise God and remember Christ together.

5. Generosity

They respond to the practical needs of other believers.

6. Unity

Their shared faith produces a strong sense of community.

7. Witness

Their life together accompanies the continuing spread of the Gospel.

Three Questions for Reflection

1. When I read Acts 2, am I more impressed by the signs of Pentecost, or by the way the Holy Spirit directs attention toward Jesus Christ?

2. Which of the practices in Acts 2:42 needs greater attention in my own Christian life: biblical teaching, fellowship, worship, or prayer?

3. Does my relationship with Christ affect the way I use my time, possessions, relationships, and opportunities to serve others?

Chapter Summary

Acts 2 marks a major turning point in the mission Jesus began.

The Holy Spirit comes upon the believers, enabling them to proclaim God's mighty works.

Peter explains the event from Scripture and boldly proclaims that Jesus was crucified, raised from the dead, exalted, and made both Lord and Christ.

The people are convicted, many repent and are baptized, and about three thousand believers are added.

The chapter closes by showing the kind of community the Gospel produces: a people devoted to truth, fellowship, prayer, worship, generosity, and witness.

Remember:

The Spirit gives power, the Gospel proclaims Christ, repentance produces change, and transformed believers learn to live together as the people of God.

HBM In-Depth Study Rhythm

Read it • Examine it • Understand it • Believe it • Live it

Read Acts 2 again slowly. Watch how Peter moves from the event of Pentecost to the Scriptures, from the Scriptures to Jesus, from Jesus to repentance, and from repentance to a transformed community of believers.
