Psalms Overview — Worship, Prayer, Wisdom, and Trust in God

The book of Psalms gives God's people words for worship, prayer, praise, sorrow, repentance, thanksgiving, confidence, and hope. Through many different circumstances, the Psalms continually direct the heart toward God.

Psalms at a Glance

Psalms is a collection of 150 inspired songs, prayers, poems, and expressions of worship.

The Psalms reflect nearly every condition of the human heart: joy and grief, confidence and fear, victory and suffering, repentance and thanksgiving, confusion and hope.

Yet the central focus is not human emotion alone. The Psalms continually turn our attention toward the character, works, promises, justice, mercy, faithfulness, and kingship of God.

Central Truth: The Psalms teach God's people to bring every part of life before Him while continuing to trust, worship, obey, and hope in Him.

Book

Psalms

A collection of 150 inspired psalms arranged in five major books.

Authors

Several writers contributed to Psalms, including David, Asaph, the sons of Korah, Solomon, Moses, Heman, Ethan, and anonymous writers.

Main Emphasis

Worshiping and trusting God in every circumstance of life.

The Five Books of Psalms

The 150 Psalms are arranged into five major divisions. Each division ends with an expression of praise or blessing to God.

Book One — Psalms 1–41

Many of these Psalms are associated with David. Themes include trust, conflict, righteousness, suffering, prayer, and dependence upon God.

Book Two — Psalms 42–72

These Psalms frequently express longing for God, deliverance, worship, kingship, and hope in God's righteous rule.

Book Three — Psalms 73–89

This section wrestles with difficult questions involving suffering, injustice, national crisis, God's covenant, and His faithfulness.

Book Four — Psalms 90–106

This section strongly emphasizes God's eternal kingship, His sovereignty, His faithfulness, and His rule over all creation.

Book Five — Psalms 107–150

The final section contains strong themes of thanksgiving, God's Word, pilgrimage, deliverance, worship, and growing praise.

Major Types of Psalms

Psalms of Praise

These Psalms celebrate who God is and what He has done.

Psalms of Lament

These prayers bring suffering, confusion, injustice, grief, and fear honestly before God.

Biblical lament does not mean abandoning faith. It often expresses pain while continuing to look toward God in trust.

Psalms of Thanksgiving

These Psalms thank God for His deliverance, protection, provision, mercy, and answered prayer.

Wisdom Psalms

Wisdom Psalms contrast the way of righteousness with the way of wickedness and teach God's people how to live wisely.

Royal Psalms

These Psalms focus on Israel's king, God's rule, and the promises associated with His anointed king.

Penitential Psalms

These Psalms express confession, repentance, sorrow over sin, and dependence upon God's mercy.

Major Themes in Psalms

God Is King

The Psalms repeatedly proclaim God's sovereignty over creation, nations, history, and His people.

God Is Worthy of Worship

Praise is rooted in God's character and mighty works.

God Is Our Refuge

Many Psalms present God as refuge, fortress, shield, shepherd, and deliverer.

God Is Holy and Righteous

God judges wickedness and loves righteousness and justice.

God Is Merciful and Faithful

God's steadfast love, compassion, mercy, covenant faithfulness, and forgiveness appear throughout the Psalms.

God's Word Gives Wisdom

Psalms such as Psalm 1 and Psalm 119 emphasize delight in, meditation upon, and obedience to God's Word.

God Hears Prayer

The psalmists bring praise, confession, fear, sorrow, questions, thanksgiving, and requests directly to God.

Hope Must Rest in God

Circumstances change, enemies arise, strength fails, and suffering comes, yet the Psalms repeatedly call God's people back to confidence in Him.

Important Psalms to Know

Psalm 1 — Two Ways to Live

The righteous delight in God's instruction, while the way of the wicked ultimately perishes.

Psalm 2 — God's Anointed King

The nations rebel, but God establishes His King and calls people to take refuge in Him.

Psalm 23 — The LORD Is My Shepherd

God provides, guides, protects, and remains with His people.

Psalm 32 — Forgiveness and Confession

The Psalm celebrates the blessing of forgiveness and the importance of confessing sin.

Psalm 51 — Repentance

David seeks God's mercy, cleansing, renewal, and restoration after sin.

Psalm 91 — Trust in God's Protection

The Psalm expresses confidence in God as refuge and shelter.

Psalm 103 — Bless the LORD

God is praised for forgiveness, compassion, mercy, healing, and steadfast love.

Psalm 119 — God's Word

The longest Psalm celebrates the truth, wisdom, guidance, and value of God's Word.

Psalm 139 — God Knows Us Completely

God knows, sees, surrounds, and understands us completely.

Psalms 146–150 — A Final Call to Praise

The Psalter concludes with repeated calls for everything to praise the LORD.

Psalms and Jesus Christ

The New Testament repeatedly uses Psalms when explaining the identity, suffering, resurrection, kingship, and exaltation of Jesus Christ.

Certain Psalms receive especially important Christ-centered application in the New Testament.

Psalm 2

God's Anointed King and Son is opposed by the nations.

Psalm 22

Its language of suffering is connected in the New Testament with the crucifixion of Jesus.

Psalm 110

Jesus and the New Testament writers repeatedly use this Psalm in relation to the Messiah's lordship, kingship, and priesthood.

Christ-Centered Truth: We should first understand each Psalm in its own biblical setting.

We should also recognize how the New Testament uses the Psalms to help reveal the person and work of Jesus Christ.

How to Read the Psalms

Psalms should not be read merely as isolated quotations. Each Psalm has its own message, structure, setting, movement, and purpose.

  1. Read the entire Psalm before focusing on one verse.
  2. Notice who is speaking and what situation is described.
  3. Look for what the Psalm reveals about God.
  4. Observe movement in the Psalm — for example, from fear to trust, lament to praise, or confession to forgiveness.
  5. Consider the type of Psalm you are reading.
  6. Ask how the truth should shape your worship, prayer, faith, obedience, and daily life.
  7. Where Scripture itself makes the connection, consider how the Psalm helps us understand Christ and God's redemptive plan.

HBM Teaching Rhythm for Psalms

Read it • Understand it • Believe it • Live it

Read it: Read the entire Psalm carefully.

Understand it: Examine what the Psalm says, its setting, its movement, and what it reveals about God.

Believe it: Trust what Scripture reveals about God's character, promises, justice, mercy, and faithfulness.

Live it: Allow the truth of the Psalm to shape your worship, prayer, decisions, attitudes, and obedience.

Key Truths to Remember

Psalm 1: God's Word shapes the life of the person who delights in it.

Psalm 23: The LORD shepherds, guides, and cares for His people.

Psalm 46: God is our refuge and strength.

Psalm 51: Sinners need God's mercy, cleansing, and renewal.

Psalm 90: God is eternal, while human life is brief.

Psalm 103: God is compassionate, gracious, forgiving, and rich in steadfast love.

Psalm 119: God's Word gives truth, wisdom, guidance, and direction.

Psalm 150: The Psalter ends with a universal call to praise the LORD.
