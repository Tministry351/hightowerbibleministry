Handling Worry

Trusting God When Life Feels Uncertain

Worry often pulls our minds toward what might happen tomorrow. Scripture teaches us to bring our concerns to God, trust His character, and faithfully obey Him today.

Study Overview

Primary Scriptures: Matthew 6:25–34; Philippians 4:6–7; 1 Peter 5:7; Proverbs 3:5–6.
Central Teaching Truth:

Worry pulls our attention toward what might happen, but faith brings our attention back to who God is, what He has promised, and how He calls us to live today.

1. Worry Focuses on What We Cannot Control

Worry often begins with a real concern. There may be a health problem, financial pressure, a family issue, uncertainty about the future, or a decision we do not know how to make.

The concern itself may be legitimate. The problem comes when our minds become consumed by possibilities we cannot control.

Worry repeatedly asks, “What if?”

What if something goes wrong? What if I lose what I have? What if my prayer is not answered the way I hope? What if tomorrow becomes more difficult?

Teaching Truth:

Worry tries to carry tomorrow's burdens with today's strength.

2. Jesus Tells Us to Look at the Father's Care

Matthew 6:25–30

Jesus directs His disciples to look at the birds of the air and the flowers of the field.

Birds do not control tomorrow, yet the Father feeds them. Flowers do not manufacture their own beauty, yet God clothes them.

Jesus is not teaching laziness. Birds still gather food, and people are still responsible to work, plan, and make wise decisions.

His point is that God's children are not forgotten by their Father.

Teaching Truth:

The answer to worry begins by remembering the character and care of God.

3. Worry Cannot Control the Future

Matthew 6:27

Jesus asks whether worry can add anything meaningful to a person's life.

Worry feels productive because the mind is busy, but mental activity is not the same as solving a problem.

We may think about a situation repeatedly without gaining any greater ability to control it.

Biblical Truth:

Worry consumes energy without giving us authority over tomorrow.

4. Seek God's Kingdom First

Matthew 6:31–33

Jesus redirects the believer's attention.

Instead of allowing life to revolve around food, clothing, possessions, security, and future circumstances, He tells His followers to seek first God's kingdom and righteousness.

This changes the question.

Instead of asking only, “How do I make everything work out?” we begin asking, “How can I honor God in this situation?”

Teaching Truth:

Faith does not require us to know the outcome. It calls us to obey God in the present.

5. Deal With Today's Responsibilities Today

Matthew 6:34

Jesus teaches His disciples not to worry about tomorrow.

Tomorrow will bring responsibilities of its own. Today's responsibility is to faithfully walk with God today.

This does not mean Christians should never plan. Scripture encourages wisdom and preparation.

The difference is that planning prepares responsibly, while worry attempts to control what God has not placed in our hands.

Practical Truth:

Do what God has given you to do today, and leave tomorrow in His hands.

6. Bring Your Anxiety to God in Prayer

Philippians 4:6–7

Paul does not simply tell believers to stop being anxious.

He gives them something to do instead: pray.

Prayer takes the burden that is circling in our minds and deliberately brings it before God.

Paul also includes thanksgiving. Thanksgiving helps us remember God's past faithfulness while we are facing present uncertainty.

Teaching Truth:

Prayer turns worry into conversation with God.

7. God's Peace Does Not Depend on Knowing the Outcome

Philippians 4:7

Paul says that God's peace will guard the hearts and minds of believers in Christ Jesus.

Notice that the passage does not promise that every circumstance will immediately change.

God's peace can be present even while the answer is still unknown.

Biblical peace is not based upon having every problem solved. It is based upon knowing that our lives remain in God's hands.

Teaching Truth:

Peace is not always the absence of uncertainty. It is confidence in God within uncertainty.

8. Cast Your Cares Upon God

1 Peter 5:7

Peter tells believers to cast their cares upon God because He cares for them.

The foundation of this command is God's personal concern for His people.

We are not placing our burdens into the hands of someone who is distant or indifferent.

We bring them to a Father who knows us, sees us, and cares for us.

Biblical Truth:

We can release our burdens to God because His care for us is greater than our ability to carry them.

9. Trust God When You Do Not Understand

Proverbs 3:5–6

Scripture tells us to trust in the Lord with all our heart and not lean entirely upon our own understanding.

Human understanding is limited.

We see the present moment. God sees the whole picture.

We know some of the facts. God knows every detail.

Trust means acknowledging that God's wisdom is greater than our own.

Teaching Truth:

Faith does not require complete understanding before we trust God.

10. Concern and Worry Are Not the Same Thing

Scripture does not teach Christians to become careless or emotionally detached.

Concern may lead us to pray, seek counsel, make a plan, help someone, correct a problem, or take responsible action.

Worry often continues after we have done what we are able to do.

At that point, the question becomes whether we will trust God with what remains outside our control.

Important Difference:

Concern asks, “What faithful action should I take?” Worry asks, “How can I control what I cannot control?”

Practical Steps When Worry Begins

1. Identify the Concern

Name specifically what is troubling you. Vague fear often becomes clearer when we identify the actual concern.

2. Ask What Is Within Your Responsibility

Determine whether there is a wise, biblical action you should take.

3. Bring the Situation to God

Pray specifically rather than allowing the concern to circle repeatedly in your mind.

4. Return to Scripture

Remind yourself of what God has actually said rather than allowing fear to determine what is true.

5. Remember God's Past Faithfulness

Thanksgiving reminds us that the God we are trusting today is the same God who has carried us before.

6. Take the Next Faithful Step

You may not know the entire solution. Obey what you already know God wants you to do.

7. Release What You Cannot Control

Some situations remain unresolved. Faith entrusts those situations to God.

Three Questions for Reflection

1. What situation am I repeatedly trying to control through worry?

2. What truth about God's character do I need to remember in this situation?

3. What is one faithful step I can take today while trusting God with what I cannot control?

Closing Teaching

Worry does not disappear simply because we tell ourselves to stop worrying.

Scripture gives us a better direction.

Look at the Father's care. Seek His kingdom. Pray about your concerns. Remember His faithfulness. Trust His wisdom. Obey what He has placed before you today.

Remember:

We may not know what tomorrow will bring, but we know the God who will be there.

Closing Prayer

Heavenly Father, thank You for knowing every concern before we bring it to You.

Help us trust Your wisdom, remember Your faithfulness, and seek Your kingdom first.

Give us wisdom to handle the responsibilities You have placed before us, and faith to release the things we cannot control.

Guard our hearts and minds with Your peace as we learn to depend upon You.

In Jesus' name, Amen.
