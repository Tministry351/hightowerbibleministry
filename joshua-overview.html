Historical Books

Joshua

God Is Faithful to Keep His Promises

Book Overview

The Book of Joshua begins after the death of Moses.

Joshua is called by God to lead Israel into the land that God had promised to give His people.

The book records Israel crossing the Jordan River, entering the Promised Land, facing their enemies, receiving their inheritance, and being called to remain faithful to God.

Main Theme:

God faithfully keeps His promises, and His people are called to trust Him, obey His Word, and move forward with courage.

Where Joshua Fits in the Bible

Joshua is the first book in the Historical Books of the Old Testament.

It continues the story that began in the first five books of the Bible.

Israel had been delivered from Egypt, traveled through the wilderness, and now stood ready to enter the land God had promised.

Moses had died, and Joshua was now called to lead the people forward.

Major Sections of Joshua

Joshua 1–5 — Entering the Promised Land

Joshua is commissioned, Israel prepares to enter the land, Rahab helps the spies, and the people cross the Jordan River.

Joshua 6–12 — Conquering the Land

Israel begins taking possession of the land, including the fall of Jericho, the defeat at Ai, and later military victories.

Joshua 13–22 — Dividing and Settling the Land

The land is divided among the tribes of Israel, and the people begin settling in their inherited territories.

Joshua 23–24 — Joshua's Final Instructions

Joshua reminds the people of God's faithfulness and calls them to remain faithful and choose whom they will serve.

Major Themes

God's Faithfulness

God fulfills the promises He made to His people.

Courage Rooted in God's Presence

Joshua is repeatedly told to be strong and courageous because God is with him.

Obedience to God's Word

Joshua is instructed to meditate on God's Word and carefully obey it.

Faith Expressed Through Action

Israel had to move forward in response to what God commanded.

The Seriousness of Sin

The account of Achan shows that disobedience has serious consequences.

Remembering God's Faithfulness

The memorial stones in Joshua 4 were established so future generations would remember what God had done.

Choosing Whom You Will Serve

The book ends with Joshua calling Israel to wholehearted commitment to God.

Key Verses

Joshua 1:8–9

Joshua is commanded to remain grounded in God's Word, obey what God has revealed, and move forward with strength and courage.

Joshua 21:45

Scripture emphasizes that none of the good promises God made to Israel failed.

Joshua 24:15

Joshua calls the people to choose whom they will serve and declares that he and his household will serve the LORD.

Important People

Joshua

Joshua succeeds Moses and leads Israel into the Promised Land.

Rahab

Rahab helps the Israelite spies and demonstrates faith in the God of Israel.

Caleb

Caleb remains faithful and later receives the inheritance God had promised him.

Achan

Achan's disobedience demonstrates the seriousness of sin and its consequences.

Practical Truth for Believers

God's promises call for a response.

We are called to trust Him, obey His Word, remember His faithfulness, and move forward with courage.

Joshua teaches us that faith is not passive.

God's people must trust Him and respond in obedience.

Difficult circumstances do not mean God has abandoned His promises.

His faithfulness gives us courage to keep moving forward.
