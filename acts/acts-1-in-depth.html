Acts In-Depth Study • Chapter 1

Acts 1 — Empowered to Be Witnesses

The Risen Christ, the Promise of the Holy Spirit, and the Beginning of the Church's Mission

Acts 1 records the final instructions of the risen Jesus before His ascension and prepares the disciples for the mission that will unfold throughout the book of Acts.

Study Overview

Primary Passage: Acts 1:1–26

Supporting Scriptures: Luke 24:44–49; Matthew 28:18–20; John 14:16–17; John 16:13–14; Zechariah 4:6.
Central Teaching Truth:

Acts 1 teaches that the risen Jesus prepares His disciples for mission, promises the power of the Holy Spirit, commissions them to be His witnesses, ascends to heaven, and leads the early believers to wait in prayer, unity, and obedience.

1. Acts 1:1–3 — The Risen Jesus Continues His Work

Observation

Luke begins Acts by referring back to his first account, which recorded what Jesus began to do and teach.

After His suffering, Jesus presented Himself alive to His disciples with many convincing proofs.

He appeared to them over a period of forty days and continued teaching them about the kingdom of God.

Interpretation

The resurrection was not presented to the disciples as a vague spiritual idea. Jesus repeatedly appeared to them and gave them convincing evidence that He was alive.

Luke's wording also shows that the Gospel account described what Jesus began to do and teach. Acts records the continuation of His work through His followers by the power of the Holy Spirit.

Teaching Truth:

The ministry of Jesus did not end with His resurrection. The risen Christ continues His work through Spirit-empowered witnesses.

2. Acts 1:4–5 — Jesus Commands the Disciples to Wait

Observation

Jesus tells the disciples not to leave Jerusalem.

They are commanded to wait for the Father's promised gift.

John baptized with water, but Jesus tells them that they will soon be baptized with the Holy Spirit.

Interpretation

The disciples had already spent years with Jesus. They had heard His teaching, seen His miracles, witnessed His death, and now encountered Him risen from the dead.

Yet Jesus still told them not to begin the mission in their own strength.

Knowledge, experience, and enthusiasm were not enough. They needed the power God promised through the Holy Spirit.

Teaching Truth:

God's work must be done in God's power, not merely through human ability.

Application

There are times when obedience means moving, and there are times when obedience means waiting.

Waiting on God is not inactivity when we are waiting according to His command.

3. Acts 1:6–8 — Power to Be Witnesses

Observation

The disciples ask Jesus whether He is now going to restore the kingdom to Israel.

Jesus tells them that the times and seasons are under the Father's authority.

He then redirects their attention to the mission before them.

They will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon them, and they will be His witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.

Interpretation

The disciples are still thinking about the restoration of Israel.

Jesus does not tell them that God's kingdom purposes are unimportant. Instead, He tells them that the timing belongs to the Father's authority.

Their immediate responsibility is not to calculate God's timetable. Their responsibility is to receive the Spirit's power and become faithful witnesses.

Biblical Truth:

Jesus redirects His followers from speculation about God's timing toward participation in God's mission.

Application

Christians can become so focused on future events that we neglect the work Jesus has already given us.

We are called to faithfully represent Christ where we are and wherever He sends us.

4. The Holy Spirit Gives Power for Witness

Acts 1:8

Jesus connects the coming of the Holy Spirit directly with the disciples' ability to be His witnesses.

The power of the Spirit is not given merely for personal experience. It equips believers to testify about Jesus Christ.

Their witness will begin locally in Jerusalem, move outward into Judea and Samaria, and eventually extend throughout the world.

Teaching Truth:

The Holy Spirit empowers believers to make Christ known.

Acts itself follows this pattern. The Gospel moves outward from Jerusalem toward the nations.

5. Acts 1:9–11 — Jesus Ascends to Heaven

Observation

After speaking to the disciples, Jesus is taken up before their eyes.

A cloud hides Him from their sight.

While the disciples continue looking upward, two men in white clothing appear beside them.

They explain that this same Jesus who has been taken into heaven will return in the same manner in which they saw Him go.

Interpretation

The ascension marks the end of Jesus' visible earthly ministry and prepares the way for the Spirit-empowered mission of His disciples.

The disciples had witnessed many miracles, but seeing Jesus ascend would have been an especially powerful confirmation of His authority and glory.

The angels also redirect their attention. They are not called merely to stand staring into heaven.

Jesus will return, but until then they have work to do.

Teaching Truth:

Christ's return is certain, but His promise of return should motivate faithful service, not passive waiting.

6. Acts 1:12–14 — Waiting in Prayer and Unity

Observation

The disciples return to Jerusalem after Jesus ascends.

They gather together with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus, and Jesus' brothers.

They devote themselves continually to prayer.

Interpretation

Jesus had commanded them to wait, but their waiting was not careless or inactive.

They waited together. They waited in unity. They waited in prayer.

Jesus had repeatedly demonstrated the importance of prayer during His ministry, and now His followers continue that pattern.

Teaching Truth:

Prayer and unity prepare God's people to carry out the mission He gives them.

Application

Before attempting great work for God, believers should learn to seek Him together.

Ministry should grow out of dependence upon God, not merely activity.

7. Acts 1:15–20 — Peter Addresses the Loss of Judas

Observation

Peter stands among the believers and speaks about Judas.

Judas had been numbered among the Twelve and had shared in their ministry, but he betrayed Jesus and abandoned his place.

Peter understands these events in light of Scripture.

Interpretation

The early believers do not ignore the painful failure of Judas.

They face the reality of what happened while also trusting that God's purposes have not failed.

Peter looks to Scripture to understand how the group should move forward.

Teaching Truth:

Human failure does not cancel God's purposes. God's people must respond to painful circumstances according to His Word.

8. Acts 1:21–22 — The Need for a Twelfth Witness

Observation

Peter explains that the replacement for Judas must be someone who had accompanied the disciples during Jesus' earthly ministry, from John's baptism through the ascension.

That person would become a witness with them concerning the resurrection of Jesus.

Interpretation

The emphasis is again upon witness.

The apostles are not merely forming an organizational leadership team.

They are establishing a group of witnesses who can testify personally to the life, ministry, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus.

Teaching Truth:

The resurrection of Jesus stands at the center of the apostolic witness.

9. Acts 1:23–26 — Matthias Is Chosen

Observation

Two qualified men are put forward: Joseph called Barsabbas, also known as Justus, and Matthias.

The believers pray, acknowledging that the Lord knows every heart.

They ask God to show which man He has chosen to take Judas' place.

Lots are cast, and Matthias is numbered with the eleven apostles.

Interpretation

The believers recognize that human beings can evaluate outward qualifications, but God alone fully knows the heart.

Their decision-making process includes biblical qualifications, prayer, and dependence upon God's direction.

The selection of Matthias restores the number of the apostolic group to twelve before the coming of the Holy Spirit in Acts 2.

Teaching Truth:

God's people should make important decisions with biblical wisdom, prayer, and dependence upon the Lord who knows the heart.

What Acts 1 Teaches Us About Jesus

Jesus is risen.
He presented Himself alive with convincing proofs.

Jesus is still teaching and directing His followers.
He prepares His disciples before sending them.

Jesus gives the promise of the Holy Spirit.
His mission is carried out through divine power.

Jesus commissions His followers to be witnesses.
The Gospel is intended to move outward to the world.

Jesus ascended to heaven.
His earthly ministry did not end in defeat, but in exaltation.

Jesus will return.
His followers live and serve in expectation of His promised return.

What Acts 1 Teaches Us About the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is not presented as merely an influence or emotion.

Jesus promises that the Spirit will come upon His followers and give them power for their mission.

The disciples are specifically told not to begin their work until they receive this promised power.

Biblical Truth:

Christian ministry depends upon the presence and power of the Holy Spirit.

Four Marks of the Early Believers

1. Obedience

They return to Jerusalem because Jesus told them to wait there.

2. Unity

They gather together rather than separating after Jesus' ascension.

3. Prayer

They devote themselves continually to prayer.

4. Dependence on Scripture

Peter looks to God's Word as the believers decide how to respond to the loss of Judas.

Three Questions for Reflection

1. Am I more concerned with knowing God's timetable, or with faithfully doing the work Jesus has already given me?

2. Do prayer and dependence upon the Holy Spirit come before my ministry activity, or only after I encounter difficulty?

3. Where has Jesus placed me right now to be a faithful witness for Him?

Chapter Summary

Acts 1 stands between the earthly ministry of Jesus and the Spirit-empowered mission that begins in Acts 2.

The risen Jesus proves that He is alive, teaches His disciples, commands them to wait, promises the Holy Spirit, and commissions them to become His witnesses.

After Jesus ascends, the disciples return to Jerusalem, gather in unity, devote themselves to prayer, and use Scripture to guide them as Matthias is chosen to replace Judas.

Remember:

Jesus gives the mission, the Holy Spirit gives the power, and believers are called to respond with obedience, prayer, unity, and faithful witness.
