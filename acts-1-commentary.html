Bible Book Commentary

Acts 1 — Witnesses for Christ

Christ Gives the Mission — The Holy Spirit Gives the Power

Central Theme

Jesus prepared His disciples to continue His work after His ascension.

He commanded them to wait for the power of the Holy Spirit and then become His witnesses from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth.

The mission of the church does not depend upon human strength alone.

Christ gives the mission, the Holy Spirit gives the power, and believers are called to faithfully carry the message of Jesus Christ to others.

Acts 1:1–3 — Jesus Continues His Work

Luke begins Acts by referring to his first account, the Gospel of Luke.

He explains that his earlier writing recorded what Jesus “began both to do and teach.”

That word began is important.

Jesus completed His earthly ministry, died for our sins, rose from the dead, and returned to the Father.

But His work on earth did not stop.

The Book of Acts shows Jesus continuing His work through His followers by the power of the Holy Spirit.

After His resurrection, Jesus appeared to His disciples over a period of forty days.

He gave them many convincing proofs that He was alive and continued teaching them about the kingdom of God.

The resurrection was not simply something the disciples were told to believe.

They personally encountered the risen Christ.

Key Truth:

Christianity is built upon the reality of the risen Jesus Christ.

The disciples were not being sent out to proclaim an idea.

They were being sent to testify about Someone they had seen alive after His death.

Application:

Our faith rests upon Jesus Christ — His life, death, resurrection, and continuing work.

As believers, we are continuing a mission that Jesus began.

Acts 1:4–5 — Wait for the Promise of the Father

Jesus commanded the disciples not to leave Jerusalem.

They were to wait for the promise of the Father — the coming of the Holy Spirit.

Jesus reminded them that John baptized with water, but they would soon be baptized with the Holy Spirit.

The disciples had already spent years with Jesus.

They had heard His teaching.

They had witnessed His miracles.

They had seen Him crucified.

They had seen Him alive after His resurrection.

Yet Jesus still told them:

Wait.

Knowledge and experience alone were not enough to accomplish the mission Jesus was giving them.

They needed the power God would provide through the Holy Spirit.

Key Truth:

God's work must be accomplished through God's power.

Application:

Christians can have knowledge, experience, ability, and good intentions, but spiritual ministry cannot be successfully accomplished by human strength alone.

We need to depend upon the Holy Spirit.

Sometimes obedience also means waiting until God prepares us for what He has called us to do.

Acts 1:6–8 — The Mission of the Church

The disciples asked Jesus:

“Lord, wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel?”

Their attention was still focused upon Israel and the restoration of the kingdom.

Jesus did not give them the timetable they wanted.

Instead, He told them that the times and seasons belonged to the Father's authority.

Then Jesus redirected their attention toward the mission they had been given.

“But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me...”

Their primary responsibility was not to know God's timetable.

Their responsibility was to be faithful witnesses.

Jesus described how that witness would spread:

Jerusalem
Judea
Samaria
The uttermost part of the earth

The gospel would begin where they were and continue outward.

Key Truth:

Believers are not called to know everything God has chosen to keep within His authority.

We are called to faithfully carry out the mission He has clearly given us.

Application:

Christians can become so focused upon prophecy, dates, future events, or unanswered questions that we neglect the work God has already told us to do.

Jesus has given His followers a clear mission:

Be My witnesses.

Our witness begins where God has placed us — with our families, churches, communities, workplaces, and relationships — and continues outward as God gives opportunity.

Acts 1:8 — The Power of the Holy Spirit

Jesus did not simply command His disciples to witness.

He promised that they would receive power.

The disciples were not expected to accomplish Christ's mission using only their own courage or ability.

The Holy Spirit would empower them.

This becomes one of the major themes throughout the Book of Acts.

Ordinary people would accomplish extraordinary things because God was working through them.

Key Truth:

The mission belongs to Christ, and the power to accomplish that mission comes from the Holy Spirit.

Application:

Serving Christ requires dependence upon God.

We prepare. We study Scripture. We pray. We speak. We serve.

But we must remember that spiritual results belong to God.

Acts 1:9–11 — Jesus Ascends Into Heaven

After speaking to His disciples, Jesus was taken up before their eyes.

A cloud received Him out of their sight.

The disciples stood looking upward as Jesus ascended.

Then two men dressed in white appeared and asked:

“Why stand ye gazing up into heaven?”

They told the disciples that the same Jesus who had gone into heaven would return in the same manner.

The ascension confirmed that Jesus' earthly ministry had entered a new stage.

He returned to the Father, but His disciples now had work to do.

They were also given a promise:

Jesus will return.

Key Truth:

Jesus Christ ascended to heaven, and He will return.

Application:

The return of Christ should not cause believers to become inactive.

It should motivate us to faithfully accomplish what Jesus has called us to do while we are waiting for Him.

We should live with expectation — but also with responsibility.

Acts 1:12–14 — The Disciples Gather and Pray

After the ascension, the disciples returned to Jerusalem.

They went to the upper room and gathered together.

The group included the apostles, several women, Mary the mother of Jesus, and Jesus' brothers.

Verse 14 gives us an important description:

“These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication.”

Jesus had told them to wait.

So they waited together.

And while they waited, they prayed.

They were united.

They were obedient.

They were dependent upon God.

Prayer had always been an important part of Jesus' ministry.

Now the disciples were learning that prayer would also be essential to their mission.

Key Truth:

Waiting upon God should never mean spiritual inactivity.

The disciples waited through prayer, unity, and obedience.

Application:

Prayer should not simply be something Christians do when everything else fails.

Prayer should be part of the foundation of everything we do.

Churches, ministries, leaders, and individual believers need prayer because God's work requires God's direction and power.

Acts 1:15–20 — Peter Addresses the Loss of Judas

During this period of waiting, Peter stood among approximately 120 believers.

He addressed what had happened with Judas.

Judas had been chosen as one of the twelve but had betrayed Jesus.

Peter understood the events surrounding Judas in light of Scripture.

The betrayal had not caught God by surprise.

Judas was responsible for his actions, but God's purposes were still moving forward.

Peter cited Scripture to explain that Judas' place of service would need to be filled.

Key Truth:

Human failure does not stop God's plan.

Application:

People may fail.

Leaders may fail.

Circumstances may change.

But God's purposes continue.

When disappointment or failure occurs within the church, believers must continue looking to Scripture and trusting God.

Acts 1:21–26 — Matthias Is Chosen

Peter explained that the person replacing Judas needed to be someone who had accompanied them during Jesus' ministry and could serve as a witness to His resurrection.

Two men were put forward:

Joseph called Barsabas, also known as Justus

Matthias

Before making the final decision, the believers prayed.

“Thou, Lord, which knowest the hearts of all men, shew whether of these two thou hast chosen.”

They understood something important:

God knows the heart.

They could evaluate a person's outward qualifications, but only God could fully know the person.

After praying, they cast lots, and Matthias was numbered with the eleven apostles.

Key Truth:

Spiritual leadership requires both biblical qualification and dependence upon God's direction.

Application:

When churches select leaders, teachers, servants, or ministry workers, decisions should not be based only upon personality, popularity, or outward ability.

Character matters.

Faithfulness matters.

Spiritual maturity matters.

Prayer matters.

And ultimately, God knows the heart better than we do.

Major Lessons From Acts 1

1. Jesus Is Alive

The entire mission of the early church was built upon the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

2. Jesus Continues His Work Through His People

The Gospel of Luke records what Jesus began to do and teach.

Acts records the continuation of His work through His followers.

3. The Holy Spirit Gives Power for the Mission

Jesus did not send His disciples out in their own strength.

4. Every Believer Is Called to Be a Witness

Acts 1:8 establishes the direction of the Book of Acts.

The message of Christ moves outward.

5. We Do Not Need to Know Everything God Knows

Some things belong to the Father's authority.

Our responsibility is to obey what He has made clear.

6. Jesus Will Return

The same Jesus who ascended into heaven will return.

7. Prayer and Unity Are Essential

The disciples prayed together while waiting for God's promise.

8. Leadership Matters

The replacement of Judas shows that leadership in God's work should be approached seriously.

Acts 1 and the Church Today

Acts 1 gives us a picture of what Christ expects His church to be.

Centered on Jesus Christ.

Dependent upon the Holy Spirit.

Grounded in Scripture.

Committed to prayer.

United in purpose.

Faithful in witness.

Expecting Christ's return.

Pastors and leaders should faithfully guide and encourage God's people.

Believers should support, encourage, and pray for their pastors and leaders.

Together, the entire body should work to accomplish the mission Christ has given the church.

The mission does not belong to one pastor, teacher, missionary, or ministry.

Every believer has a part.

Questions for Reflection

1. What evidence does Acts 1 give that Jesus truly rose from the dead?

2. Why did Jesus command the disciples to wait before beginning their mission?

3. What does Acts 1:8 teach about the relationship between the Holy Spirit and Christian witness?

4. Why did Jesus redirect the disciples when they asked about restoring the kingdom to Israel?

5. Where should our personal witness for Christ begin?

6. What does the ascension teach us about Jesus?

7. How should the promise of Christ's return affect the way we live today?

8. What can we learn from the disciples praying together while they waited?

9. What does the selection of Matthias teach us about spiritual leadership?

10. What part of Acts 1 most challenges you personally?

Practical Application

Read Acts 1 again slowly.

As you read, ask yourself:

Where has God placed me to be a witness for Jesus Christ?

Write down the names of two or three people God has already placed within your life.

Pray for them.

Ask God to give you an opportunity to demonstrate Christ through your life and, when appropriate, share the truth of Jesus with them.

Do not depend only upon your own ability.

Pray for the Holy Spirit to give you wisdom, courage, and the right opportunity.

Remember

Jesus did not leave His disciples without direction.

He gave them a mission.

He promised them power.

He gave them a message.

And He promised that He would return.

Christ gives the mission.

The Holy Spirit gives the power.

Believers give the witness.

And God receives the glory.
