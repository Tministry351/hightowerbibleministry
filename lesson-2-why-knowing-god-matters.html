Lesson 2 — The Nature and Attributes of God

Part 1 — Why Knowing God Matters

Before we study what God is like, we must understand why knowing Him is more valuable than human wisdom, strength, possessions, or achievement.

Primary Scriptures

Jeremiah 9:23–24

God contrasts human boasting in wisdom, strength, and riches with the far greater privilege of understanding and knowing Him.

Supporting Scriptures:

John 17:3
Hosea 6:3
Psalm 46:10
Philippians 3:8–10

Big Idea

The greatest pursuit of human life is not merely gaining knowledge about God, but truly knowing Him as He has revealed Himself.

Our understanding of God shapes how we understand ourselves, how we interpret life, how we respond to suffering, how we make decisions, how we worship, and how we relate to other people.

A weak or distorted understanding of God will eventually produce weak or distorted beliefs about faith and Christian living.

Why Begin With Knowing God?

Biblical doctrine is not intended merely to fill our minds with religious information.

Doctrine teaches us what is true about God, His character, His works, His purposes, and His relationship with humanity.

The more accurately we know God, the more clearly we can understand His Word and respond to Him faithfully.

Teaching Truth: What we believe about God affects how we live before God.

Jeremiah 9:23–24 — The Context

Jeremiah ministered during a time when the people of Judah had turned away from God.

They trusted in human wisdom, political strength, wealth, religious traditions, and their own sense of security.

Yet those things could not protect them from the consequences of rebellion against God.

Against that background, Jeremiah declares that the proper reason for confidence is not what a person possesses or accomplishes, but that the person understands and knows the LORD.

Observation — What Does the Text Say?

Jeremiah identifies three things people are often tempted to boast in:

Human Wisdom

Intelligence, education, knowledge, and human reasoning.

Human Strength

Physical ability, influence, power, position, and personal capability.

Human Riches

Money, possessions, resources, success, and financial security.

None of these things is necessarily evil. The problem comes when they become the foundation of our confidence or the source of our identity.

Jeremiah then identifies something far greater: understanding and knowing God.

Interpretation — What Does It Mean?

Jeremiah is not teaching that wisdom, strength, and resources have no value.

Scripture recognizes all of these as things that can be used responsibly.

The warning concerns boasting — placing our confidence, identity, security, or ultimate trust in them.

Human wisdom is limited. Human strength eventually fails. Material wealth can disappear.

God alone remains worthy of our ultimate confidence.

Teaching Sentence: The highest ground for confidence is not what we know, what we can do, or what we possess, but that we know the living God.

What Does It Mean to Know God?

Scripture distinguishes between knowing information about God and genuinely knowing Him.

A person can learn biblical facts, memorize verses, understand theological vocabulary, and still fail to walk in a real relationship with God.

Knowing God includes understanding what He has revealed about Himself, trusting Him, responding to Him, obeying Him, and living in relationship with Him.

Important Distinction: Knowing about God involves information.

Knowing God involves truth, faith, relationship, trust, obedience, and growing fellowship with Him.

What God Reveals About Himself

Jeremiah 9 does not simply say that we should know that God exists.

God identifies qualities that reveal His character.

Steadfast Love

God demonstrates faithful, covenant love.

His love is not merely sentimental. It is faithful, purposeful, and consistent with His character.

Justice

God always acts according to what is right.

He does not ignore evil, distort truth, or judge unfairly.

Righteousness

God Himself is the standard of what is right.

His character, judgments, actions, and purposes are morally perfect.

Biblical Truth: To know God rightly is to understand Him according to the character He has revealed in Scripture, not according to our imagination or preferences.

Why God's Attributes Matter

When Scripture teaches us that God is eternal, all-knowing, all-powerful, holy, righteous, loving, just, faithful, and unchanging, these are not merely theological definitions.

Each truth affects the way believers understand and trust Him.

Because God is eternal, He is never limited by time.

Because God is all-knowing, nothing is hidden from Him.

Because God is all-powerful, nothing can prevent Him from accomplishing His purposes.

Because God is holy, He is completely separate from evil.

Because God is faithful, His promises can be trusted.

Because God is loving, His people can trust His heart even when they do not understand His ways.

Knowing God Changes the Way We Live

The purpose of studying God's attributes is not simply to answer theological questions.

Knowing God should transform how we live.

Knowing God Changes Our Worship

The more clearly we understand who God is, the more deeply we recognize that He alone deserves worship.

Knowing God Changes Our Trust

Faith becomes stronger as we learn that God's character is trustworthy.

Knowing God Changes Our Obedience

God's commands are not arbitrary rules. They flow from His holy, righteous, and loving character.

Knowing God Changes Our View of Ourselves

We stop making ourselves the center of life and begin understanding ourselves in relationship to our Creator.

Knowing God Changes Our Relationships

As we understand God's love, justice, mercy, forgiveness, and righteousness, those truths should influence the way we treat other people.

Knowing God Through Jesus Christ

The New Testament teaches that knowing God is inseparably connected with knowing Jesus Christ.

Jesus reveals the Father, makes salvation possible, and brings believers into relationship with God.

In John 17:3, Jesus connects eternal life with knowing the true God and Jesus Christ whom He sent.

Therefore, knowing God is not merely an intellectual pursuit.

It is a relationship made possible through Jesus Christ and experienced through faith, the Word of God, prayer, obedience, and the work of the Holy Spirit.

Christ-Centered Truth: Jesus Christ does not merely teach us facts about God. Through Him we are brought into relationship with God and learn what the Father is like.

Personal Application

Consider these questions carefully:

  1. What do I tend to place my confidence in — wisdom, ability, money, position, experience, or God?
  2. Do I primarily know facts about God, or am I growing in a personal relationship with Him?
  3. Which truth about God's character do I most need to trust right now?
  4. Does my understanding of God come primarily from Scripture, or from my feelings, experiences, traditions, or assumptions?
  5. How is knowing God changing the way I worship, obey, make decisions, and treat others?

Review

Before moving to Part 2, make sure you can explain these truths:

1. Human wisdom, strength, and riches cannot provide ultimate security.

2. Knowing God is more important than merely knowing information about Him.

3. God reveals His character through Scripture.

4. God's attributes help us understand who He is and how we should respond to Him.

5. Knowing God should produce worship, trust, obedience, and transformed living.

6. We come to know God personally through Jesus Christ.

Memory Passage

Jeremiah 9:23–24

As you review this passage, focus especially on the contrast between boasting in human ability and finding our confidence in understanding and knowing the LORD.

Biblical doctrine should lead us beyond information into deeper faith, greater obedience, and a growing relationship with God.
