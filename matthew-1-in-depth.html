Matthew In-Depth Study • Chapter 1

Matthew 1 — The Promised Messiah Comes

Jesus Christ, Son of David, Son of Abraham, Savior, and Immanuel

Matthew begins by grounding the coming of Jesus Christ in God's covenant promises and in real human history. The chapter reveals Jesus as the promised Messiah, the rightful King, the Savior from sin, and Immanuel — God with us.

Study Overview

Primary Passage: Matthew 1:1–25

Supporting Scriptures: Genesis 12:1–3; 2 Samuel 7:12–16; Isaiah 7:14; Luke 1:26–35; John 1:14; Galatians 4:4–5.
Central Teaching Truth:

Matthew 1 presents Jesus as the promised Messiah who fulfills God's covenant promises, enters human history by the power of the Holy Spirit, saves His people from their sins, and is truly Immanuel — God with us.

1. Matthew 1:1 — Jesus Stands in the Line of Promise

Observation

Matthew opens by identifying Jesus Christ as the Son of David and the Son of Abraham.

These titles immediately connect Jesus to two major covenant promises in the Old Testament.

Interpretation

Abraham received God's promise that through his offspring all nations of the earth would be blessed.

David received God's promise that his royal line would continue and that a king from his house would reign.

By placing Jesus in both lines, Matthew is declaring from the beginning that Jesus is connected to God's covenant faithfulness.

Teaching Truth:

Jesus does not appear suddenly or without preparation. His coming stands within the long unfolding plan of God.

2. Matthew 1:2–17 — The Genealogy Reveals God's Faithfulness

Observation

Matthew traces the genealogy from Abraham through David, through the Babylonian exile, and finally to Joseph, the husband of Mary.

The genealogy includes kings, ordinary people, faithful people, sinful people, and unexpected individuals.

Interpretation

Genealogies may seem unfamiliar or unimportant to modern readers, but Matthew is making a theological point through history.

God remained faithful to His promises across generations, even through human weakness, national failure, exile, suffering, and sin.

The presence of people such as Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, and the wife of Uriah also reminds us that God's redemptive purpose moves forward through lives and circumstances that human beings might not expect.

The genealogy does not glorify human perfection. It highlights God's faithfulness.

Teaching Truth:

God's plan does not depend upon perfect human beings. His faithfulness continues even through broken generations.

Application

We should never assume that our past, our family history, or human failure can place us beyond the reach of God's grace.

God is able to accomplish His purposes even through imperfect people who are brought into His redemptive plan.

3. Matthew 1:18 — The Birth of Jesus Was Supernatural

Observation

Mary was promised in marriage to Joseph, but before they came together, she was found to be pregnant through the Holy Spirit.

Interpretation

Matthew makes clear that the conception of Jesus was not the result of ordinary human generation.

The birth of Jesus begins with God's action. His conception is supernatural, accomplished through the Holy Spirit.

This prepares the reader to understand that Jesus is no ordinary child.

Biblical Truth:

The coming of Jesus Christ into the world is an act of God, not merely an event of human history.

4. Matthew 1:19 — Joseph Chooses Mercy

Observation

Joseph believes Mary is pregnant by another man. Matthew describes Joseph as a righteous man.

Yet Joseph does not want to expose Mary publicly, so he plans to divorce her quietly.

Interpretation

Joseph's first response reveals both righteousness and compassion.

He does not simply ignore what he believes is wrong, but neither does he seek to humiliate Mary.

Biblical righteousness is not cruelty. Joseph shows that obedience to God and mercy toward people should not be separated.

Teaching Truth:

A righteous response should reflect both truth and mercy.

5. Matthew 1:20 — God Gives Joseph Understanding

Observation

While Joseph considers what to do, an angel of the Lord appears to him in a dream.

Joseph is told not to fear taking Mary as his wife because the child conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.

Interpretation

Joseph had reached a reasonable conclusion based upon the information available to him.

But Joseph did not yet know what God was doing.

God revealed information that Joseph could never have discovered through human reasoning alone.

Teaching Truth:

Our understanding is limited, but God's knowledge is complete.

Application

There are times when circumstances appear to tell one story, while God is working in ways we cannot yet see.

Faith does not require us to deny reality. It requires us to remain open to God's truth when our understanding is incomplete.

6. Matthew 1:21 — Jesus Came to Save From Sin

Observation

Joseph is told that Mary will give birth to a son and that he must name Him Jesus.

The reason is given clearly: He will save His people from their sins.

Interpretation

Matthew immediately tells us why Jesus came.

Israel lived under Roman rule, and many people longed for political deliverance. Yet the deeper human problem was sin.

Jesus came to deal with the problem that lies beneath every other human problem: separation from God because of sin.

Central Gospel Truth:

Jesus did not come merely to improve human circumstances. He came to save sinners.

Application

Christianity cannot be reduced to moral improvement, better habits, religious activity, or personal success.

At the center of the Gospel is our need for salvation from sin and God's provision of that salvation through Jesus Christ.

7. Matthew 1:22–23 — Immanuel: God With Us

Observation

Matthew connects the birth of Jesus with the prophecy of Isaiah.

The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and He will be called Immanuel, which means “God with us.”

Interpretation

Matthew wants the reader to understand that the birth of Jesus fulfills God's prophetic word.

The title Immanuel communicates something profound about the identity of Jesus.

God has not remained distant from humanity. In Jesus Christ, God has entered human history and drawn near to us.

Teaching Truth:

Jesus is not merely a messenger telling us about God. In Him, God has come near.

8. Matthew 1:24–25 — Joseph Responds With Obedience

Observation

Joseph wakes from the dream and does what the angel of the Lord commanded him.

He takes Mary as his wife and later gives the child the name Jesus.

Interpretation

Joseph's faith becomes visible through obedience.

He does not merely receive new information from God. He acts upon what God has revealed.

His obedience may have exposed him to misunderstanding, criticism, and personal difficulty, but he chooses faithfulness.

Teaching Truth:

Genuine faith responds to God's revealed truth with obedience.

What Matthew 1 Teaches Us About Jesus

Jesus is the Son of Abraham.
He stands within God's covenant promise of blessing.

Jesus is the Son of David.
He is the promised royal Messiah and King.

Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit.
His entrance into the world is supernatural.

Jesus is Savior.
He came to save His people from their sins.

Jesus is Immanuel.
In Him, God has come near to humanity.

Three Questions for Reflection

1. Where do I need to trust God's faithfulness when I cannot yet see how He is working?

2. Do I primarily think of Jesus as someone who improves my life, or as the Savior who rescues me from sin?

3. What truth from God's Word do I already understand but still need to obey?

Chapter Summary

Matthew 1 begins the Gospel by establishing the identity of Jesus Christ. He is rooted in God's covenant promises, descended from Abraham and David, conceived by the Holy Spirit, born in fulfillment of prophecy, and sent to save His people from their sins.

The chapter also shows us the quiet faithfulness of Joseph, whose obedience demonstrates what it looks like to trust God when circumstances are difficult to understand.

Remember:

The Gospel of Matthew begins not merely with the birth of a child, but with the arrival of the promised Messiah: Jesus Christ, the Savior and Immanuel — God with us.
